Events planner EP2, 22 October 2026, version 1. Branch claude/plan-events-final, from build 0.64.14 (claude/integration f6b9176). Planning only: the game hasn't changed, and nothing is built from this until you've played and said yes. The numbers marked "measured" come from a new game in 0.64.14. In short
  • These are the last two ship-wide events in scope. The design doc's calendar has four before the career ends: Crossing the Line (built, switched off, waiting for your go), the ion storm (built and on), and these two. Everything later stays parked.
  • Comet viewing, Tuesday Day 16, 21:30. One gentle evening. Sato's Mail in the morning; at 21:30 the comet is in every outside window, and the crew gather at the bow lounge windows, the observatory scope and the observation room. No score, nothing to fix.
  • Its little story (recommended): "The comet knot". On the Veyli long ship, a comet was a calendar, and the youngest aboard tied a knot for it. Envoy Saruth, alone of her people on the Wren, asks the newest hand to tie it: you. The knot goes on her door cord, and at the visit the Speaker notices it.
  • The Veyli visit, the finale, Days 26-30. Named on Monday Day 22. The warning on Friday 26 after Calder's question. Saturday 27, 10:00: the crew line the shuttle bay as the delegation walks down the Tern's ramp. Sunday 28: one formal dinner in the Captain's quarters. Monday 29: the gift exchange in the morning, and the crew line the bay again to see them off at 17:30, after shifts. Tuesday 30: the afterglow and Vance's debrief.
  • Its little story (recommended): "Forty questions". Moleth, the Veyli scholar, has sent forty questions for Works. You get the list, show Moleth round on Saturday, and answer the last question your own way. At the farewell Moleth hands you question 41.
  • Your help counts, as in the storm: four prep jobs, each moving one part of the result (warm cabins, the herbs, the dinner on time, courtesy). Left alone it comes out bumpy; with all four, smooth.
  • Big crowds (measured): about 55 at the comet, about 50-55 at the greeting and about 60-65 at the farewell. All under the 80 your performance test allowed.
  • About 14-16 build turns for both, plus the writer's lines (about 25 for the comet, about 100 for the visit). 10 questions for you, each with a recommended answer (section 10).

The comet exists only as an old draft in plans/comet-viewing-draft.js. The visit exists in the game as an old long version (nine days, two dinners), switched off as a draft.

3. Comet viewing (Tuesday Day 16)

The story

Each option has a beginning, a middle where your one choice matters, and a payoff. All use people already aboard on their real rotas.

A. "The comet knot" (recommended)
  • Why it fits: the Veyli lore you chose already has Saruth say at the comet: "On the long ship, a comet was a calendar. Somebody always tied a knot for it." This gives that line a small story.
  • Morning: Sato's Mail. At breakfast or in the corridors the crew talk about window seats.
  • 21:30, at whichever viewing place you go to (Saruth goes to the observatory first, then the bow lounge at 22:00, so you can find her at either): she has a length of cord in her hands. "On the long ship, the youngest aboard tied the comet's knot. There is no Veyli younger than me on this ship. There is you." You're the newest hand: nobody has come aboard since you (Basil arrives on Day 21). Your choice:
    • "Show me how." She guides your hands through it, slowly, and names the knot. You tied it.
    • "Tomas has never seen one either. Can we both?" (or whoever from your bunk room is there) "Then you both hold the cord." You tied it together.
    • "It's your people's knot. You tie it. I'll watch." She ties it, saying each turn under her breath. "Watching is also a way of keeping it."
  • If you're not there, she ties it herself at 22:30. Nothing is lost.
  • Wednesday: the cord on her door has a new knot, and its tap text says so (and who tied it).
  • The payoff, Saturday Day 27 at the greeting: Speaker Teshavi stops at Saruth and touches the cord at her wrist. "A comet knot. Who tied it?" Saruth's answer depends on your choice; if it was you, Teshavi turns to you: "Then you have been to the long ship, a little."
  • No spoilers: nothing about why Saruth is aboard, nothing about Holt, nothing about time or the long ship repeating, and the knot's meaning stays a Veyli thing ("some things are kept").
  • Cost: about one turn, and about 20-25 lines from the writer.
B. "What the comet is called" (not recommended)
  • Sato finds the comet isn't on any Concord chart, so the Wren may log a name for it. She asks three people for a name during the day, you among them, and reads the chosen one out at 21:30 in the observatory.
  • Your choice: a practical name, a name from home, or a name for the Wren.
  • Weaker: it happens at Sato's desk and inside Survey, away from you. And past the Line the Veyli got there first: "every star's got a Veyli name", so a Concord name sits awkwardly with the lore you chose.
C. "The watch with no window" (not recommended)
  • The engine night watch (measured: 11 crew at work at 21:30, bridge and engine) never sees anything. You carry the observatory's picture down to the engine watch room on a tablet.
  • Weaker: one person, away from the crowd, and it needs a new "picture on a screen" piece just for this.

4. The Veyli visit (Days 22, 26-30)

The story

A. "Forty questions" (recommended)
  • Who: Moleth, the Veyli scholar (they/them), who studies Concord ships, "has a list of forty questions for Chief Calder" and "wants to see the engine room more than anything" (both already in the game). The lore you chose turns their questions to what each side kept.
  • Monday Day 22: Vance's memo has a second page: Moleth's forty questions, sent ahead. Calder passes them to you with one line: "You're Works. Answer what you can. Torres has the engine room on Saturday." Nothing to do yet; the list sits in your Mail and some questions make you look at things (the water recycler, the lift, the hull plates).
  • Saturday 11:00, the tour (about an hour, while you're off duty; Torres is on shift): the Works bay, your name on the beam, then the engine room with Torres. Moleth asks as you go; the replies they get are the lines you'd expect from Works. At the coils, the fortieth question: "You still say 'cheers' when you drink. We stopped. Why did you keep it?" Your choice:
    • Tell them about home (Mum and Gran, the kitchen table). "So it is a thing for the people who are not in the room. We have that too. It is a knot."
    • Show them something on the ship (the mug rack in the Works bay, everyone's mug). "So it is a thing for the people who are in the room."
    • Turn it round: "What did you keep?" Moleth is quiet a long time. "Tea at the same hour. Six hundred years. Nobody asked us that before."
    Each nudges your friendship with Moleth and the courtesy part of the result a little; none is wrong.
  • Rank: if you're an Engine Specialist by then, Torres lets you lead the engine room part. Otherwise Torres leads and you fill in.
  • If you miss the tour, Torres does it alone and Moleth Mails you the fortieth question on Saturday evening; you can answer at the dinner (if you have a seat) or in passing on Sunday.
  • Monday 17:30, the farewell: at the foot of the ramp Moleth hands you a folded page: question 41, unanswered. "For next time." It goes in your Record, and the crew retell the tour.
  • No spoilers: Torres's lines stay on the tour (his own story is the crew-stories seat's). Nothing about Holt's past, the seven knots' contents or why Saruth is aboard.
  • Cost: about one turn, and about 35 lines.
B. "Who stands at the door" (not recommended)
  • Ithra, the Veyli pilot and guard, and Chief Holt clash over who stands at the Captain's door before the dinner (already in the old visit as chatter). You can bring Holt a tea, suggest they split the door, or leave it.
  • Weaker: it's Holt's story, which has its own beats kept for later (and for the crew-stories seat), and it puts the finale's emotion into a quarrel.
C. "Kesrin's notebook" (not recommended)
  • Kesrin, the Speaker's aide, writes everything down, jokes included, and is secretly delighted by the Deck 3 vending machine. He asks you for one Wren custom for the notebook the council will read.
  • Weaker: light and funny but small, with no payoff at a big crowd moment.

Why A: it's about the theme you chose (near meets far, what each side kept), your trade is the way in, and it pays off in front of the whole crew at the farewell. It still works if you skip it. The comet knot (section 3) pays off at the greeting, so both stories land inside the visit.
