Who the Veyli are: the plan

Veyli lore planner LW1, 2026-10-21, version 1.1 (the reviewer ER2's four fixes folded in). Branch claude/veyli-lore, from claude/events-lines. Planning and words only: the game hasn't changed. Your answers go in section 7.
Your decision (02:50 UTC):
"We also need to learn as players who the Veyli are. I think they should be human somehow, only humans in this world. Maybe another culture that splintered from Earth. Hence they represent reaching into the distance."
Also decided: Crossing the Line means "guests in someone else's sky" (line-q10 A).
In short
  • Recommended history (A, "The ones who went on"): about six hundred years ago, early in the age of leaving Earth, a slow ship went much further than anyone else. It took four lifetimes to get past Tavessa, and its people settled a world out there. Nobody heard from them for centuries, and the Concord grew up behind them among the near stars. About eighty years ago a Concord survey ship reached the Line and found them. The Veyli say: "We did not leave. We went on."
  • What grew different comes from the long voyage, so every custom already written now has a reason: the knot cord, tea at the same hour, silence, warm rooms, no bowing, single names, their own language.
  • The theme: the Veyli are what reaching into the distance does to people. They're still people, still kind, and changed by how far they went. The Wren follows where they went first, and you, far from home, are doing the same thing on a small scale.
  • How you learn it: a plain fact from the first week ("They're people. They just went further out."), then more, a little at a time, through the month: Ferreira, the Envoy's tea, the Line, the storm, the comet, the visit. About 23 new lines and 15 changed ones. Nothing about why Saruth is aboard, Holt's beats or the visit's turns.
  • Pronouns: they/them for all Veyli stays, now as a Veyli custom that the crew respect (their language has no he or she). The old rule "the Veyli are not described in human terms" is retired.
  • What must change: 15 lines that set "humans" against the Veyli or call a Veyli "she", plus the canon notes in facts.md and design-doc.md. Nothing in Holt's arc changes.

1. Who the Veyli are: three histories

What's already written, and every option keeps
  • The knot cord on the door, a new knot each month aboard.
  • Tea kneeling at a low table, at the same hour every day.
  • Silence preferred, and many words for "quiet".
  • Warm cabins.
  • "Do not bow. Most of you do it wrong."
  • A council, and students of the cord.
  • Veyli seedlings.
  • Their own language: Taavek has "no common words for most of the parts".
  • Veyli ships are rare visitors, and the frontier is "shared with the Veyli".
  • Holt's bad day "years ago", and a Veyli ship's six-hour silence.
  • The Wren in the reach two years ago, on a quiet charting run.
  • Saruth aboard five months, and "the Veyli homeworld".
  • Tavessa, "a star cluster the Veyli named".
  • The Line, where the Concord's charts grow thin.

A. "The ones who went on" recommended

  • When and why: about six hundred years ago. Early in the age of leaving Earth, one slow ship set out to go further than anyone had. It wasn't fleeing anything and there was no quarrel. It wanted to see how far people could go. The voyage took four lifetimes. Past Tavessa they found a world and stayed.
  • How long apart: centuries with no word either way. The Concord formed among the near stars without them. About eighty years ago a Concord survey ship reached the Line and met a Veyli ship. Since then the two have been careful, courteous neighbours: rare ships and a few envoys.
  • What grew different (all from the voyage):
    • The knot cord. On the long ship they counted the months in knots, a cord you could read by touch in the dark. It's still their calendar, their record and their way of saying things that matter. Saruth's "a new knot each month aboard" is exactly this: they're on a voyage too.
    • Tea at the same hour. The long ship kept one still hour a day, so the day had a fixed point. ("A fixed point is a kindness you give yourself.")
    • Silence. On a long ship you listen to the air and the hull, so quiet is a kind of attention, and there are many words for it. Their silence rites stay secret (section 3).
    • Warmth. The voyage ran cold, so a warm room is hospitality.
    • No bowing. The Concord bows the way old Earth pictures show it. The Veyli kept bowing and changed it, so a Concord bow looks to them like an old drawing done wrong.
    • Names. One name each, no family name. That's already true of all six: Saruth, Teshavi, Kesrin, Moleth, Ithra and Taavek.
    • The language grew out of old Earth tongues over six centuries and is now its own. Here and there an old root still shows, which is a small joy for Ferreira.
    • Seedlings from seeds the long ship carried from Earth, changed over the centuries.
    • Looks (as drawn today): ordinary people, often tall and slim, with a cord band at the brow. Some dye their hair in cord colours (teal, white, grey). Nothing else.
  • Their own saying: "We did not leave. We went on." It's gentle pride, not a dispute.
  • Why A: it explains every custom already written without changing one. It fits the oath ("we left the near stars behind us") and your "reaching into the distance" best. Eighty years of acquaintance fits the Wren's run two years ago, rare Veyli ships and a "shared" frontier. No war anywhere in it.

B. "A quiet parting"

About a hundred and fifty years ago, a movement on the Concord's own worlds wanted a slower, more ritual life and went out past the Line together. They were never lost, so there are family stories on both sides.

Weaker: 150 years is too short for a language with "no common words" and many words for quiet. It's more a neighbour than a distance. And "parted from us" can sound like a falling-out, which needs care to stay gentle.

C. "Lost and found"

A colony from over a thousand years ago lost all contact and kept only legends of Earth until they were found again.

Weaker: it raises questions the game doesn't want ("did they know they were human?"), and a lost colony reads as a mystery to solve rather than a people to meet. It's also hard to square with their pride, their council and their envoys.

2. Why they matter: "reaching into the distance"

  • They are the reach, made into people. They're the far edge of how far humans have gone, and still people: tea, courtesy, a joke a sentence late. Distance changed their customs, not their kindness.
  • The Wren follows where they went first. Past the Line (Day 7) the stars have Veyli names because the Veyli got there first. "Guests in someone else's sky" gains a warm second meaning: it's a cousin's sky.
  • The oath answers the Veyli saying. The oath has "We left the near stars behind us, and we did not go alone." The Veyli say "We did not leave. We went on." Both are about going far together. The game never spells it out; the player can notice it.
  • You're reaching too. You're the newest hand, far from Mum and Gran, counting your days aboard. Saruth is the one person aboard further from home than you, alone of their people, counting months in knots. Their tea hour is your mirror.
  • The visit is near meeting far: two branches of one family, careful and curious. Moleth's forty questions are about what each side kept.

4. Pronouns and how the Veyli are described

Today: "the Veyli are not described in human terms", they/them for every Veyli, and "the Envoy" is always safe. Now that the Veyli are human, the first part goes.

  • Recommended (a): they/them stays, as a Veyli custom. The Veyli language has no "he" or "she"; it grew that way on the long ship. A Veyli asks to be "they", and the crew respect it, like learning to knock twice. "The Envoy" and "the Speaker" stay safe. It changes nothing already written except three slips in the game (Teshavi as "she" and "her", and the Envoy as "she" at the Captain's table) and one code comment. One line from Ferreira at most: "No 'he' or 'she' in Veyli. Just the person."
  • (b) Each Veyli gets their own pronouns, like the crew. More ordinary, but six sets to choose and old drafts to check.
  • (c) they/them stays, with no reason given. Less work, but it reads oddly now they're human.
The new description rule (for facts.md and the voice bible, after your answers): "The Veyli are people: a human culture that went further out, centuries ago. Describe them as people with their own ways. Never call them strange, never 'aliens', never 'their kind', and never point at their bodies. Crew say 'us' or 'Concord folk', never 'humans' set against the Veyli. A Veyli speaks of 'your people' and 'my people'."

6. What goes into the canon after your answers

facts.md, the Veyli (the head writer's seat writes it; with the recommended answers):
  • The Veyli are human. About six hundred years ago a slow ship left Earth to go further than anyone. Four lifetimes later its people settled a world past Tavessa ("the Veyli homeworld"). No contact for centuries. A Concord survey ship met them about eighty years ago, at the Line. Careful, courteous neighbours since; Veyli ships are rare. Their saying: "We did not leave. We went on."
  • Their customs come from the long ship: the knot cord, the still tea hour, quiet as attention, warm rooms, a bow they changed, one name each, a language grown from old Earth tongues.
  • Round numbers only ("about six hundred years", "four lifetimes", "about eighty years ago"). No dates. No war, ever. Nothing links the eighty years to Holt's bad day.
