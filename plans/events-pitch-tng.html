Echo Signal Day 18, Thursday evening · cozy, moving · one evening

The premise. A probe drifting at the edge of the survey loop sends out a faint, broken signal. Under the noise, Dr. Sato's team finds music: a recording made on Halden, a colony that went silent long before most of the crew were born. They clean it up during the day, and in the evening the whole ship gathers in the observation room to hear it. Nothing goes wrong. It's simply a moving evening, and afterwards the tune lives on the ship.

Who's in it: Dr. Imra Sato (Survey), the comms crew, Captain Marrow, Matthias Oduya (who leads the quiet watch), whoever you're closest to, and an older crew member whose grandmother came from Halden (the crew designer picks one with a fitting background).

How it runs

  1. 09:00A signalSurvey hears something under the probe's carrier. Chatter starts in the Survey lab; by 11:00 the word is "music".
  2. 12:30LunchEveryone's talking about it in the mess. Sato's Mail at 13:00: "Observation room, 20:30. Bring nothing. Just come."
  3. 16:00A jobThe recording has a hum in it: the sensor array's coolant pump, picked up by the comms line. Works is asked to quieten it (a job on the board).
  4. 20:15GatheringThe observation room on Deck 1 fills: crew on the floor, the steps and the window ledges, the lights down low. The night watch listen on the bridge.
  5. 20:30The musicSato says a few words and plays it: a small choir and a piano, a little out of tune, and at the end a child's voice counting in for a second song that never comes. About three minutes. Then silence.
  6. 20:40The CaptainA few lines about Halden. People stay and talk quietly, or leave without a word.
  7. Day 19AfterwardsPeople hum it at breakfast. That night someone plays it on the music room's piano, and it joins the late piano tradition.

The ship: comms busy for a day (the array working the signal); mood up, quiet and warm for two days.

Goes well (the hum quietened in time): the whole tune, clear. The trace: "The Halden Song" joins the music room piano's pieces (late piano plays it from now on), and Sato frames the probe's call sign by the observatory door. Goes poorly (the hum stays in): only half the tune comes through clearly. The piano plays the fragment, and crew hum the rest their own way, which is lovely in its own way.

Why it's fun: it's the quiet one. After the night-shift build-up it's a breath: the whole crew in one room, still, the stars behind them. It also leaves something you can hear for the rest of the game.

Loss of Power Day 27, Saturday morning · cozy-tense, people · about two hours

The premise. A relay on the spine's power feed fails on a Saturday morning, and the lifts stop with the lights in the shaft. You're in the main lift with an odd little group: Chef Auda Brennan with a crate of eggs, Nell (eight, a passenger's daughter, aboard for the week) and Commander Teo Vance. For two hours you're stuck between Decks 3 and 4 with a dim emergency lamp, an intercom, your tool roll and three people you'd never otherwise spend two hours with. You fix what you can from inside; the ship fixes the rest from outside.

Who's in it: Brennan (prickly, practical, worried about the eggs), Nell (curious, brave until she isn't), Vance (the ship's dry, careful second-in-command, more human in a stuck lift than on the bridge), Torres on the intercom, the Works crew outside.

How it runs

  1. 10:00Hold the doorsWherever you are near the spine that morning, someone calls "Hold the doors!" and you step in with them (if you're nowhere near, see the questions: you help from outside instead).
  2. 10:04The stopA clunk, the car stops between Decks 3 and 4, the lights go out and the small emergency lamp comes on. Every lift on the ship stops too (felt everywhere: the lift lights go dark).
  3. 10:10The intercomTorres: "Everyone all right in there? It's the spine relay. Give us an hour. Maybe two. Don't do anything clever." Nell: "Is this an adventure?"
  4. 10:20-11:00InsideThe car's panel: you can open it and reset the car's own relay (a work step), which gets the fan and the light back. Then talk. Each stretch of conversation is a moment with one of them.
  5. 11:00-11:45The long middleIt gets warm. Nell gets scared. Brennan's eggs become a plan (she shares the boiled ones from the bottom of the crate). Vance tells a story.
  6. 11:50DownWorks outside replaces the spine relay; your reset means the car can creep down to Deck 3 on its own power. The doors open on a small crowd and applause.
  7. 12:30LunchNell insists you all have lunch together, so you do.

The ship: transport down for two hours (meals late, crew on the spine ladders, a Saturday's traditions shuffled). Mood: a ship full of stories.

Goes well (you reset the car, everyone stays calm): a big friendship boost with all three (a full step each), and Nell's drawing of "the lift people" (four stick figures and some eggs) pinned on the Works bay board. Brennan's Lift Eggs appear on the Saturday menu from then on. Goes poorly (a long, hot wait): still friends after (a smaller step), and the drawing has frowns on it.

Why it's fun: it's a chamber play. A tiny space, three people you only know by their jobs, and two hours to find out who they are. It's the biggest friendship moment in the game, and it comes from bad luck.

Space Creature Days 29-31, Monday to Wednesday · cozy, wonder · three days

The premise. For a day the power keeps dipping and Works can't find a fault anywhere. Then the port windows glow. A baby space creature, soft and translucent, a bit like a jellyfish the size of a shuttle, has latched onto the hull near a power conduit and is feeding on the Wren's power. It isn't hostile; it's hungry. Nobody wants to hurt it. So Works weans it off, lowering the conduit's output a step at a time until it loses interest. On the third morning it lets go, and far out a much larger glow comes to meet it.

Who's in it: Dr. Sato (who works out what it is), Chief Calder and Torres (the weaning plan), Captain Marrow, the observatory crowd, and the whole ship at the windows.

How it runs

  1. Day 29 Mon, all dayDipsThe lights soften now and then; the galley's ovens slow; jobs come in for faults that aren't there. Works checks the junctions and finds nothing. Grumbling.
  2. Day 29, 21:10The glowFelt everywhere: a soft blue-green glow drifts across every port-side window. Crew stop and stare. Sato runs to the observatory.
  3. Day 29, 22:00The CaptainHer address: "We have a visitor. A young one. It's hungry, it's not dangerous, and we're going to be gentle with it."
  4. Day 30 Tue, 08:15The planA meeting at the Works board: Sato, Calder and Torres. Lower the hull conduit's output in small steps so it drifts off without starving. A job for every two hours of the day shift: one step down, then read how it reacts.
  5. Day 30, all dayWeaningEach step is a tuning job at the conduit's panel (M5's verb: one notch at a time, watch the reading). Too fast and it clings harder (the power dips); too slow and the ship stays dim. The crew name it (a vote: "Sprat" leads). The observatory is packed all evening.
  6. Day 31 Wed, 06:40Let goFelt everywhere: the glow lifts off the hull and drifts out; a far larger glow comes to meet it. Applause on the promenade from the early risers. The power comes back to full.

The ship: power a little low for two days (soft lights, slower ovens, a slower lift); mood up (wonder, gossip, a full observatory).

Goes well (steady, gentle steps): it lets go on time, unhurt. The trace: "Sprat" in the ship's log and Sato's sketch of it framed in the observatory. Goes poorly (steps too fast or skipped): it clings a day longer, the ship stays dim, and a scorched hull plate where it fed becomes a job for later. It still swims off safe.

Why it's fun: a mystery fault with a gorgeous answer, and a repair that's about care instead of force. It uses a skill you learned on your fourth day (tuning) for something nobody expected.

The Visiting Inspector Days 32-33, Thursday and Friday · cozy-tense · two days

The premise. Fleet Inspector Dagny Krell comes aboard for a two-day audit: logs, drills, machines, the job board, how the crew answer questions. She's strict, fair and impossible to read. Chief Holt is nervous, Calder is calm in a way that worries everyone, and Works scrambles to clear the board. You can impress her or embarrass the department, but nobody ever fails: the worst outcome is a notice pinned up for the crew to joke about.

Who's in it: Inspector Krell (a two-day guest), Commander Vance (her escort), Chief Holt (the Watch, sweating), Chief Calder (Works, unflappable), Chef Brennan (who will not be inspected, and is), the Works crew.

How it runs

  1. Day 31 Wed, 16:00WarningVance's Mail to all departments: "Fleet inspection, tomorrow and Friday. Be yourselves. Tidy yourselves first." Holt drills the Watch that evening. The job board's backlog suddenly matters.
  2. Day 32 Thu, 10:00ArrivalShe comes off the shuttle (a short meeting at the bay): a handshake for the Captain and a clipboard for everyone else.
  3. Day 32, 10:30-17:00The walkShe walks the decks with Vance, opens panels at random, reads the logbook, and asks crew questions where she finds them (you, about a machine you really serviced).
  4. Day 33 Fri, 09:00Surprise drillA fire drill she calls herself. You run the Works part (as a Senior Officer); she times it.
  5. Day 33, 11:00The galleyShe inspects the galley. Brennan, very politely, says three things nobody has ever heard Brennan say.
  6. Day 33, 15:00FindingsA meeting in the observation room: her findings read out department by department, short and fair. Works Friday tea after, loud with relief.

The ship: every system gets checked, and a little extra wear from the rush; the board nearly empty by Friday; mood tense, then a big lift at Friday tea.

Goes well (the board cleared, honest answers, a quick drill): a framed fleet commendation for Works on the Works bay wall, and a line about you in her findings (your Record keeps it). Goes poorly: a "notice of improvement" pinned in the Works bay, which the crew joke about for weeks ("Room to improve!" whenever someone drops a spanner). Never a failure, never a punishment, and it comes down the next time Works has a smooth event.

Why it's fun: everyone's on their best behaviour, which is very funny to watch. Your everyday work suddenly matters out loud: the honest answer about a repair you really did, and a drill you really learned.
