In short

Today your job on day 40 feels the same as on day 2: the crew fix nearly everything before you get to it, looking at a machine simply tells you what's wrong, and there's no way to grow past your first rank. And of the big ship-wide events, only the ion storm is switched on. Pillar 5 fixes both.

1. Real faults reach youYou still get 3 to 6 jobs a day, but about half are machines really misbehaving, with a cause to find.
2. Working out what's wrongLooking at a machine shows its parts and a reading for each. You work out which part is failing. A wrong guess just means a walk back to storage, never a fail.
3. You get betterRepairs on a kind of machine get quicker with practice. Your Record says it in words ("Ovens: you know these well"). No points, no numbers.
4. Your first promotionCalder's talks tell you how close you are, then she sets a supervised test job. Pass it and you're an Officer, with a new badge and your own cabin. You're never passed over, only asked to try again in a week.
5. Deeper workAfter promotion, faults can start in one place and show in another: weak showers on Deck 4, a valve on Deck 5. You trace them through the pipes. New rooms open with your key.
6. The ship's big eventsThe comet viewing and the diplomatic visit switched on; then the power surge, the late supply shuttle, something in the garden (the ship gets a pet) and the ship-wide cough. Each one stands on its own.

About 3-4 helpers for the career, starting as soon as you approve; 2 more for the events, after Pillar 4's third wave, when Pillar 4 is mostly writing. Four check-ins with you.

Your work day

  1. Morning at the boardAbout half the jobs waiting for you are real faults: a pump losing pressure, a light relay chattering. The crew leave your kind of faults for you a little longer than now. On your days off and at night they take everything, so the ship never suffers for your rest.
  2. The scannerCalder hands you a handheld scanner early on. Point it at a machine on your way past and it tells you if it's starting to wear. Catch it early and it's a quick service instead of a repair later. Jobs you find yourself are marked "found by you".
  3. Looking it overAt the machine, a small panel in the device's old-screen style shows its parts, each with a reading. Usually one is clearly off. Sometimes two are, and what you saw on the deck (the drip, the flicker) tells you which.
  4. Fetch and fixYou fetch the part you chose. If you guessed wrong, the machine tells you ("Fitted a new seal. Still low flow: it's the pump") and you go back. That's all a wrong guess costs.
  5. Getting quickerThe tenth oven repair is clearly quicker than the first. Crew notice the first time you fix something fast.

The week's small duties, made real

  • Wednesday drill (15:00, already in the crew's week): you muster at the Works bay for 15 minutes. Turning up counts toward Calder's trust; missing it gets a word from her the next day.
  • Thursday inspection: the duty officer walks the junior wing at 06:30. A grubby uniform or an untidy berth gets a gentle remark, a tidy one a nod.

Your first promotion

Steady work→Calder: "getting there"→The test job→Your badge→Your own cabin
  • How you get there: doing jobs well, catching faults early, picking the right part, and being reliable (turning up, finishing what you start). Reliability is the same "Work" reputation Pillar 4 builds, so the ship has one idea of how reliable you are. Roughly 2 to 3 ship weeks of steady work (about 10 to 14 hours of play).
  • The test job: Calder sets a real bigger job (the water recycler or an air scrubber) and follows you through it, saying a word at each step (Zeke Torres if it's her day off). Go wrong and she tells you what to practise and sets another in a week.
  • The moment: next morning at the board, in front of the Works crew, Calder hands you the Officer's badge. The Captain sends her congratulations by Mail, and the ship talks about it.
  • Your own cabin: the spare cabin 4-23 on Deck 4's officers' corridor. Your bed and locker move, Tomas says goodbye as a bunkmate, and Pillar 4's "come over" visits start.

Deeper work after promotion

  • Connected systems. Water and air machines are joined by pipes and ducts. A fault can show in one place and start somewhere else. The job card says where the symptom is; finding the cause is yours.
  • The pipe kit lets you read the flow board in Deck 5's manifold room and follow the low reading back along the pipes.
  • New rooms open with your key: Deck 5's air plant, fan hall and manifold room, the pipe galleries and the crawlways in the ship's spine, each with real machines you can walk to.
  • Night on call (you choose to join): about one night a week. If something breaks overnight your device wakes you; fix it, back to bed, and a lie-in the next morning. Never more than once a night.
  • The two higher ranks (planning shutdowns around the crew; leading team jobs on the engines) wait for a later pillar.

The ship's big events

As agreed in Pillar 3, an event is something the whole ship lives through, never a mission you're given: rumours, Mail, the Captain's address, gatherings, new jobs on the board, and crew talking about it for days. In the agreed order:

Nebula drift and salvageIf the pillar has room: every window full of colour and strange dreams; then the shuttle flies out for the first time and comes back with crates of odd finds.mainif room
Small incidentsAbout one a week between the big events: a burst pipe, the lift stuck with someone in it, a scorched pan setting off the galley alarm.an hour or twonew

Later pillars: the Founding Day festival (waits for the real-phone crowd test), the Veyli ship that needs help, and the déjà vu day (replaying a whole day is the riskiest thing on the list). Crew rotation day is already Pillar 4's crew coming and going. After the list runs out, events come back in new versions, more rarely.

After promotion, every event has a part only you can do: tracing the surge's cause, rigging a filter bypass, walking the inspector through the manifold room. The first senior briefing at the conference table comes with the power surge.

Each event stands on its own

  • One event, one piece. Each lives in its own block, switched on or off by itself. Taking one out breaks nothing else.
  • No event needs another to have happened. A new game still runs them in the agreed order.
  • You can try any one by itself: a special link starts a chosen event on a chosen day, for reviewing.
  • Each has its own test, and you play and sign off each one like a deck (or two at a time).

Risks, and what we do about them

  • Phones slowing down. New rooms, a furnished cabin, event props, the pet, crowds. Each prop has a small fixed allowance and only appears on its deck while its event runs; every new room and every event's busiest moment is measured against the 450 limit.
  • The game must play the same every time. Everything that's chosen (which part fails, when incidents happen, on-call nights) is worked out from the day and the machine, never by chance, and each check runs twice to prove it.
  • Two big pieces of work in one file. New parts of the file with one owner each, Pillar 5 merged after Pillar 4, and the events held back until Pillar 4 is mostly writing.
  • Breaking your first week. The career never touches the jobs Pillar 4 holds for you, and the first-week test runs on every change.
  • Too gamey. Words everywhere, never numbers or points.
  • The parts panel feeling like a quiz. Usually one reading is clearly off, a wrong guess costs only a walk, and we tune it after you play it at check-in A.
  • Too much going on. Incidents never on an event day or a tradition evening; on-call only if you choose it.
