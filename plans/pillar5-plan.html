Pillar 5: your Works career final plan

Pillar 5 is the core of the game: new ways to work, repair puzzles you solve by deduction, and your whole career from Junior Officer to Senior Officer and Engine Specialist, in about 12 to 13 hours of play. All your answers are folded in. Nothing is built until you approve it, and each piece gets a short page for you to approve first. It's built on its own, after Pillar 4, by one small team. The detailed version for the team is plans/pillar5-plan.md.

In short

You arrive able only to fetch a part and replace it (Pillar 4's first week). Over about five ship weeks the job opens up a little at a time, each new thing taught to you by a person on a real job.

New ways to workRounds, your uniform and laundry, the drill and inspection, hauling, tuning, small incidents, the scanner. Then tracing faults and team jobs, then power, shutdowns and the sensor array, then engines and the reactor.
Head-scratchersFrom your first days as an Officer, some repairs become puzzles. You gather clues around the ship, cross out suspects and call the cause, then fix it by hand. There are nine kinds, getting harder with each tier.
Your careerGood work raises your reputation, which brings harder jobs, then a test job, then promotion: Officer on day 16, Senior Officer on day 23, Engine Specialist on day 30.
Moments along the wayAn officer ceremony with the Captain, your own cabin, then Deck 2's suite, two nights on the night shift, the Monday briefing, leading a team, a day mentoring a junior, and "Calder's question" at the end.
Side jobsThe workshop, tool upkeep, the logbook, leak hunting, irrigation, rewiring and the lift roof, plus crew favours and a board of old mysteries. They're there whenever you want them, and never needed to rank up.
Jobs mapped to rankEvery job needs a tier, and some are reserved for a rank. Junior jobs stop being given to you after promotion, but stay on the board if you want them.

What changed because of "10 to 15 hours"

You said the game should be about 10 to 15 hours, not 65. The journey is now about 33 ship days instead of 98. Skipping the nights when you sleep, a ship day plays in about 25 real minutes, so that's about 12 to 13 hours, your first week included. To fit, a few earlier agreements change. How you rank up: one clear rule

Good work→Works reputation grows→Harder jobs→A test job→Promotion
  • Your Works reputation is shown in words, never a number. It goes up with jobs done well, faults caught early, the right part first time, clever puzzle fixes and turning up to the drill and inspection. It goes down with missed shifts and jobs left half-done.
  • It decides the work you're given: New, Steady, Reliable, Trusted, then Relied on, when Calder sets your test job. It starts again with each new rank. You're never demoted.
  • Your Record always shows your rank, your reputation and one "What's next" line. Test jobs are only ever delayed, never failed.

Which jobs need which rank

Every job has a tier (its machine's tier), and some are reserved for a rank. The crew follow the same rule, and senior crew take reserved jobs too, so the ship never waits on you. Junior jobs aren't given to you after promotion, but stay on the board if you want them.

Head-scratchers: repairs you solve by deduction

Some faults don't show their cause. The job names a symptom, and the device's Fault notes page lists the suspects. You gather clues by walking the ship and asking people, cross suspects out, and call it; then you fetch the part and fix it by hand. There are no timers and no scores. A wrong call costs a walk and some time (and from Tier 2, a seal or cartridge). There's always "the long way": fit each suspect in turn.

The nine kinds

1. When does it happen?Brennan's oven runs cool, but only at dinner. Its log shows it sags when the big range and the extractor run too. A bad element would be cool all day: it's the power junction, sagging under load. Later, the logger tool records a whole day for you.Torresday 18
2. Who saw what?The mess door sticks now and then. Ask around: it stuck when the food trolley went through, twice; fine for Pell at breakfast. Tomas swears it stuck at 13:00, but he was at the gym then (he means the gym door; nobody lies). Only wide things: the safety sensor.Tomasday 19
3. What's been tried?Bunk 2's lamp flickers. Torres's note: "Swapped the bulb, still flickers." Pell's: "Reset the breaker: fine for an hour, then back." A relay warming up. The gentlest one, and the first you meet.Torresday 17 · your first
4. Which gauge is lying?The cold store's display says 3°, but the milk's going off. The scanner on the back shelf reads 9°. The sensor is lying, so the chiller never works harder. Later: a table of scrubbers, one out of step.Torresday 22
5. Follow the lineDeck 4's showers are weak all day. Read the gauges along the water line (each one a walk) and halve it each time until you find where it drops. The manifold's Deck 4 valve is sticking. This is how tracing faults gets harder; later it's power lines too.Calderday 24
6. Does it add up?The garden's far beds are dry. The recycler sends 15; the branches get 6, 5 and 2. Two are going missing; check the joints in the pipe gallery until the sums match.Calderday 26
7. Faded labelsA junction's four relay labels have worn off, and one relay chatters. Pell's old sketch, what you saw at the Wednesday power drill, and flipping test switches tell you which feeds what. Then you relabel the panel for good.Calderday 29 · a grid
8. Swap testingOne of five recycler cartridges leaks. The machine shop's test rig says how many in a group leak, not which, and you have three runs. Test 1 and 2: none. Test 3: one. Out of runs? Calder says "new ones all round", which costs parts.the machine shop techday 30 · the test rig
9. The clashThe recycler trips on Tuesdays and Fridays at 06:40. The calendar and the crew's week show what lands on both mornings. The gym class plus a second big draw. Service it, and ask the laundry hand to move Tuesday's wash to 08:00.Calderday 25 · Tier 3

Hints, gentlest first

  • The Chief's notes: rules of thumb, with a page added for each kind.
  • Ask a Works hand what kind of clue you're missing.
  • Talk it through with Torres or Calder: they cross one suspect out with you.
  • The long way: fit each suspect in turn. It always works.

Risks, and what we do about them

  • Too much, too fast: at most one big and one small new thing a workday; later stages and side jobs are off the main path; weekends are free.
  • Unfair puzzles: every case is checked for exactly one answer before you see it, and there's always "the long way".
  • Phones slowing down: each new prop is tiny and only drawn while in use, and phone speed is measured on every deck.
  • The same every time: faults, puzzles and incidents are worked out from the day and the machine, never by chance, and checked twice.
  • The night shift: only two nights, with a half day to sleep before and Sunday off after.
  • Your first week: Pillar 5 starts on day 8, and Pillar 4's first-week test runs on every change.
