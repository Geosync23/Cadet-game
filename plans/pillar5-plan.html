In short

Today the Works job does one thing: fetch a part and replace it. Even the odd jobs on the board (a vent, a delivery, a round) all play the same way: walk there and hold to work. Pillar 5 gives the job a ladder of new mechanics, each a different way of working. They open slowly, one at a time, and each is taught to you by a person on a real job. You start simple, and the job keeps opening up as you work on more of the ship.

One at a timeAbout one new mechanic every 2 or 3 ship days after your first week. Never two on one day. If you're slow, the ladder waits.
Taught, not toldEach new mechanic starts with a crew member doing one job with you, then watching you do one. You get anything new from them: a clipboard, a key, a tool.
Hands, not mini-gamesEverything happens in the world: walking, looking, carrying, turning a valve, folding. Doing something badly costs time or a remark, never a fail.
It keeps growingEach mechanic has 2 or 3 stages that open as you use it, so even old work keeps changing.
Around the ladderMore real faults reach you. Repairs get quicker with practice, said in words. Off shift: tinkering, touches for your cabin, logging stars. Your device gets pages for your training, your work log and your tools.
Your first promotionAfter about 2 ship weeks of steady work beyond your first week: a test job, the Officer's badge, your own cabin. Only ever delayed, never passed over.

The rules every mechanic follows

  • Start simple. Your first week (built in Pillar 4) teaches fetch and replace. Everything new comes after that, one piece at a time.
  • Taught by a person. Short lines, on a real job. At most one small control tip.
  • Physical. When you have to choose (which part, which valve), you choose from what you've seen on the deck.
  • Words, not numbers. Your Record says "Trained: rounds, laundry, hauling" and "Ovens: you know these well". No points, no bars.
  • The ship never depends on you. The crew can do every kind of work too, just more slowly, so there's always work for you but nothing falls apart without you.

Your first promotion

Steady work→Calder: "getting there"→The test job→Your badge→Your own cabin
  • Counts: jobs done well, faults caught early, the right part first time, and the mechanics you've been trained in. Reliability is Pillar 4's "Work" reputation plus turning up to the drill and the inspection.
  • The test job uses what you've learned: a round finds it, the scanner reads it, you fit the part, maybe a tuning step. Calder follows you (Torres on her days off). If it goes wrong, she tells you what to practise and sets another in a week.
  • Next morning at the board she hands you the Officer's badge, the Captain sends Mail, and you move into the spare cabin 4-23. The bigger story around promotion comes in Pillar 7.

Alongside Pillar 4, without getting in its way

  • A new part of the game file holds the work: one small block per mechanic, each with one owner. Take a block out and only that mechanic goes.
  • The machines and jobs part and your needs and activities part are ones Pillar 4 barely touches. Pillar 4's busy parts (the device, the crew's schedules, social, traditions) only get small asks, and mostly after Pillar 4's third wave.
  • The ladder starts on day 8, after Pillar 4's first week, and never touches the jobs that week holds for you. Its test runs on every change.
  • A second deck builder works in different rooms (the laundry, storage, Deck 5's machine rooms, your cabin) from Pillar 4's (mostly Deck 3).
  • Two "cross-pillar coordinators", as you asked, whose only job is keeping the two pillars straight: one checks every Pillar 5 change against Pillar 4's before it's combined (which parts of the file, names, save data, numbers), and one watches for the same thing being built twice (like the two tasting votes).
  • A second build master combines and tests Pillar 5's work. There's still one ship build and the lead releases it: each round Pillar 4 goes first, then Pillar 5 takes that release in, tests everything again, and goes next.

Risks, and what we do about them

  • Too much for a new player: one mechanic at a time, taught by a person, never two on a day.
  • Mechanics becoming mini-games: everything in the world with your hands, feedback from the machine itself, no timers or scores.
  • Phones slowing down: each mechanic's props are tiny (2 or 3 draw calls), only on their deck while in use, and measured against the 450 limit.
  • The game must play the same every time: faults, drift and incidents are worked out from the day and the machine, never by chance, and checked twice.
  • Two big pillars in one file: one new part with a block per mechanic, a second build master, two coordinators checking every change against Pillar 4's, and Pillar 4 released first each round.
  • Breaking your first week: the ladder starts on day 8, and Pillar 4's first-week test runs on every change.
