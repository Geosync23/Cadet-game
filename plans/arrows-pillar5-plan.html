Arrows for Pillar 5: Steps 1 and 2

The plan for arrows that teach every new thing in your Works career, from your first tracked shift to the end of your junior week (Days 1-8). Before the arrows, the list of what is broken today, found by playing build 0.64.15 in a browser. By AR1 (with AR2 on Step 2), 2026-10-22. Nothing in the game has been changed.

In short

The everyday work loop already has a good arrow: clock in, the job board, the part, the machine, turn it in, the next job. Played end to end it reads clearly at every step. What goes wrong is everything around it. The arrows the career adds (a meeting, a drill, the Chief's office, your new cabin) never go away when their moment is over, and while they are up they hide the work loop's arrow. And once you have taken a job, the career's meeting arrows never appear at all. On top of that, three things in the junior week cannot be done the way the game points you: the fire drill, the scanner lesson and the first burst pipe.

So the plan has three parts: fix the broken list first (the arrows would point at things that don't work), then one set of arrow rules (one arrow at a time, in a fixed order of importance, each one clearing itself), then the arrows themselves, moment by moment, in the order you meet them.

How the arrow works today

  • One arrow in the world. A gold ring with a pointer at the screen's edge when the spot is off screen, a bobbing pin when it's on screen, and a short label. Off your deck it says "Take the lift to Deck N".
  • Who gets it: anything that asks for it (a tracked appointment, a needs arrow, a career arrow) wins; otherwise your job's next step; otherwise, on a shift day, "Clock in" then "Take a job". There is no order of importance: whoever asks last wins, and some never let go.
  • Day 1's first-time arrows (clock, board, dinner, wardrobe, tea, call home) already work the right way: shown the first time you are told, never over someone else's arrow, gone once done or past its time. The new rules copy them.
  • Tips (the green card at the bottom) show once each and can't point. Rounds pairs a tip with each arrow step: the model for teaching.
  • No pointer inside panels. Nothing can point at a button on the job board, the clock screen, a device app or a card. That is new work.

The new arrow rules

1. One world arrow, one panel pointer, never more. The world arrow is the one we have. The panel pointer is new: the same gold ring, sitting on a button inside whatever panel is open (the job board, the clock screen, the device). Both are page elements over the 3D view, so neither adds draw calls on a phone.
2. A queue with an order of importance. Everything that wants the arrow asks the queue; the queue shows the most important one that is live now, and the rest wait their turn:
  • 1, right now: an emergency or a summons (the burst pipe call, the Chief's office, the drill in 10 minutes)
  • 2, in the next 15 minutes: a meeting or event you are expected at (a training, the inspection, a team job)
  • 3, while you are learning: the first-time steps of a new thing (watch the teacher, your first round)
  • 4, your shift: clock in, take a job, each step of your job, turn it in, clock out
  • 5, your body: food, toilet, sleep (raised to 1 when urgent, as today)
  • 6, things you chose: a tradition or place you tracked yourself (raised to 2 when it starts soon)
3. Every arrow knows when it is done. Each one comes with its own "done when" (you arrived, the meeting started, the job is taken, the time passed) and the queue clears it. Nothing can be left up by accident.
4. First time, then quiet. Teaching arrows (rule 2's level 3, and every panel pointer) show the first time, or the first two or three times for something you do daily, then never again (saved like tips, so a reload doesn't repeat them). The shift's own arrows (level 4) show every shift while tracking is on, as now.
5. Words with the arrow. The arrow's label stays short (about 30 characters). A teaching step can bring one tip with it (the existing green card) when there is more to say, as Rounds does.
6. Your choice still wins. If you switch tracking off, only levels 1 and 2 still show. If you track something yourself, it stays until it is done, and the queue's level 1 can only borrow the arrow and give it back.

How it could be built

Pieces that can go to separate builders at the same time without touching the same lines. Sizes: S = part of a turn, M = about one turn, L = two or three turns. Each piece is switched off until it is switched on, like every new feature.

MA. Fix the broken list (first, before any arrow). Clear career arrows when their moment ends, and stop the "is a job tracked" checks blocking them. Side jobs (the drill) get their taps and their arrow. The scanner lesson's part list. The burst pipe's after-talk and the job at the pipe. Lessons that flash past. The Tuning text. "What's next". Dev mode's tracking switch. The main lift's spot. Mostly owners' fixes in their own sections: the interactions seat (job arrow, side jobs), the Works career seat (section 24 core, 24p), seat M1 (drill), seat M2 (incidents, tuning), seat H (scanner and cases), Dev mode's seat, the writer (texts).
MB. The arrow queue (rules 2-6). One new small block in the interactions seat's slot: things ask the queue instead of setting the arrow; the queue picks, clears, remembers what was seen. The existing callers move to it one by one, each in its own section (so B lands first, alone; the moves ride with C-F). B also gives pop-ups a short queue, so a new message waits a moment instead of wiping the one on screen (today Torres's first invitation lasts under a second).
MC. The panel and device pointer. A gold ring that sits on a named button: a job board row, the clock screen, a device app tile, a tab, a card button. One at a time, only while that panel is open; when it isn't, it points at what opens it (the Device button). In the device seat's slot. Can be built at the same time as B.
SD. The shift chain (S1-S9). The queue entries for clocking in and out, the board, turning in, the empty-board line, the first Work log and reputation moments; the two label fixes. Needs B and C.

On a phone

  • No new draw calls: the arrow and the pointer are page elements drawn over the 3D view.
  • Never more than one arrow and one pointer on screen, and the pointer only inside an open panel.
  • The label stays short so it fits a 390-pixel screen beside the arrow; longer words go in a tip.
  • Teaching arrows show once (or two or three times for daily things) and are remembered, so the screen gets quieter as you learn.
  • Arrows don't pile up: anything waiting in the queue shows later or drops when its time has passed.
