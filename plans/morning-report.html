What's new to try

Days and sleep

  • Days are longer: a whole ship day takes 40 real minutes, and a working day (06:00 to midnight) about 30.
  • You can stay up 16 to 18 ship hours before you feel tired.
  • Get into your bunk after 20:00 and the night passes by itself: the screen fades and you wake at 06:00. Earlier than that, the game tells you it's too early.

Your needs and your device

  • Needs now help as well as hurt. When a need is high (75 or more) you get a bonus; when it's low (under 25), a penalty. The device shows a small up or down mark beside each one, with the reason.
  • A Mail tab on your device: notices about shortages, spoiled food, birthdays and ship traditions.
  • The store sells snacks and small comforts. You have a credit balance on the device.
  • Chatting with crew, and eating at a table with others, raises your social need.

Getting around

  • Tap any free chair or bench to sit down. Resting slows tiredness, and sitting near people chatting cheers you up a little.
  • You can squeeze past crew who are sitting or working at a station (they say something polite). People walking still block you.
  • Crawlways make you crouch.
  • The cargo hatch to the shuttle bay on Deck 5 is open.

Machines and jobs

  • You can see when machines are in trouble: worn screens dim, failing ones blink red, lights flicker, showers spray weakly, plants wilt.
  • Ship health boards in the Works bay (Deck 5) and on the bridge (Deck 1) list every system and how it's doing.
  • The Works crew now leave you about 6 small jobs a day on the day shift, of many kinds: swapping lamps, cleaning vents, calibrating galley machines, deliveries between decks, inspection rounds, small faults. Bigger and urgent ones they take straight away.
  • You can take the food cart run yourself from the job board. Meals you deliver go into the cold store when you unload them.
  • Tapping a machine tells you how it is and, where it makes sense, uses it (a washer, an oven).
  • You can use everything the crew use: the bar and tea table, reading seats, lounges, the garden beds, beanbags, the piano, guitars and record player, the laundry, the telescope and both observatories. Tap the Works bay job board to take or drop a job, and an arrow on screen shows you the way, even across decks ("Take the lift to Deck 5").

Life aboard

  • Tap a crew member and you'll see how they feel ("Tomas looks tired"), sometimes grumbling when the ship is in poor shape.
  • Everyone has a birthday, about one a week. The galley bakes a cake and the mess gathers after dinner. You have one too.
  • Small ship traditions, the Stardew kind: a weekly observatory night on Deck 2 and other little rituals through the week. Mail tells you what's on.
  • On the bridge, the watch changes over at 06:00 and 18:00, and the Captain takes an evening walk through the observation room.
  • The galley has a menu cycle: crew favourites, a Friday treat, and a Sunday dinner the crew voted for. Queues happen more often.
  • Officers' door plates show their names on Decks 2 and 4. Crew now carry trays, toolboxes and datapads.
  • Seated crew sit properly on their chairs now, at the right height and facing their tables: all 267 seats on six decks were measured and fixed.
  • Machines run late sometimes: about one delivery in five is late, and Stores sends you Mail when it is, or when a part runs out.

Decisions we made for you

You asked the lead to interpret your answers overnight. These are the choices that needed a call, and why. Tell us if any is wrong and we'll change it.

You said: days feel too short18 ship hours now take 30 real minutes.A full day is 40 minutes. Long enough to do a few jobs and have an evening, short enough that days still pass.
You said: going to bed should skip the nightFrom 20:00, your bunk fades straight to 06:00, with no question asked.Before 20:00 it says it's too early, so you can't skip the whole day by accident. The crew catch up on their night as they did before.
You said: needs should give bonuses tooHigh needs give a bonus, low ones a penalty, nothing in between.For example, high energy makes tasks 15% faster and being well fed makes you tire a little slower. We softened a few numbers so that "16 to 18 hours awake" still holds: an unfit cadet lasts about 16 hours, an average one 17, a fit one 18.
Not answered: should broken machines slow you down?No. Faulty machines slow the crew, never you.Kept as before, so faults feel like work to do rather than a punishment.
You said: faults for me, but balancedAbout 6 small jobs a day are left for you on the day shift.We aimed for 3 to 6 and landed at the top, because you also asked for more kinds of jobs. Small jobs wait three hours for you before the crew take them; the cart run waits one hour. Big and urgent ones the crew handle at once.
You said: tier locks, yesYou're Tier 1, a Works junior.Closets open for you; plant rooms (Tier 2) and batteries and junctions (Tier 3) come with promotion later.
You said: shortages simulated, and the game must tell meThey arrive in a simple Mail inbox on your device.The full device comes in Pillar 3; Mail is built so it carries over.
You asked: small traditions, like StardewOne new system runs every ship tradition, including observatory night and birthdays.Two helpers had built birthdays and observatory night separately; we merged them into one so nothing happens twice. It's also the base the Pillar 3 events will grow from.
You asked: let me sit anywhereTap a free seat to sit. Resting slows tiredness; company cheers you up.It uses the same seats and pose the crew use.
You asked: I should be able to use everything the crew useA new helper is giving you everything the crew use, plus taking jobs from the board with an arrow to guide you.The arrow is built so the future map on your device can use it too.
We found: you could get stuck behind a busy chefYou can now squeeze past anyone sitting or working at a station.Chef Brennan's station blocked the only way to the galley plant panel. People walking still block you, so the crowd still feels solid.
You said: the crew sitting looked wrongEvery seat was checked. 176 of 264 were off, and all are fixed.Some were too low, some beside the chair, some facing the wrong way at angled tables. Tall stools now have a footrest ring.
You said: show me faults on the decksOne shared system draws machine trouble on every deck.At first each deck builder made their own warning lamps, which clashed. One system is consistent and cheaper for your phone.
You said: birthdays, yesEveryone's birthday falls on a ship day; about one a week, yours included.A ship year is 52 weeks of 7 days, starting on your first day.
You said: shopping should buy thingsA credit balance on your device; the store sells snacks and small comforts.Snacks help a little with hunger, comforts with your mood, and they're kept in your locker.
We found: two helpers both drew a birthday cakeOne cake: the galley bakes it, the traditions side runs the gathering.The cake sits on the servery from 19:30 and everyone gathers in the mess at 20:15. The galley also bakes for Founding Day, and harvest days get fresh garden salad with dinner.
We found: small events were crowded outAn event's own seats are kept free for its guests while it's on.On one test day nobody got into the music jam because evening regulars had every seat. Holding only each event's own seats also stops a birthday from emptying quiz night when the two overlap.
Rank and locked roomsYou're Tier 1; junior Works crew are Tier 2.Plant rooms hold junior work (scrubbers, heating), so junior crew need to get in. You'll reach them with promotion.

Things you said no to, or "later"

  • No helping in the galley, no lunch, no grab shelf cleared overnight, store not open every day, the ship doesn't run while the game is closed.
  • Gym time slots and new gym kit: later (the Pillar 3 pitch suggests some).
  • The visitors' cabin stays a cabin, kept for guests during ship-wide events.
  • The ready room, officers' quarters and meetings: in the Pillar 3 pitch.
  • Your full device: its own big part of Pillar 3.
