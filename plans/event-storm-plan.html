A. "Sixty by lamplight" (recommended)

It comes straight from the ship's rota. Brennan's days off are Friday and Saturday, and the ship's facts already say Lukas Dlamini covers for her. So the storm's two worst days fall on Lukas's watch, and the lantern supper is his. He's a galley hand, eight months aboard, from Port Halloran: few words, dry, and he cares about one thing above all, that Brennan's galley doesn't slip on his watch. He sleeps in Bunk 1, where you started. If you've been promoted you moved out on Wednesday; if your promotion is running late, he's still your bunkmate, and his lines say whichever is true.

  • Friday evening, at the servery. The front hit at two. Lukas is on the galley line all through dinner. When you're near him in the dinner queue or the galley, he says a number, the way he does: "Sixty." Covers. Tomorrow. The lantern supper, by lamp light, on her day off. You can:
    • offer a hand tomorrow (he'll keep the lantern job for you);
    • tell him she left him in charge for a reason ("She left me a list. Eleven of the twelve lines say don't panic.");
    • ask what the worst could be (he just says the number again).
    If you don't see him before his bunk, he sends a Mail: "Lantern supper tomorrow. If you've got hands, I've got onions."
  • Saturday afternoon, the galley. The surge went through the ship at 11:20. When Lukas comes on at 14:30, the range is down to two rings of six: "Surge took four rings. Works has a list. I'm near the bottom. Sixty bowls on two rings. Or I go and knock on her door." Your one choice:
    • "I'm Works. Let me look at it." A small job is kept for you, whatever your rank: reset the galley range (about 20 minutes). Done before five, he has all six rings.
    • "Ask her. I'll go." You carry his question down to Brennan, wherever she is that afternoon (her cabin or a bench on Deck 4). She won't come in: "It's his night." She sends one line back: "Tell him: one pot, low and slow, lid on. And tell him it's his galley tonight."
    • "Two rings is plenty. Stew's one pot." He nearly laughs. His aunt was a pilot and cooked on two rings in a cockpit for twenty years. He makes her stew.
    If you're not there, nothing is lost: he manages on two rings, his way.
  • Saturday 18:00, the lantern supper. The mess by lamp light, about 55-60 people. Brennan comes to eat: she's off, and her own day already has her at dinner in the mess then. She finishes every bit and says one thing:
    • if you fixed the range: "Six rings on a storm night. Somebody's been in my galley with a spanner." A nod to you, then to Lukas: "Good."
    • if you asked her: "Low and slow. He listened. Nobody listens."
    • if he did it his way: "That's not my stew." A pause. "Make it again on Friday."
    When the last bowl goes out, Lukas says a number (the covers), and that's all he says.
  • Sunday. Brennan is back before five. The crew retell it ("Lukas ran the lantern supper on two rings. Or six. Depends who you ask."), and a storm lantern stays on the galley pass from then on.
  • If you skip all of it, the supper still happens, Lukas still runs it, and Brennan still eats it. You hear about it on Sunday.
  • No spoilers: Lukas only talks about tonight's supper and Brennan's galley, never his own worries, the person he married or his count of days. Brennan's lines never touch her own story (the restaurant, the soup, her book, any offer), and never hint at handing her galley on: it's his for tonight only. Calder never says "well done" (that's saved for her story). Nothing about the Veyli.
  • Cost: about one turn, and about 30-35 new lines from the writer.
