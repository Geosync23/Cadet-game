A full weekend on the Wren

You asked for traditions to fill the weekend, so that Saturday and Sunday have something going on all day. This page proposes nine new ones (eight, plus one optional) that slot into the gaps the current week leaves. Nothing here is built. Answer each one at the bottom: yes now, later, or no.

New traditions8 core + 1 optional, 4 or 5 on each day
RoomsAll in rooms the Wren already has. One needs your OK on a rule (the garden picnic)
BirthdaysNone of the new ones runs within an hour of a birthday party (20:15-21:10)
Busy rule keptNever more than two at once, never two in one room

Where the gaps are today

The weekend already has a good spine: the Saturday long breakfast, the garden hour, the Tiles ladder, film night and the observatory; on Sunday, the slow stretch, the swap meet, Winner Stays On on the rec deck, the reading corner, the long table and the Lap of the Wren. What's missing:

  • Saturday late morning (09:30-14:00) has nothing except the Sowing morning once a season.
  • Saturday early evening (16:30-20:00) is just dinner.
  • Saturday late night: the observatory ends at 22:35, and then the ship goes to bed.
  • Sunday early (before 08:00) and Sunday early afternoon (12:30-16:00) have only Winner Stays On, which is down on the rec deck.
  • Sunday late night: nothing after the Lap of the Wren ends at 21:15.

Saturday run

Dr. Nandakumar has started running before breakfast and tells everyone to try it. Every other Saturday she leads three easy laps of the promenade before the long breakfast opens. Nobody is timed. Commander Vance times everyone anyway.

Place
Promenade, Deck 3 (the route the Lap of the Wren already uses)
Who
Nandakumar always; runners and gym regulars about half; Vance most weeks; anyone else now and then. About 8-12.
You
Run the laps with them (a walking pace counts). Breakfast after, at the long breakfast.
You see
A small group jogging past the big windows; a chalk "3 laps" sign at the lift end.
Mail
"Saturday run tomorrow at seven. Three laps of the promenade, any speed. Then breakfast. Then water." (Nandakumar)
Build
Small to medium It uses the walking-gathering kind the Lap of the Wren already has; the jog is a faster walk.
SAT 10:00-11:30 · weekly

Mend-it morning

Pell sets out a mat and his screw tin on the big table in the junior common room, and people bring him anything small that's broken: a torch, a clock, a lamp, Ivo's hairdryer again. You can sit with him and fix things too.

Place
Junior common room, Deck 4
Who
Pell always; Works juniors about a third; people who "made" things half; anyone now and then. About 6-10, coming and going.
You
Sit and help. Each half hour there you get a "you fixed someone's..." line and a little Works standing, the same small amount a favour gives.
You see
A cloth mat covered in screws, a desk lamp, a short queue of people holding broken things.
Mail
"Mend-it morning tomorrow, ten till half eleven, common room. Bring your broken things. I'll bring the screw tin and the good lamp." (Pell)
Build
Small to medium A trad file and a mat-and-lamp decor. The "help" lines can be words only at first.
Note: in Pell's close-friend talk he dreams of "a repair corner in the common room". If you say yes, that talk gets reworded so it's about how the morning started, not a wish. The tradition's words stay off his mum's shop, which is part of his story and only comes up once you're friends.
SAT 13:00-13:50 · weekly

Garden picnic

Brennan's galley packs Saturday lunch in paper parcels. People take one from the servery and eat it in the garden on rugs under the big tree, just before the garden hour.

Place
Garden, Deck 3 (straight after the Sowing morning on its Saturdays; ends before the garden hour at 14:00)
Who
Garden lovers most; people who linger at meals; juniors; Cruz always. About 15-25.
You
Take a parcel and sit on a rug. It's your lunch, like any meal tradition.
You see
Checked rugs on the grass, paper parcels, someone lying on their back looking up into the tree.
Mail
"Picnic in the garden tomorrow at one. Lunch is in parcels at the servery. Rugs are by the tree. Mind the beds." (Chef Brennan)
Build
Medium A meal tradition outside the mess, rug props and new places to sit on them.
Needs your OK: your rule from 2026-10-17 says extra meal seats only appear inside the mess (no trestle table on the promenade). Rugs in the garden would be an exception. The other way to do it: no new seats, people take their parcel to the garden's existing benches and the edge of the beds.
SAT 17:00-17:50 · weekly

Every song

Five people aboard say they know every song in the record cabinet, and they don't agree on the words. Every Saturday before dinner they sing through the cabinet in the music room, one record at a time, arguing about the second verse. Anyone can join in.

Place
Music room, Deck 4
Who
Zeke Torres, Wen Takahashi, Olu Farouk, Lars Achterberg and Tamsin Haddad always; musicians and music lovers about half; anyone now and then. About 10-14.
You
Sing along. Now and then you're asked which words are right: three choices, none wrong, and the room takes sides.
You see
The record cabinet open, a record on, five people singing slightly different words at once.
Mail
"Every song, tomorrow at five, the music room. This week: the lighthouse one. I'm right about the second verse. Come and tell Takahashi." (Zeke)
Build
Small A trad file using the music room's places. The "which words?" choice is a short meeting, also small.
SAT 22:30-23:30 · weekly

Night owls' cocoa

For the people who aren't ready for bed after film night and the observatory. Davi Keane, off the night shift on Saturdays and wide awake as always, makes a big pot of cocoa in the bow lounge with the lights down low.

Place
Bow lounge, Deck 3 (free on Saturday nights; Tap night is Friday)
Who
Keane always; night owls most; film-night goers about a quarter; anyone now and then. About 8-12.
You
Sit with a mug. It gives social, and a little energy cost for staying up.
You see
The bow lounge's curved windows full of stars, low lamps, a big cocoa pot on the bar.
Mail
"Night owls' cocoa tomorrow from half ten, bow lounge. Lights low, cocoa high. Pyjamas welcome." (Davi Keane)
Build
Small A trad file using the lounge's places, plus a cocoa-pot decor.

Bread day

Already a line in the game: Zeke tells you on your first night that "Sunday's bread day; she lets juniors knead, if you're early." This makes it real. Brennan opens the galley early on Sundays and anyone who turns up kneads a loaf for the week's bread. (Chief Calder kneads too, very quietly. Only close friends find out.)

Place
Galley, Deck 3 (the working galley behind the servery)
Who
Brennan always; galley crew; early risers; juniors; Calder most weeks. About 6-10.
You
Knead a loaf. Your loaf turns up in Monday's breakfast basket ("{me}'s loaf" on a card).
You see
Flour on the galley counters, a row of tins, the ovens warming. The mess smells of bread by eight.
Mail
"Bread day tomorrow at seven, in the galley, love. Clean hands, rolled sleeves. You knead, I bake, and Monday's toast is yours." (Chef Brennan)
Build
Medium New kneading places at the galley counters, flour decor, and the Monday card.
SUN 13:00-15:30 · weekly

The big jigsaw

A huge jigsaw of the Wren sits on the table in the Observation room on Deck 1 all Sunday afternoon. People drift in and out and add a few pieces. Commander Vance always does the edges first. The picture fills in week by week, and when it's done a new one starts.

Place
Observation room, Deck 1
Who
Vance always; quiet-corner people and puzzlers most; anyone now and then. About 6-10 at a time, coming and going.
You
Add pieces (about 20 minutes). It counts as your puzzles hobby. You see how far the picture has got.
You see
A long table with the puzzle half done, its box lid propped up, a little dish of loose pieces.
Mail
"The jigsaw is out tomorrow from one, Observation room. Edges are done. Edges are always done first. That is not negotiable." (Commander Vance)
Build
Medium with a still picture. Large if the picture really fills in week by week and the save remembers it.
SUN 14:30-15:15 · every other week

Gerald clinic

Every cactus on the Wren is called Gerald, and they all came from Tove Cruz's cuttings. Every other Sunday Cruz holds a clinic in the garden: bring your Gerald for a look-over, a repot or a little water, and take home a new one if you haven't got one.

Place
Garden, Deck 3 (the potting bench end)
Who
Cruz always; the Gerald keepers (Janssen, Esposito, Alvarez, Walsh) always; gardeners half; anyone now and then. About 8-12.
You
Get your own Gerald the first time you go: a little cactus on your shelf. After that you bring it along.
You see
A row of small cacti in mismatched pots on the bench, Cruz murmuring to them.
Mail
"Gerald clinic tomorrow at half two. Bring your Gerald. Mm. Or come and get one. They're very easy. Mostly." (Tove Cruz)
Build
Small A trad file and cactus decor. Medium if your Gerald becomes a shelf item you keep.
SUN 22:15-22:55 · weekly

The Sunday story

A bedtime story for grown-ups. Chief Holt reads one chapter of a long, old adventure book to whoever comes to the junior common room, with the lights low. He does the voices, badly. Nobody admits they come for the voices. Holt is an early riser, so why so late? It's straight after his last round of the decks on Sunday, the one night of the week he lets himself sit down after it.

Place
Junior common room, Deck 4
Who
Holt always; juniors about a third; the book circle lot; night owls. About 10-15.
You
Listen, then go to bed. Miss a week and someone tells you what happened at breakfast.
You see
Holt in the big chair with the book, everyone else on beanbags and the floor.
Mail
"Chapter nine tomorrow at 22:15. The common room. Be on time. I don't recap." (Chief Holt)
Build
Small A trad file using the common room's places. The story itself is a writing job: a few lines per chapter, about a year of chapters.

The Sunday story runs at 22:15, not straight after the Lap of the Wren (21:15), so it keeps an hour clear of a 20:15 birthday party, as your new rule asks.
