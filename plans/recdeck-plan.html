1. Where it fits

  • Between Deck 4 (Quarters) and Deck 5 (Operations). The hull narrows from Deck 4's 50 × 20 to Deck 5's 44 × 16, and the rec dec sits in between at 46 × 18. It's on the daily path (sleep on 4, work from 5), so crew drop in after shift without a long ride.
  • No renumbering. Decks 5 and 6 keep their numbers. The lift panel shows an R button between 4 and 5; the lift says "Rec deck" and crew say "the rec dec". In-world, the Wren was refitted with it between two of its old decks (a line or two of crew lore, see section 9).
  • Double height. The auditorium's rake, the climbing wall, the pool's hull windows and the scoreboard want the height. The whole deck gets taller walls (about 1.6 × the usual); the small rooms (changing, office, washroom, booth) have a dropped ceiling line, drawn as a header band, so they still read as small rooms.

  • Lighting mood: the most colourful deck aboard. Each room has its own colour (below), joined by the concourse's neon stripes, cyan to port and magenta to starboard.

4. The disc game

Working name Lightdisc (crew just say "disc"; the name is an open question). A Tron-style disc and tennis game that plays like Pong: two players, one at each end of the court, return a glowing disc to each other. You only move; you hit back automatically.

The court, as you see it while playing
Rules
  • The point: get the disc past the other player's back line. It bounces off the force-field side walls with a flash; it passes through the net (the net only glows and counts returns).
  • First to 7, win by 2 (at 6-6, play on to two clear). A quick game is first to 5; league matches are quick games.
  • Serving: whoever lost the last point serves; the disc launches on its own after a one-second count, at an angle from the game's own random stream (seeded per game, so a game replays the same).
  • Speed: the disc starts slow and gets about 5% faster with every return in a rally, up to a cap, then resets for the next point. Long rallies get frantic.
  • Where it hits you: as in Pong, a disc that meets you off-centre goes back at a steeper angle (up to 60°); dead centre goes straight back.
  • Curve: if you're moving when it meets you, the disc curves the way you were moving. Swerving into a return is how you beat someone.
  • Charged shot: after three returns in a row your next return glows and goes a third faster. It's automatic: just keep the rally going.
Controls
  • Phone: put your thumb anywhere on your half of the screen and drag. Your player follows your finger across the court, and a little forward and back inside your zone (the shaded strip by your back line). Lift your thumb and you stop.
  • Desktop: the mouse the same way, or the arrow keys / A and D (W and S for forward and back).
  • Hitting: none. When the disc comes within reach, your player swings and returns it. Miss it only by being in the wrong place.
  • Leave game under the court, as in Tiles.

How it fits the game

  • Seen like Tiles: a panel over the game, the court drawn top-down in neon on a 2D canvas, your end at the bottom. Behind it, the two players stand on the real court in 3D in their ready poses. (Cheaper on phones than a live 3D game; a 3D version is an open question.)
  • Finding someone to play: exactly the Tiles way. Tap the court and you step onto your baseline; someone already waiting plays you at once, otherwise a free crew member on the deck (likes disc or has played you first, then a friend, then the nearest) walks over. Nobody free, or after 21:45: "Nobody's around to play right now."
  • Playing stops time, as in Tiles; the social comes in one go at the end (full for your first game with someone each day, a quarter after). A game also costs a little energy and gives leisure.
  • Crew levels: easy, steady and sharp, with a personality each: how fast they react (a quarter to half a second), how fast they move, how often they misjudge, and whether they like to curve. Pell swerves everything; Chief Holt plays straight and safe and never gives up a point; Commander Vance hits charged shots and talks while he does it; Rina is annoyingly good.
  • How to play: four short steps on your first game (move, the auto return, the curve, the charged shot), each moving on when you do it, with Next and Skip, under 62 characters a step.
  • Results: the scoreboard on the stern wall shows the score live; your record with each person is kept, and the crew talk about a good win.
  • Check: a new disc_check.py plays many seeded games at each level (balance: easy wins about a quarter of the time against a steady player, and so on), and checks the replay, the timing, the social and the save.
