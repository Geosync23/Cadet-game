1. Where it fits

  • Between Deck 4 (Quarters) and Deck 5 (Operations). The hull narrows from Deck 4's 50 × 20 to Deck 5's 44 × 16, and the rec dec sits in between at 46 × 18. It's on the daily path (sleep on 4, work from 5), so crew drop in after shift without a long ride.
  • No renumbering. Decks 5 and 6 keep their numbers. The lift panel shows an R button between 4 and 5; the lift says "Rec deck" and crew say "the rec dec". In-world, the Wren was refitted with it between two of its old decks (a line or two of crew lore, see section 9).
  • Double height. The auditorium's rake, the climbing wall, the pool's hull windows and the scoreboard want the height. The whole deck gets taller walls (about 1.6 × the usual); the small rooms (changing, washroom, utility) have a dropped ceiling line, drawn as a header band, so they still read as small rooms.
  • The spine lines up. Lift at [20, 9]: car on columns 19-20, rows 7-9, on the deck's centre line, trunks behind glass on row 6 and the ladder on row 10, at the same world spot as every deck.
  • Open from Day 1, 06:00 to midnight. From midnight to 06:00 the deck is closed for quiet (it lies under Deck 4's officers' corridor): the lights go down to a night glow, the pool and rivers lie still, and crew don't come. You can still ride through it, and Works jobs there still count.
  • Lighting mood: the most colourful deck aboard. Each room has its own colour (below), joined by the concourse's neon stripes, cyan to port and magenta to starboard.

4. Lightdisc

A Tron-style disc and tennis game that plays like Pong, played live on the real court in 3D: two players, one at each end, return a glowing disc to each other. You only move; you hit back automatically.

The game view on a phone
The camera
  • Tap the court (or its gate) and you walk to your baseline at the court's bow end, facing the stern. Over about 0.8 s the camera eases to the game view: behind and above your baseline, looking down the length of the court at about 55°. The opponent and the giant scoreboard are at the top of the screen and you're near the bottom, with the stands along one side.
  • Framed for phones: the court's full width fits the screen held upright, with your player about a fifth of the way up, so your thumb sits below you and never hides the disc. Held sideways, the camera drops a little lower and closer. The wall nearest the camera fades, as the cutaway already does.
  • Seeing the disc in depth: a ring on the floor under the disc, a short light trail behind it, a flash where it hits a side wall, and a pulse of light on each return.
  • At the end, the result sits over the court (Again, Done), and the camera eases back to your normal view.
  • Reduced motion: the camera cuts instead of easing, and there's no flash or shake.
Controls
  • Phone: put your thumb anywhere on the screen and drag. Your player follows your thumb's sideways position (left edge of the screen = left side of the court), not like a joystick: where your thumb is, you go. Dragging up and down moves you forward and back inside your zone (the shaded strip by your baseline). Lift your thumb and you stop.
  • Desktop: the mouse the same way, or the arrow keys / A and D (W and S for forward and back).
  • Hitting: none. When the disc comes within reach (a generous tile), your player swings and returns it.
  • Score small at the top, and big on the scoreboard in the scene. A Leave button in a corner. Nothing else on screen.
Rules
  • The point: get the disc past the other player's back line. It bounces off the force-field side walls with a flash and passes through the net (the net only glows).
  • First to 7, win by 2. A quick game is first to 5 (Winner Stays On plays quick games).
  • Serving: whoever lost the last point serves. The disc launches on its own after a one-second count, at an angle from the game's own random stream (seeded per game, so a game replays the same).
  • Speed: the disc starts slow and gets about 5% faster with every return in a rally, up to a cap, then resets for the next point.
  • Where it hits you: as in Pong, off-centre sends it back at a steeper angle (up to 60°); dead centre goes straight back.
  • Curve: if you're moving when it meets you, the disc curves the way you were moving.
  • Charged shot: after three returns in a row, your next return glows and goes a third faster. Automatic.

How it fits the game

  • Finding someone to play: the Tiles way. Tap the court; someone waiting on the other baseline plays you at once. Otherwise a free crew member on the deck walks over (someone who likes Lightdisc or has played you comes first, then a friend, then the nearest). If nobody is free, or the deck is closed: "Nobody's around to play right now."
  • Playing stops the ship's clock, as in Tiles, but the scene stays alive: people nearby keep their idle movements, and the stands cheer and groan at each point. The social comes in one go at the end (in full for your first game with someone each day, a quarter after that). A game also costs a little energy and gives leisure.
  • The game runs at a fixed 60 steps a second, whatever the frame rate, so a slow phone plays the same game, just less smoothly.
  • Crew levels: easy, steady and sharp, each with a personality: reaction time (a quarter to half a second), speed, how often they misjudge, and whether they like to curve. Pell swerves everything; Chief Holt plays it straight and safe; Commander Vance hits charged shots and talks while he does it; Rina is annoyingly good.
  • Crew against crew: two crew can play a real, visible game on the court (pick-up games, and Winner Stays On). It's the same game code with both sides played by the computer, seeded so it always comes out the same. Off-screen, the result is worked out on paper and nothing is drawn.
  • How to play: four short steps on your first game (move, the auto return, the curve, the charged shot). Each moves on when you do it, with Next and Skip, and each is under 62 characters, shown at the bottom of the screen.
  • Results: your record with each person is kept, and crew talk about a good win.
  • Check: a new disc_check.py plays many seeded games at each level (for balance), checks that a game replays the same and that its timing is the same at different frame rates, and checks the social, the save and crew-against-crew games.
