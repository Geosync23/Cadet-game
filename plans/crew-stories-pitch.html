Crew stories: who gets one, how they're told, and where they land

Your brief: narrative arcs for some of the crew, mostly dialogue, tender and building to an emotional high point, simple to make, and more of them than one playthrough can hold. A lot of Works folks, Pell and Zeke for sure. This page is a pitch to talk over. Nothing here is built or written yet, and the answers form at the bottom keeps whatever you tick and type.

The short version

  • We aren't starting from nothing. Ten crew already have a story in the game, told as sit-down chats one step at a time (Pell, Zeke, Calder, Kovac, Tomas, Rina, Brennan, Vance, Captain Marrow, Holt). They're gentle, but they end quietly in a chat card. None of them has the big moment you're asking for.
  • The new idea is the crescendo. Every crew story ends in one staged scene with other people there, at a real place and time, that hits harder than a chat. To keep it simple, each crescendo rides on a gathering that already happens: a Friday tea, the send-off in the shuttle bay, Knit and natter, the storm, Postcard night, your night shift. Nobody's day has to be re-planned.
  • 11 candidates: 9 from Works (Pell, Zeke, Calder, Kovac, Halloran, Theo Teague, Basil Bramwell, Leila Eklund, Saad Salo) and 2 new ones from elsewhere (Dev Raman on the helm, Anja Kowalczyk on the night bridge). The six non-Works stories already in the game (Tomas, Rina, Brennan, Vance, Marrow, Holt) can stay as they are or be upgraded later.
  • You can't see them all. A crescendo needs a close friend, and in the first month you'll make maybe four to six. Several stories also have a window: Theo leaves on Day 21, Basil only arrives that day, Leila's day is fixed, Kowalczyk's only happens on your night shift.
  • My recommendation: build 8, write Pell, Zeke and Theo first, and keep it tender but never bleak: grief and illness happen to people off the ship, told gently, and nobody dies on screen.
Where we start

What the game already has

The pieces a crew story needs are already built and working in the game. This is what I'd use, unchanged:

  • Story moments. A crew member walks up when you're both off duty and asks you to sit with them. Their lines play one by one, you pick one reply out of three (listen, cheer up, practical, share), and there's never a wrong answer. Each step opens with friendship and time known, at most one heavy moment a day.
  • Meetings. Scenes with several speakers, faces and names, one or two choices (the training scenes, First Light Cocoa, the storm briefing). This is what a crescendo is made of.
  • Favours that matter. A practical reply can put a personal job in your Jobs app (fetch blue sage from the garden, help Pell practise in the machine shop, service the lift's brakes).
  • Mail, keepsakes and memory. A Mail can follow a moment; a resolution leaves a keepsake on your shelf; every reply is remembered, so later lines can call back to it.
  • Traditions and gatherings with times, places, Calendar entries and Mail the evening before. The rotation on Day 21 (Theo out at 15:00, Basil in at 17:40), the farewell supper, the shuttle bay send-off and the new hand's tea are all on the calendar already.
  • Goodbyes. A leaver's friends get a goodbye conversation, and can say they miss them afterwards.

Also there: twelve shared "struggle themes" (homesick, exams, loss, burnout and so on) that give the other sixty-odd crew a short three-step thread made from fill-in templates. Those stay as the background hum. Crew stories are the hand-written, foreground ones.

1 · How a crew story is told

Four quiet steps, then one big one

Every crew story has the same simple shape, so the writers have a pattern and the engine has nothing new to learn. Steps 1 to 4 are ordinary story moments (a chat card that opens into a short scene). Step 5 is the crescendo.

Step 1 · colleague

The spark

Something small and funny you notice: a comic pinned in the common room, a scarf that's far too long, a joke nobody laughs at. Often it's their card line or a bit of chatter first.

Step 2 · colleague

The thread

They let you in a little. A light moment, and usually a favour you can choose to do: the player's part in the story.

Step 3 · friend

The weight

The heart of it, told plainly at a quiet hour: the dad who's forgetting, the shop at home, the day each year. One choice. You can't fix it; you can be there.

Step 4 · friend

The turn

They decide something, or ask you something. What you did in steps 2 and 3 shows here.

Step 5 · close friend, or its day

The crescendo

A short scene with other crew there, at a gathering that happens anyway. It's on your Calendar like any appointment. A keepsake, a Mail after, and the crew talk about it for a day or two.

How a story starts

  • With a moment, not a menu: the person comes over when you're off duty, or the hint shows on their card ("Got a minute?").
  • Only after week 1 (your training week stays clear), and only once you've met them a few days.
  • A story that has a window (Theo, Basil) starts earlier and asks less friendship, so it can still land in time.

How it moves on

  • By friendship and time: each step needs a tier, some days known, and two days' gap after a heavy one.
  • By what you did: a favour taken or not changes their next lines, and which version of the ending plays.
  • At most one heavy moment a day for you, and at most one crescendo a day.

How it lands

  • The crescendo is put on your Calendar when the story is ready for it, with a Mail the evening before ("Pell's practical is tomorrow at the open-door tea. He says don't come. Come.").
  • Be there and it plays. Miss it and it still happens: a Mail tells you how it went, the crew talk, and the story ends with a smaller, kind moment later.
  • There's no bad ending. A full ending if you were part of it, a quiet one if you weren't.

Why you only see some

  • Friendship: a workmate you eat and work with reaches friend in one to two weeks and close in about three; people outside Works take longer. So a month gives you four to six close friends, and that many crescendos.
  • The calendar: leavers, arrivals, fixed days and the night shift open and shut windows.
  • Your department: the Works stories come to you easily; the bridge and helm ones you have to go looking for.
  • Your choices: who you sit with, which favours you take.

Around the big days

  • Week one stays training: no story steps until it's done.
  • Crossing the Line (Day 7) is a spark day: two or three stories open there with a light step (Theo sketches the ceremony; Raman flew the crossing).
  • The storm (Days 11-15) holds back every heavy step except Salo's, which is about the storm.
  • Fridays are good crescendo days: everyone's off from 15:30 and gathered at four.

Rotation and career

  • Day 21: Theo leaves, Basil arrives. Leavers' stories must finish by their last day; arrivals' stories start late and move faster. Later rotations work the same way.
  • Your promotions are never in the same hour as a crescendo (the same guard that spaces the Captain's scenes).
  • Calder's and Zeke's crescendos sit on career days on purpose, so your promotions feel like they mean something to someone.
4 · How simple it is to make

Mostly writing, with four small pieces of code

Already there, used as is

  • The story-moment engine: steps, gates by friendship and time, places, choices, flags, keepsakes, Mail after.
  • Meeting scenes with several speakers and choices.
  • Favours that put a personal job in your Jobs app.
  • The traditions, the rotation, the send-off, the storm and the career days, all with times and places.
  • The writers' story file format (one plain-text file per story) and the checker for it.
  • The four existing Works stories, already written.

Small new pieces

  • A "day" condition for a step: from Day N, until Day N (for Theo, Basil, Eklund). One small addition to the condition list.
  • A crescendo hook: when you're at a named gathering and a story is ready, its scene plays there. It uses people who are there already, so no one's day is re-planned.
  • A Calendar entry and Mail the evening before a crescendo, and a Mail after if you missed it.
  • A pace rule: at most one crescendo a day, and the quieter ending when a window closes.
  • The rest is writing: 7 new story files and 11 crescendo scenes, plus a line each for the people who are there.

Rough size: the four code pieces are one engineer's turn or two in the social stories section, switched off until you've played them. The writing is a writer's job of about two to three days of sessions per batch of four stories. Kovac's lift ride, Salo's storm fault and Kowalczyk's lights are the three that touch another owner (the lift, the storm, the bridge), so they'd go to those owners first.

5 · Open questions, with my recommendations

Your answers

Each question has my recommendation ticked to start with. Change anything, add a comment anywhere, then press Save. You can come back and change your answers; the latest save is the one we use.

1. How many crew stories should we build?

A player sees four to six in a month. Building more than they can see is what makes a second playthrough feel different.

2. Which should be written first?

Pell and Zeke are your must-haves and already half written. Theo is the one that proves the "window" idea, and the rotation is already built.

3. How dark or sad may they get?

You asked for tender and touching. The game's current rule is: grief is for people off the ship, told gently, and nobody dies on screen.

4. Endings: can a story go wrong?

The game never fails you anywhere else. A story with a window can close before you get there.

5. Repeats: what about a second playthrough?

Stories are told once per save. The question is whether a new game should feel different.

6. Zeke's crescendo on the evening you take the job he wanted?

It's the most bittersweet beat in the set: you become Engine Specialist on Day 23, the job he's been passed over for twice, and that evening he gives you the scarf.

7. The existing stories: rework or leave?

Pell, Zeke, Calder and Kovac already have written steps. The pitch keeps their words and adds a crescendo scene to each.

8. How busy may the last week get?

A player close to Works could have a crescendo on Days 23, 24, 25 and 26 in a row, in the same week as the Veyli visit.
