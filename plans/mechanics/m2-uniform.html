In plain words

  • Already in 0.60.7: you change every morning at your wardrobe; after 5 days you take the hamper to a washer and back; if you don't change by 10:00 you're "in yesterday's clothes". No laundry duty.
  • New: some jobs are oily (ovens, the range, the extractor, the galley plant, cart docks, the lift, pumps, the main fan). After a couple of them your uniform is grubby: it looks a shade duller (a tint, nothing new drawn), and people notice.
  • A shower doesn't clean it; a change does: tap your wardrobe and change (it uses a clean outfit, even if you already changed today).
  • Never a fail: grubby costs a little hygiene and a remark or two, and matters on inspection mornings and at the officer ceremony.

Day 8, Monday

Your first oily job turned in on Day 8 or later (from Day 8, oily jobs make you grubby): Torres catches you at the board or the tea corner. "You've got half the galley plant on you. Change before tea. Calder notices." A line on the Training page: "Your uniform: change after oily jobs."

Day 8's big new thing is your first incident with Calder (M6); this is its small one.

Why it matters

  • Hygiene: grubby drains hygiene 1.5× (the same as "yesterday's clothes"; they don't add up).
  • Remarks: crew may say something (the existing path, simNote('uniform')), friends kindly, Calder briefly.
  • The Thursday inspection (M3) checks a clean uniform; the officer ceremony (Day 11, Step 3) does too.
  • The washers wear with your loads, as with the crew's, so you see their faults coming (and, later, service them).
