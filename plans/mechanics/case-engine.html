In plain words

Some faults don't show their cause. The crew call them head-scratchers. The job names a symptom ("the lamp in Bunk 2 flickers"); the device's Fault notes lists the suspects (the parts or causes it could be); you walk the ship collecting clues (a machine's log, what someone saw, the last shift's note, a gauge, the calendar), cross suspects out, and call it. Then it's an ordinary job: fetch the part, fit it by hand, turn it in.

  • Every clue is real. It comes from what really happened on the ship: who was where, how hard the machine worked each hour, who tried what. Ask the same person twice, you get the same answer.
  • Every case has exactly one answer, checked before you ever see it. If a case can't be made fair, it simply stays a plain repair.
  • No timers, no scores, no fail. A wrong call costs a walk and some time (from Tier 2, a used seal or cartridge). There's always the long way: fit each suspect in turn. And there are hints: the Chief's notes, a word from a Works hand, talking it through with Torres or Calder.

This page is the machinery. What your first case looks like is on p3-whats-been-tried.html; each later kind gets its own page with its step.

A case's life

  1. PostedA real fault becomes a caseA fault on a machine at your tier, on a shift day, of a shape some open puzzle kind can use; then a roll at the rank's rate. It's built, checked and frozen at once.
  2. On the boardA head-scratcher card"Head-scratcher: Bunk 2 lamp flickers", with a small mark. Taking it opens its Fault notes.
  3. CluesWalk, look, askEach clue place or person gives its clue when you get there ("+ clue: Pell"). A "Where next?" button points the arrow at a clue you haven't got.
  4. Cross outYou decideTap a suspect to cross it out (tap again to bring it back). The game never crosses anything out for you.
  5. Call itName the causeThe job's part is set and the arrow points at storage. Right: fetch, fit, done ("Right first time"). Wrong: the machine tells you when you fit it ("New bulb in. Still flickers."), and the case goes on.
  6. Not takenThe crew solve it the long wayIf you don't take it by the end of your shift, it goes to the crew, who take longer (a part at a time). The ship never waits on you.

The promises (the approved rules, in short)

  • Clues are in the world; the device only holds your notes. The fix stays physical.
  • Words, not numbers, for you ("Right first time", "A clever fix"); a machine's gauge may show its reading, as the scanner does.
  • Only real faults, never servicing, chores, hauling, the drill, an incident's make-it-safe step or a team job.
  • No setting for more or fewer (the user).

The fault tables (what can go wrong with each kind of machine)

For each kind of machine, a short list of causes, written in plain words by the designer (Tier 1 kinds first). Each says what's wrong, what you'd notice, when it shows, and what each kind of clue would show. They're data in section 17 (FAULT_CAUSES, the foundation's 17.4); real faults use them to pick a cause, and cases use them to pick suspects and clues.

FAULT_CAUSES.washer = [ { id: 'pump', part: 'pump', w: 3, shows: 'leaks', sign: 'puddle under it', when: 'after-use' }, { id: 'seal', part: 'seal', w: 2, shows: 'leaks', sign: 'drips at the door', when: 'after-use' }, { id: 'valve', part: 'valve', w: 2, shows: 'leaks', sign: 'water left in the drum', when: 'after-use' }, ... ]

shows is the job card's symptom (causes that share it can be suspects together); sign is what you see up close (different within a card). when is one of always, peak, after-use, warm, touched, time, with:<kind>. The full tables, in plain words, are fault-tables.html; every part they name is already in STOCK.

Puzzle kinds as data

CASE_KINDS[id] = { name, ladder, fits(cause, m), suspects(j), clues(j, cause, sus, seed), view(c), hint(c) }, one per puzzle kind, each registered by its own sub-block (P3's on p3-whats-been-tried.html; the others with their steps). The engine knows nothing about any one kind.

For the builder · the solver (every case, at posting)

  • Exactly one answer: apply every reachable clue's kills; exactly one suspect is left, and it's the cause.
  • Needs thinking: no single clue leaves one suspect; the smallest set of clues that does (brute force over subsets: at most 6 clues, 64 sets) is at least the band's "fewest".
  • Reachable: each clue's place exists on its deck and isn't behind a locked door for you (hatchPassable, room locks); each person is aboard (movedOff false), on the Roster and somewhere you can find them during the case's window (their plan, planDay); each tool is one you have (hasTool).
  • In its band: suspects, clues and decks within the band's numbers.
  • Fails: next sub-seed (k + 1), up to 8 tries; then it's a plain fault (j.case unset), counted in CASE_STATS.fallback for puzzle_check. A case that passes is frozen and never re-checked, so the ship moving on can't change it; if a clue person leaves the ship mid-case, that clue is given by Mail ("Before I go: it stuck at 17:10. Rina").
  • Cost: under 5 ms per case on the phone profile (puzzle_check measures).

For the builder · calling it, wrong calls, hints, the long way

  • Call it (caseCall(caseId, suspectId)): asks "Call it: the relay?" (Yes / Not yet); sets the job's parts to the suspect's part (or makes it a tuning step for a setting, once M5 is in); the arrow points at storage; the job's normal loop runs.
  • Right: the job finishes as any repair; SHIP.works.log flags right first time (no wrong call) and a clever fix (right first time, no talk-it-through, clues collected ≤ the fewest + 1); simNote('cleverFix', { machine, kind }); reputation as the foundation's 24.4.
  • Wrong: at the machine, after fitting, one line from the machine (FAULT_CAUSES' words: "New bulb in. Still flickers."); the wrong suspect is crossed out for you by the evidence (it's now known), the part goes back to the shelf unused (Tier 1) or is spent (Tier 2 seals and cartridges, the user's choice); the case goes on. After two wrong calls, Torres or Calder offers to talk it through (a toast, an appointment if you say yes).
  • Hints, gentlest first: the Chief's notes (free, always); Ask a Works hand at the board or tea corner: what kind of clue you're missing ("Have you read the notes on the job?"), free; talk it through with Torres or Calder: they cross one decoy out with you (no clever fix, still right first time); the long way: "Fit them one by one" on the case, which turns it into a plain job that fits each suspect's part in turn (always ends).
  • Not taken by shift end (17:00, or dropped): released to the crew, who do it the long way on paper (jobRun segments, one part each); no remark.

Builders' questions

How does a sub-block add a tab to the Works app? (seat H, 2026-10-18)

24 core exposes worksTabAdd({ id, title, order, view, act }); the works app shows its own tabs (Training, Work log, Tools) and every added tab in order. Sub-blocks call it typeof-guarded at load. Before 24 core is in a build, 24k registers a stand-in app deviceApp({ id: 'faultnotes', title: 'Fault notes', icon: 'wrench', order: 32, ... }), hidden while no case kind is open; once worksTabAdd exists, 24k uses it and doesn't register the stand-in.

When is a puzzle kind "open"? (seat H, 2026-10-18)

When its ladder row has opened, not when you're trained in it: Object.values(SHIP.works.op || {}).some(ids => ids.includes(kind.ladder)), read defensively. (Trained is set only when the first case is solved, and the first case is posted after the row opens, so "trained" would never let the training case appear.) puzzle_check's stand-in kind writes its own entry in SHIP.works.op.
