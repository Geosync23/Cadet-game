P3 · What's been tried? (Day 5, Halloran): your first head-scratcher spec for approval

The gentlest kind of head-scratcher, and the first you meet, the day you get the scanner. Notes left by the crew who already tried something tell you what it isn't. In plain words

  • The idea: the crew have had a go already. Taped to the job (and on the machine's panel) are their notes: what they tried and what happened. Each thing someone tried and that didn't fix it is crossed off your list. What's left is the cause.
  • The rule of thumb (the first page of the Chief's notes): "If someone fitted it and it's still wrong, it isn't that."
  • The notes are true: the crew really did try those things earlier, on their shift; you can even ask them.

A change from the plan's example (for the lead)

The machine. The plan's example was "the lamp in Bunk 2 flickers" on Deck 4's light relays, but Deck 4 has no light-relay machine with a service panel in the game (its lamps hang off the Tier 3 power junctions in the closets), so the first case is a laundry washer instead: Tier 1, real, with a panel (4-washer-N-svc), in the junior wing you know from your Day 2 laundry, with a puddle you can see. The lamp case stays as an example for Deck 3's or Deck 1's light relays later.

The clues. The plan's example also had Pell's note say "Reset the breaker: fine for an hour, then back", and the answer "a relay warming up". But that one note on its own rules out the lamp, the switch and the wiring together, so it breaks the engine's rule that no single clue may solve a case (the solver would refuse it). Here each note rules out exactly the thing that was tried, so you need both. "Fine for an hour, then back" stays as a later P3 clue on harder cases, where it narrows two or three suspects among five.

Later P3 cases

  • From Day 6 to 9, about 1 in 10 of your real repairs is a P3 case on a Tier 1 machine (the case engine's table), with 3-4 suspects and 2-3 notes.
  • Notes come from the people who really tried (whoever was on shift), at the job (taped to the card), at the machine's panel, or in the Works log (once officers write it, M12: your own lines come back as notes).

Doesn't the scanner give the answer away? (reviewer R, 2026-10-18)

It did: with M7's normal rule the cause is always the lowest bar. On a head-scratcher's job every suspect reads in the same "worn" band (40-60, hashed, unrelated to the cause; new parts read worn while they bed in), and non-suspects read good. The scanner narrows the field to the suspects and stops there; the clues decide. The reading is not a clue in the solver. Day 5's case: pump, door seal and drain valve all read worn.
