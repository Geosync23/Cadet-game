In plain words

  • The verb: at a faulty machine's panel, look it over with the scanner: the device shows its 3-5 parts, each with a reading (a bar and a word: good, worn, low, failing). Usually one is clearly off. Sometimes two are, and what you saw on the deck (the job says "runs cool"; you saw the door seal gap) tells them apart. You choose the part to fetch.
  • A wrong pick costs a walk back, never a fail: "Fitted a new seal. Still runs cool: it's the element."
  • Where it lives: in your locker and on the Works app's Tools page; never in your hands' two slots.

  • Readings are machine numbers (like any gauge); your progress is still never a number.
  • When two read low, what you see up close (the look-over's line: "cool all over" or "cool by the door") and the Chief's notes ("Cool all over: the element. Cool by the door: the seal.") tell them apart.

A wrong pick

You fetch the part and fit it; the machine tells you ("Still runs cool: it's the element"), the wrong part goes back on its shelf unused (Tier 1; at Tier 2 a used seal or cartridge is spent), and the job now names the right part. A walk and a few minutes, nothing worse.
