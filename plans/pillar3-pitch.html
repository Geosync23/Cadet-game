The ship gets a voice

The Wren already works: the galley cooks, machines wear out, the Works crew fix them and so can you. Pillar 3 gives that ship a voice. Your personal device becomes the one screen you use for everything, and it looks like an old-school screen. The crew become people you know, who remember you, chat, invite you places and ask for favours. Ship-wide events begin, starting with the ion storm: a few days when the whole ship changes and everyone talks about it. There are no cutscenes and no mission markers, and the game never stops for a story.

This is what you agreed. The full plan is in plans/pillar3-plan.md.

What you'd see and do

  • Walking through the mess, you overhear someone say "Sato hasn't left the chart room since last night", and later find it in your news feed.
  • You tap Pell and get a short chat, with buttons to chat, ask about something, offer help, or meet up.
  • You make an appointment to go to the gym with Tomas at 18:00, and he's there.
  • A friend asks you to fix the lamp in their cabin. It shows up in your jobs, and the same on-screen arrow you use for any job points the way.
  • The Captain's face appears across the top of the screen with a ship-wide announcement. The crew around you stop and look up for a moment, then carry on.
  • At the start of shift, Chief Calder gathers the Works crew at the job board. Her lines play one by one in a panel at the bottom of the screen, and you can offer to take a job, while the ship carries on around you.
How ambient events work

The ion storm, through your eyes

You're never given a mission. You hear about it the way everyone does, and help as much as you choose to.

  1. Day 1, morning: a rumour

    Nothing official yet. At the start of shift, Calder holds a quick storm briefing at the Works bay job board: her lines play in the panel, and you can offer to take the bridge battery run. At breakfast a Survey officer says the sensors are "doing something odd". By dinner, half the mess is talking about it. A Works notice lands in your Mail: storm prep, secure loose fittings, top up filters. New jobs appear on the board, such as securing the bow lounge's glassware and carrying battery packs to the bridge. You take some of them, or you don't. That's up to you.

  2. Day 2, 14:00: the storm hits

    You're fixing a lamp on Deck 4 when the screen jolts, every light dips, and then they start to flicker. The Captain's announcement appears across the top of the screen:

    "We're in it. Expect flickers and no calls home for a while. Look after each other."

    The call-home booths close, the lounge is shut, the mess is quiet, and bridge crew are on double shifts.

    felt on every deckthe Captain's address
  3. Day 3: it gets harder

    Relays fail, and one deck at a time drops to dim emergency lighting. The chef makes storm stew and sends trays up to the bridge; you can push the cart. People are tired and snappy when you tap them. Each job you do keeps the lights on for someone.

    more faultstired crew
  4. Day 4, mid-morning: all clear

    The Captain gives the all-clear in the observation room, and it goes out to every deck as an announcement. Calls home come back on, and there's a queue at the booths all afternoon. In the evening there's a party in the bow lounge.

  5. Afterwards: no score, just the ship
    If it went smoothlyThe storm crew's signed duty roster goes up in the Works tea corner, and people thank you.
    If it went roughlyThere's a scorch mark in the relay room and the ship is grumpy for a couple of days, then it recovers.

    You can't fail. If you did your part you may get a commendation, which counts toward promotion. If you slept through it, the crew coped, and you hear all about it the next day.

Big moments, in four kinds

Felt everywhere

The jolt, the lights dipping, a nebula filling every window. It happens wherever you are.

The Captain's address

A banner on every deck. The crew stop and look up, and time keeps running.

Gatherings

Announced ahead, at a real place and time. If you're there, you see it; if not, people tell you about it.

Meetings with dialogue

A briefing, a summons, a greeting. If you're there, the lines play one by one, sometimes with a choice of reply. You can always walk away and get the gist in Mail.

Never a full cutscene, and the camera is never taken away from you.

Your device

It opens from a corner button or a swipe up. On a phone it slides up from the bottom and leaves the ship visible; on the unfolded TriFold it sits at the side. Time keeps running while it's open.

The look: an old-school screen, like looking at an actual device's display. It has fine interlaced scan lines, a soft phosphor glow, slightly curved, darkened edges and chunky letters, all set in a sturdy frame. Any flicker is optional and switches off on phones set to reduce motion. It's built to stay crisp at phone size and cost nothing extra to draw. Getting to know the crew

  • Stranger, acquaintance, colleague, friend, close friend.
  • You get closer by working, eating and chatting together, and by doing favours. Friendships don't fade.
  • Friends come and find you, invite you places and put in a good word with the Chief.
  • The crew have friendships and rivalries with each other.
  • Tomas, Pell and Rina get the first personal storylines.

Moving up

  • Summoned to the Captain's ready room after an event.
  • Taken by the Chief to a briefing at the conference table.
  • Invited to tea in an officer's cabin.
  • Dinner at the Captain's table.
  • Progress talks in the Chief's office.

The order of work

  1. The device (in its old-school look), relationships and chats, and appointments, built side by side, starting from what your phone test shows.
  2. Check-in 1 with you: try the new device, pick its screen colour, and try the social life.
  3. The ion storm from start to finish, with its two meetings, and the first visit to the ready room.
  4. Check-in 2 with you: play through a whole storm.
  5. Then down the event list, with a review after each main event.

What you decided

The device

Keep the swappable layers, but build the real look now: an old-school screen. Add all the extra apps except Camera.

Ambient events

Agreed, and an event can also call a meeting with dialogue, never a full cutscene.

The event order

Keep the whole list, in the order above. The ion storm is built first.

Everything else

Yes to all ten: the device never pauses time; Maps shows people you know; a missed appointment costs only a remark; your own part counts toward commendations; ordinary visitors weekly; friendship tiers and short chats, with Tomas, Pell and Rina first; visits by invitation; more gym kit and simple sounds; two new helpers.
