# Pillar 3: the ship gets a voice

*Agreed with you on 16 October 2026. The full plan is in `plans/pillar3-plan.md`.*

## The idea in a paragraph

The Wren already works: the galley cooks, machines wear out, the Works crew fix them and so can you. Pillar 3 gives that ship a voice. Your **personal device** becomes the one screen you use for everything, and it looks like an old-school screen. The **crew become people you know**, who remember you, chat to you, invite you places and ask for favours. **Ship-wide events** begin, starting with the ion storm: a few days when the whole ship changes and everyone talks about it. There are no mission markers or cutscenes, and the game never stops for a story. You live through each event as one of the crew and decide how much to help.

## What you'd see and do

- Walking through the mess, you overhear someone say "Sato hasn't left the chart room since last night", and later find it in your news feed.
- You tap Pell and get a short chat, with buttons to chat, ask about something, offer help, or meet up.
- You make an appointment to go to the gym with Tomas at 18:00, and he's there.
- A friend asks you to fix the lamp in their cabin. It shows up in your jobs, and the same on-screen arrow you use for any job points the way.
- The Captain's face appears across the top of the screen with a ship-wide announcement. The crew around you stop and look up for a moment, then carry on.
- At the start of shift, Chief Calder gathers the Works crew at the job board. Her lines play one by one in a panel at the bottom of the screen, and you can offer to take a job, while the ship carries on around you.

## Your device

It opens from a corner button or a swipe up. On a phone it slides up from the bottom and leaves the ship visible; on the unfolded TriFold it sits at the side. **Time keeps running while it's open.**

**The look (your choice):** an old-school screen, like looking at an actual device's display. It has fine interlaced scan lines, a soft phosphor glow, slightly curved, darkened edges and chunky, readable letters, all set in a sturdy frame. Any flicker is optional, and it switches off on phones set to reduce motion. It's built to stay crisp at phone size and cost nothing extra to draw. The helper building it will show you three screen colours to choose from: amber, green, or a cyan that matches the Wren's teal.

| App | What it's for |
| --- | --- |
| Schedule | Your day: shift, meals, appointments, gatherings |
| Appointments | Meet a crew member, see the infirmary, talk to the Chief. Crew answer from their real schedule and turn up |
| Maps | Every deck, where you are, find a room or a friend; the on-screen arrow points the way |
| Memos | Your own notes |
| Mail | Messages from the ship, the crew, the Captain and home |
| Calendar | Ship traditions, birthdays, and events once they're announced |
| Needs | Your five needs, with their bonuses and penalties |

| Record | Your Works progress and what the Chief thinks of you |

| Music | Later, once the game has sound |

## How ambient events work: the ion storm, through your eyes

**Day 1, morning.** Nothing official yet. At the start of shift, Calder holds a quick storm briefing at the Works bay job board: her lines play in the panel, and you can offer to take the bridge battery run. At breakfast a Survey officer says the sensors are "doing something odd", and by dinner half the mess is talking about it. A Works notice lands in your Mail: *storm prep, secure loose fittings, top up filters.* New jobs appear on the board. You take some of them, or you don't. That's up to you.

**Day 2, 14:00.** You're fixing a lamp on Deck 4 when the screen jolts, every light dips, and then they start to flicker. The Captain's announcement appears across the top: *"We're in it. Expect flickers and no calls home for a while. Look after each other."* The call-home booths close, the lounge is shut and the mess is quiet. Bridge crew are on double shifts.

**Day 3.** It gets harder. Relays fail, and one deck at a time drops to dim emergency lighting. The chef makes storm stew and sends trays up to the bridge; you can push the cart. People are tired and snappy when you tap them. Each job you do keeps the lights on for someone.

**Day 4, mid-morning.** The Captain gives the all-clear in the observation room, and it goes out to every deck as an announcement. Calls home come back on, and there's a queue at the booths all afternoon. In the evening there's a party in the bow lounge.

**Afterwards.** Nobody shows you a score. If it went **smoothly**, the storm crew's signed duty roster goes up in the Works tea corner and people thank you. If it went **badly**, there's a scorch mark in the relay room, the ship is grumpy for a couple of days, and then it recovers. **You can't fail.**

**Big moments come in four kinds**, and none of them takes the camera away from you:

- **Felt everywhere:** things that happen to the whole ship at once, like the storm's jolt.
- **The Captain's announcements.**
- **Gatherings, announced ahead.**
- **Meetings with dialogue:** a briefing, a summons, a greeting. If you're there, the people's lines play one by one, sometimes with a choice of reply that nudges a friendship. You can always walk away; you'll get the gist in Mail.

## All the events, in order

Pillar 3 builds the ion storm first, then works down the list. Nothing is dropped.

1. **Ion storm**
   - *Comet viewing*
2. **Diplomatic visit:** a greeting at the shuttle bay, formal dinners, Veyli guests in the visitors' cabins
   - *Crew rotation day*
3. **Something in the garden:** a creature eats the crops and ends up as the ship's pet
   - *Power surge*
4. **Shipboard illness**
   - *Founders' Day festival, once your phone test says big crowds are fine*
5. **Nebula drift**
   - *The late supply shuttle*
6. **Salvage:** in later playthroughs it can lead into the garden creature
   - *A Veyli vessel needs help*
7. **The déjà vu day**

The main events are in bold; the shorter ones between them are in italics.

## Getting to know the crew

- Every crew member is a **stranger, acquaintance, colleague, friend or close friend** to you. You get closer by working together, eating together, chatting (once a day each) and doing favours. Friendships don't fade.
- Friends **say hello, come and find you, invite you places**, and put in a good word with the Chief.
- The crew have **friendships and small rivalries with each other**, which change who sits together and what they gossip about.
- **Tomas, Pell and Rina** get the first personal storylines.

## Moving up

- Summoned to the **Captain's ready room** after you do well in an event.
- Taken by the Chief to a **briefing at the conference table**.
- Invited to **tea in an officer's cabin**.
- An invitation to the **Captain's table** for dinner.
- **Progress talks** in the Chief's office.

## The order of work

1. The device (in its old-school look), relationships and chats, and appointments, all built side by side, starting from what your phone test shows.
2. **Check-in 1 with you:** try the new device, pick its screen colour, and try the social life.
3. The ion storm from start to finish, with its two meetings, and the first visit to the ready room.
4. **Check-in 2 with you:** play through a whole storm.
5. Then down the event list, with a review after each main event.

## What you decided

- **The device:** keep the swappable layers, but build the real look now: an old-school screen. Add all the extra apps except Camera.
- **Ambient events:** agreed, and an event can also call a meeting with dialogue, never a full cutscene.
- **The event order:** keep the whole list, in the order above.
- **Everything else:** yes to all ten.
