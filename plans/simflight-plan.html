Land the Tern yourself

Your ask: "Sim flight mini game. Very simple touch and drag controls. Simple visuals. Landing mini game." In a sim pod you can fly the last part of the practice run yourself: bring the Tern down into the Wren's bay with one finger. No scores, just fly. Nothing gets built until you've seen this page.

1. How it starts

  • Tap a sim pod. Once you've sat through one practice run (FX14's turn S3), the pod asks: Watch a practice run or Fly the landing yourself. Before that, it's the watched run only, so the first time you see what a landing looks like.
  • You climb in and the pod's screen fills your view: the game opens over the deck, as Lightdisc does on the court. A Leave button sits in the corner the whole time.
  • The very first go shows three short tips, one at a time, with Next and Skip: "Drag to steer", "Fly through the rings", "Come in slow and level". After that they don't show again.
  • I'll agree the exact hook with FX14 (the pod's choice card, its activity and place names) so we never edit the same lines.

2. Flying it

  • One finger, anywhere on the screen. Drag left or right to turn, up or down to nose up or down. Let go and the Tern levels out by itself. A mouse drag works the same, and the arrow keys or WASD too.
  • Speed looks after itself. The Tern slows by itself as the bay gets closer. Coming in steep makes it faster, and a level approach keeps it slow, so steering is all you do. (Question 2 below offers a slow-down button instead.)
  • Gentle by design. The Tern can't spin or roll over. The rings show the way, and drifting off the line just means steering back.
  • The ship's clock pauses while you fly, as it does for Lightdisc and Tiles. Question 3 below asks if you'd rather it ran.

No grade, no numbers kept, no board: you asked for "No scores, just fly". Each go adds a little Leisure. Leaving partway through is fine and counts for nothing either way.

4. How it's built

  • The Lightdisc pattern. Lightdisc plays live in the 3D view with its own camera, a full-screen layer on top (score, Leave, the tips, the result) and its rules kept apart from the drawing. The flight follows the same pattern: the rules are plain numbers (no drawing, no random calls; a fixed 60 steps a second, so a go plays the same on any phone), and the view only draws them.
  • Its own small scene. Unlike the court, the flight happens out in space, so while you fly the game draws its own tiny scene instead of the deck: stars, the Wren as a few simple shapes with a lit bay, the rings, the Tern. The deck comes back exactly as it was when you leave.
  • Simple visuals. Flat colours, no textures beyond one soft glow for the rings, every colour through the game's usual colour step.
  • Saving. Only how many goes you've flown, so the tips show once and the pod knows to offer the choice. Nothing else is kept.
  • A switch. It sits behind its own switch, off while it's built (the game stays identical), and inside FX14's sim bay switch. A preview link lets you try it before it's switched on.

  • A new check, simflight_check.py. The rules on their own: a steady autopilot lands soft, a fast one lands hard, one that drifts misses; each the same twice. The view at phone size (390 × 844) and wide: a drag turns the Tern, Leave puts the deck, camera and clock back, draw calls stay under 15. Saving: the goes counted and read back.
