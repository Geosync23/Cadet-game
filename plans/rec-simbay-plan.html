The flight simulator bay

Your pick from the room pitch (idea 4): two sit-in sim pods where you fly a short practice run in the Tern, in the rec utility room across the concourse from the pool's bow end. The room's machines move into a narrow booth along the auditorium wall, so they can still break and be repaired. The float room stays as it is.

What you decided (2026-10-24)

  • Layout: build it as planned.
  • Sim night: build it now. Done: every other Thursday, 19:30 to 20:30, first on Day 25.
  • No scores, just fly. No landing grades and no board. A Tern cutaway poster with the bay's rules hangs between the screens instead.
  • The pods are machines that wear and break, now. Done: Works repair them like the rest of the deck's machines.
  • A landing you steer yourself: kept on record as an idea for later. Its prototype is a separate piece, not part of this build.

Everything is built behind one switch, so the game stays exactly as it was until it's switched on after your review. The parts of this page below that talk about grades or a board are kept as first written, for the record.

3. What you do in the sim bay

  1. Tap a pod. "Sim pod: fly a practice run in the Tern." If both pods are taken, you wait on the bench and get in when one frees up, the same way the climbing wall's queue works.
  2. You climb in and sit, hands on the stick. The pod's screen plays the run: the bay doors open, stars stream past, the Wren's hull lights slide by, then her bay grows ahead and you settle in. 15 ship minutes. The ship's clock keeps running (unlike Lightdisc, which pauses it).
  3. Leisure +20 over the run, the same rate as the rest of the deck's activities.
  4. Your landing gets a grade at the end, for example "Touchdown at 1.4 m/s. Smooth: 4 of 5." The grade improves with practice: early runs land mostly 2s and 3s, and after about ten runs you start to see 4s and 5s. It's worked out from how many runs you've done and the day and minute, never from the game's random numbers, so nothing else on the ship shifts.
  5. Dev Raman's board shows the five smoothest landings. Your best goes on it with your name when it's good enough. The crew's entries are fixed numbers for each game.
  6. Saved: how many runs you've done and your best grade. Older saves load with none.
  7. Open 06:00 to midnight, like the rest of the deck.

This is the "sit and watch" version from the pitch (medium size). A real landing you steer yourself with your thumb would be a bigger, separate piece of work: a question below.

4. Who uses it

  • Dev Raman, the senior helm officer who flew you in, gets the new "sim pilot" habit for good: he comes down after shift now and then to keep his hand in.
  • Helm and command crew who have no rec deck habit yet: about half of them become sim pilots.
  • A few others with no rec habit: about 1 in 25 (juniors 1 in 12) get curious and pick it up.
  • That's about 8 people. Nobody's existing rec habit changes (it's a new roll of its own), and a visit only ever takes the place of time they'd have spent resting in their bunk, as with the pool and the wall. Turns of 20-40 minutes, two pods, two waiting spots on the bench. The deck's head count limit (15 in the evenings, 6 on weekday daytimes) still holds.
  • Crew in a pod sit with their hands on the stick, and their screen runs the same flight.
  • Sim night (optional): every other Thursday, 19:30 to 20:30, Dev Raman runs a sim night: two fly, four or five watch by the board, he stands behind the pods calling it, and Mail goes out the evening before. Question below on whether to build it now or later.

6. What moves in the code and the checks

  • A switch. Everything sits behind one switch, off while it's being built (the game stays identical), with a preview link (#simbay added to the game's link) to try it. Switching it on is its own step, after your review.
  • The deck (section 4e): the map gets the new room, the doors and Works hatch, the booth's dressing, the machines' tiles, the new places (two pods, two bench spots, Dev's spot for sim night). The old room's key stays with the booth, so Maeve's place and the talent night code keep working.
  • Activities (section 15r): the pod tap, the wait, the run and its grade, the board, the save.
  • The crew (section 14): the "sim pilot" habit and what a visit does in the pods.
  • Checks: deck_check (everything reachable, the new places, draw calls), day_check (sim pilots' days, nobody stuck, no double-booked pod), player_check (the run's leisure, saving), machine_check (the moved machines still wear, break and get fixed), interactions_check (a new part: tap a pod, sit, leisure rises, the run ends, the grade is saved), check_fast, and cadet_test (Decks 1-6 pixel-identical, and the whole game identical with the switch off).
  • Records: design-doc's rec deck paragraph, the rec deck plan (the utility room's entry, marked changed, not deleted), DEV_NOTES and the build table, in the same commit as the work.
  • FX11 is working on the rec deck at the same time (the pool camera, the light disc). I'll keep in touch with them so we don't edit the same lines.
