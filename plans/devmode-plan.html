Dev mode: the plan

Dev mode planner DP1, 2026-10-20, version 1. Based on the shared branch claude/integration (build 0.64.2 and the step 2 ways of working). Branch claude/devmode-plan. This is planning only: the game itself is unchanged. The measurements were taken on the real game in the test browser (scripts in section 9).
In short
  • Dev mode is a hidden testing panel. You open it by adding #dev to the game's link. Without it, nothing of Dev mode exists in the game: no button, no panel, no change at all. That is proven the usual way: the regression test comes out identical.
  • It never touches your real save. Dev mode keeps its own save, separate from your real game's save, in the same browser.
  • Five things it does, one for each of your asks:
    1. Clock: move time forward by hours or days.
    2. Career: see and set your Works reputation and rank, and start the promotion on demand.
    3. Traditions: start any ship tradition now or later today, and watch the crew go.
    4. Jump to a day: arrive on any day from Day 0 to Day 30, with the career, rank, jobs, friendships and ship state a real player would have by then.
    5. Events: start any ship-wide event (even one that's switched off), jump to one of its phases, see its threads, and end it (the events plan's F6).
  • For the day jump I recommend replaying. The game plays a steady player's days on paper from Day 0 up to the day you pick, then loads you in. It's exact (it's the real game's own code) and it keeps working as the game grows.
    • I measured it on today's game: Day 10 in about 8 seconds, Day 26 in about 19 seconds in the test browser. On a phone-speed stand-in (the processor slowed 4×) it took 40 seconds and 102 seconds, so the first long jump on an older phone is a wait of up to 2 minutes, with a progress bar (section 4).
    • Each day it passes is then kept in Dev mode's save, so jumping back is instant.
    • The same replay twice gives exactly the same game.
  • One big finding. The tests' "steady player" scripts are not yet a whole player. My simple version stayed a Junior Technician all the way to Day 26: it never answered the incident call that the "Making it safe" training needs, so the Tier 2 test never opened. So the steady player is a real piece of work. Each part of the career adds its own line to it, and a new check proves the steady player reaches Officer on Day 10, as the Pillar 5 plan says.
  • Size: about 8-10 build turns, in 3 stops for your review. There are 10 questions for you in section 7.

2. Opening it, and keeping your real game safe

  • Opening: add #dev to the end of the game's link (the same way #debug already works; the game reads switches from the link). A small gold DEV chip then sits under the clock, and tapping it opens the panel. Without #dev there's no chip, no panel and no Dev code running. It also doesn't show up in the device's apps, the Mail, the tips or any text.
  • Its own save: your game's save lives in the browser under one name. Dev mode saves under a different name, with "-dev" added, and does the same for the "you've already arrived" marker. So:
    • You can play your real game, open the #dev link, test anything, and go back to your real game exactly as you left it.
    • Dev mode doesn't use the tests' "no saving" switch (nosave). That switch also turns off the arrival and the clothes, so testing with it wouldn't be testing the real game.
  • Always labelled: the DEV chip stays on screen while you're in Dev mode. A game that a jump or a setting has changed carries a mark in its own save (for example "set by Dev mode: xp", or "replayed to Day 14"), so a bug found later can be traced back to it.
  • It's yours to open, not hidden from you: anyone who knows to type #dev can open it. The game is yours and the link is private by default, so I think that's enough (question 1).

5. Events: phases, threads, ending.

3.1 Clock

  • What it does: the buttons +1 hour, +3 hours, to 17:00 (the shift's end), to 21:40 (the evening), next morning (06:00), +1 day, +7 days, and "to a time…".
  • Two ways of passing the time:
    • Skip (like sleeping). The game's own time jump, the one sleeping and the debug Night/Day button already use. Everything on the ship catches up: the crew's days, the machines, the galley, the Mail, the events, friendships, your needs. You weren't there, so a workday skipped is a missed shift, exactly as if you'd slept through it.
    • Play it steady (recommended for days). The steady player (section 4) lives those hours for you on paper: clocks in, takes jobs, goes to trainings, has a meal and a chat, sleeps. You come back with the day done, as a careful player would have done it.
  • Under the hood:
    • Skip calls skipTo, the same as sleeping, so nothing new is needed in the ship's systems.
    • Long skips go in steps of 30 ship minutes, with a pause between days so the phone stays responsive and a progress bar shows. One single jump over many days would skip the hourly things in between (the events plan found that a single jump over the whole storm came out "smooth").
    • Before any jump, Dev mode settles what's mid-flight: a conversation on screen ends (its gist arrives in Mail, as when you walk away), an activity stops (as sleeping does), and a Lightdisc or Tiles game is closed. It refuses during a lift ride or while you're asleep.
  • Measured: one plain 10-day skip from Day 1 takes 3 seconds in the test browser. Played steady, a day takes about 0.8 seconds.

3.2 Career

  • Shows: your rank and tier, Works reputation (the band word and the number behind it), the trainings done and the next one, your tools, missed shifts, and the ship-wide reputation (work, kindness, spirit).
  • Set:
    • xp up or down, or straight to the next band.
    • Train the next row, or open any row.
    • Give or take a tool.
    • Set the department and ship reputation.
    Each change goes through the career's own functions where they exist, so the band badges, Mail and Record update as in play. Anything set directly is marked "set by Dev mode" in the save.
  • Test the promotion: "Start promotion now" sets your test job for today (the real Tier 2 test, Calder's word, the job), and "Next step ›" moves the clock to the next beat: the summons, the move to your cabin, the ceremony. So you see the real thing, quickly, not a copy of it.
  • Setting the rank directly ("Make me an Officer") is offered too, but marked approximate, because a real promotion also gives you a cabin, a pip, the ceremony, friends' Mail and new rows. For a believable officer, use Jump to Day 11 instead.
  • Later ranks (Senior Officer, Engine Specialist) come with Pillar 5 Steps 5 and 6. Dev mode lists the ranks from the career's own table, so they appear here with no Dev mode change.
  • Under the hood: the career is kept in SHIP.works (rank, tier, xp, trained rows, tools), SHIP.career (clocking in, missed shifts, leave) and SHIP.rep. "Start promotion now" uses section 24p's own test-day setting, through a small hook its owner reviews.

3.3 Traditions

  • Shows: today's and this week's traditions with times, and a search over all 60 (birthdays, rotation teas and the rec deck's included).
  • Start one: in 20 minutes, at its usual time today, or tomorrow, optionally with its Mail the evening before ("+ Mail" sends it now). Then it shows who's going and leads you there with the goal pointer.
  • Under the hood:
    • Traditions are worked out per day by section 18 (eventsOn), and the crew's plans for the day are made from that.
    • Dev mode adds a short list to its save ("tradition X on day D at time T"), and a hook in eventsOn reads it. The list is empty when Dev mode is off, so the game is identical.
    • Then the day's plans are made again, the same way a one-off story event already does it (eventOneOff). The plans read only that list, which is fixed for the day once set, so the "plans never read records built while playing" rule still holds: the day comes out the same however often it's planned.
  • What can't simply start anywhere, and how it's handled:
    • Birthdays and rotation teas need someone to honour. Dev mode asks whose (default: the first crew member aboard).
    • Traditions waiting for a story beat (bridge hour, the rec deck's film night and all-hands) are listed with their flag, and starting one sets it in the Dev save only.
    • Rec deck traditions need the rec deck switched on (it is in 0.64.2).
    • Ones that only happen at a fixed time (dawn swim, sunrise stretch) start at that time tomorrow if it has passed.
    • A tradition with a big event running (no quiz in the storm) is refused, with the reason.
  • The one visible oddity: starting a tradition today means today's plans change, so crew on your deck can be somewhere new at once. The deck refreshes with the lift's fade, so nobody visibly jumps (question 7).

3.4 Jump to a day

Pick a day from 0 to 30 (shortcuts for the career's milestones: 0 arrival, 1 first shift, 5 the scanner, 9 the test, 10 Officer, 16 Senior, 23 Engine Specialist, 26 Calder's question, 30 the visit's afterglow) and a player (steady by default). Dev mode starts a new game in its own save, replays the days and loads you in at 06:00 on that day, waking in your own bed. Details and the alternatives are in section 4.

3.5 Events (the events plan's F6)

  • Lists every ship-wide event: the ion storm (on), the diplomatic visit (built, switched off) and, once drafted, the comet. Each shows its phases and threads.
  • Start now: the warning starts at the next quarter hour.
  • Start at a phase: the event is placed on the calendar so that phase begins at the next time it can, and the clock moves there. The earlier phases pass as if you'd slept through them: their addresses and Mail arrive quietly. This reuses the event's own catch-up code.
  • Threads live:
    • Each thread shows its number and band (smooth, bumpy, rough), with − and + buttons.
    • Also shown: the overall band, "your part" (jobs and meetings, which decide the commendation) and today's effects (blackouts, comms down).
  • End now: brings the event to its end through its own ending. The result is scored, its traces are laid, it's logged, the debrief Mail goes out and a commendation comes if earned. So the threads' after-effects ("see its threads") show up as in play.
  • A switched-off event (the visit) can be started in Dev mode only. Starting it never switches it on in the real game.
  • Under the hood:
    • It uses eventStart(id, day), eventAdvance, eventScore, eventBand and the event log.
    • After any change, the plans from today on are made again, as above.
    • Its phase jumps rely on the events plan's F4 ("safe through skips and reloads"). Until F4 is in, the panel says "phases may drop on a jump" next to the phase buttons.

4. Jumping to a day: the design

4.1 What "believable" means

Arriving on Day 14 should feel like Day 14 for someone who played well:

  • Officer since Day 10, with the cabin and the pip.
  • The trainings done, the tools in hand and a work log.
  • Friends among the crew, with stories part-way along.
  • The ship's machines worn as they would be, jobs on the board, the ion storm past (and how it went), Mail in the inbox.
  • The traditions you'd have been to, and the crew's habits drifted as they do over time.

Almost all of that lives in the save, built up hour by hour by many parts of the game.

Recommended: (a), replay, plus keeping the days it passes.
  • As the replay passes each day, it keeps that day's save in Dev mode's own storage, stamped with the build number. That's a few chosen days (0, 5, 10, 15, 20, 25 and the last one asked for, about 0.8 MB), so it stays well inside a browser's storage.
  • Jumping to a kept day, or to a later one, starts from the nearest kept day: instant, or only the few days in between.
  • A new build throws the kept days away, so they're never stale. This is (b)'s speed without its upkeep, and the game file doesn't grow.
  • At the end, you're loaded in through the game's normal "open a save" path, with a page reload. What you see is exactly what reopening that save gives.

The finding that sizes the work: today's player scripts aren't a whole player
  • My quick steady player clocked in, took the board's top job each half hour of the shift, went to every training meeting, watched and did the training jobs, and had a chat and a meal with five crew a day. It reached "Relied on" by Day 5, but it never trained "Making it safe" (m6). That training starts with an incident call you have to answer. So the Tier 2 test never opened, and it was still a Junior Technician on Day 26.
  • Its xp on Day 26 also changed with the step size: 170, 234 and 292 for 60, 30 and 10 minutes. So the step size has to be fixed as part of the script.
  • The checks each have a partial player, built for their own part of the game:
    • career_check's steady, busy and lazy players use stand-in trainings.
    • arc_check's "diligent player" does the friendships and stories.
    • events_check goes to the traditions.
    None of them is the whole game.
  • So Dev mode needs one shared steady player (DEV_PLAYER): a small table with a line per part of the game saying what a steady player does. For example: answer the incident call, keep training appointments, do the round, go to Friday tea, play a ready story moment with the first reply, call home when there's news.
    • Each part's owner adds its line, the same way they register a ladder row today.
    • A new check, devmode_check.py, proves the milestones: Officer on Day 10 today, and later ones as Steps 5 and 6 build them.
    • The checks can use the same player later, which is the "saved starting points" idea from the check-speed talk (roadmap), done safely.

4.4 Day 0, the end, and the visit

  • Day 0 is the arrival evening: Jump to Day 0 simply starts a new game by shuttle, in the Dev save.
  • Jumps past Day 0 replay the arrival evening on paper (the steady player goes through the arrival's steps) rather than "Skip to Monday", so friendships from the arrival dinner exist (question 6).
  • Day 26 (Calder's question) and the career's later ranks only exist once Pillar 5 Steps 5 and 6 are built. Until then a jump to Day 26 arrives as an Officer on Day 26 of today's game. The panel says so.
  • The visit finale (Days 26-30) needs the events plan's F0 calendar and the visit switched on. Until then, Dev mode can still show it: start the visit (off) from the Events tab after jumping to Day 26.

7. Questions for you

Pick an option and add a comment if you like. Each question has an id for saving. My recommendation is marked.

1. How should Dev mode be opened?

It has to stay out of the normal game. The game already reads switches like #debug from the link.

Question 1

2. How should Dev mode save?

Either way your real save is never touched. With no saving at all, the arrival and the clothes are switched off too (the tests' switch does that), and a reload loses your test.

Question 2

3. How should "jump to a day" work?

Section 4. Replay is exact and keeps up with the game by itself. Shipping snapshots is instant but must be rebuilt every build and makes the game file 70% bigger. Made-up state is instant but only approximate.

Question 3

4. Which kinds of player can you jump as?

The checks already have steady, busy and lazy players. Each kind beyond steady needs its own lines, tested: about one more turn each.

Question 4

5. When the clock moves forward over a workday, what happens to your shift?

In the game, sleeping through a shift is a missed shift, and enough of them get you let go (Day 20 for a player who skips work).

Question 5

6. Does a jump past Day 0 include the arrival evening?

A real new game offers "Arrive by shuttle" or "Skip to Monday". The arrival brings the welcome dinner and its first friendships.

Question 6

7. Starting a tradition today changes today's plans. How should that look?

The crew's days are planned all at once. Starting something today means making them again, so people on your deck can be in new places at once.

Question 7

8. Can Dev mode set your rank directly?

A real promotion brings a cabin, a pip, a ceremony, friends' Mail and new work. Setting the rank by hand skips those and isn't what a player would have.

Question 8

9. How often should you review?

Your rhythm is one step, then review. Dev mode is about 8-10 turns.

Question 9

10. Should Dev mode stay in the published game?

It's hidden without #dev. Keeping it means you can test the real published game any time. Taking it out means a separate build to test with.

Facts found in the code (0.64.2 + step 2)
  • The switches are read from both ?query and #hash. On claude.ai only the hash reaches the page (tokens joined by ".", so #dev.debug works).
  • Only two storage names: starship-cadet-save and starship-cadet-arrived.
  • NOSAVE also switches off the arrival (introWanted) and the clothes (CLOTHES_ON).
  • skipTo(abs) runs the machines first, then 14 catch-up steps in SIM_ADVANCE: plans, needs, galley, Mail, traces, the ship-wide event, meetings, the bridge, the career, social, the Works ladder, and the drawn crew.
  • Clocking in takes careerClock(). After the first week, being in the Works bay doesn't count without clocking in.
  • window.__sc is the checks' own hook, added to a test copy (cadet_test.instrument). It isn't in the game, so Dev mode calls the game's functions directly.
  • There are 60 traditions in SHIP_TRADITIONS (the rec deck's included): 1 birthday kind, 3 rotation kinds, 4 counted from your arrival and 3 waiting for a flag. There are 2 ship-wide events: ionStorm (on) and diplomatic (draft). The next one due is the storm, on Day 10.
  • eventStart doesn't check for drafts, so Dev mode can start the visit without it being switched on.
  • The career ladder currently ends at Tier 2 (Officer). WORK_RANKS lists 4 ranks.

9. How this was measured

The scripts aren't in the repo (planning only). They're described here so a reviewer can redo them:

  • replay_measure.py GAME DAYS: loads the game in cadet_test's Game (?nosave&debug, tips off). From Day 1 06:00 to 22:00, every 30 minutes (STEP sets the step), it calls skipTo, then worksTick, eventsTick, socialTick and worksCareerTick. As the player it:
    • clocks in at 08:00 and out at 17:00;

    • does the training job, else the board's top job, during the shift (claimJob, jobStepDone, turnInJob, a case called right);
    • chats and has a meal with five crew at 12:00;
    • sleeps to 06:00.
    It prints each day's rank, xp, band and trainings, the event log, the real save's size (made as saveGame makes it) and a fingerprint of the save. THROTTLE=4 slows the CPU four times through the browser's DevTools protocol.
