Dev mode: jump to anything in the game

A plan only. Nothing in the game or the checks changes until the controller round has passed and you say go. Your answers go at the bottom of the page.

In short. Dev mode today can move the clock, take you to any room, start any tradition, and set your career to any day up to Day 30 with eight story shortcuts. It can't reach the ship-wide events, most of Steps 5 and 6, the Veyli vessel or the ending, and its career arrow goes quiet after the Deck 2 move.

The plan adds one tab, Jump to (it replaces the empty "Big events" tab), with about 40 jumps in four groups: Career, Nights, Ship events and The ending. Each jump starts a fresh Dev game and runs the ship forward to that moment, the same way Set career does now. So the event or beat is the game's own, on its own day, never a fake. Set career gains one button per rank, the Traditions tab gains "Go to its next showing", and the career arrow follows you to the end of the game.

About 20 hours of building in 6 turns: one short groundwork turn, then three builders side by side, then the arrow and the write-up. Your real game is never touched, as now.

2. How a jump works

Every jump is the existing Set career with a landing spot. That one choice is what keeps it safe:

  1. A fresh Dev game, started the Skip-to-Monday way, as Set career does now. Two taps ("Tap again: a new Dev game"), and Undo brings back the game you had.
  2. The ship runs forward day by day to the jump's day, with the career set each morning from the table, workdays excused, needs kept. Every ship-wide event in that time happens on its own calendar day by the game's own rules, so when you land in the storm it's the real storm with its real Mail, crew plans and broken machines.
  3. Your part on the way is filled in through the game's own functions, at the moment you'd have done it: Calder's question answered with cqAnswer, the offer heard with endOfferDone, your answer given with endSay. Dev mode never writes those records by hand.
  4. The clock stops a few minutes before the moment, and you're placed in the room. Then you play it.

Safety rules, from DEV_NOTES "Rules that keep it working":

  • Your real save: never written. All of this lives in the Dev save (keys ending in "-dev"), with the space check before every write.
  • No R(): nothing in section 25 calls it. A jump only uses the game's seeded parts, so two jumps to the same moment give the same save, byte for byte (the check proves it for one jump in each group).
  • The crew's planner only reads records it's allowed to (the settled ones the game itself makes: Basil's day, Calder's question). Where a jump settles one, that day's plans are made again, as the mentoring shortcut already does.
  • No events off their calendar day. Events are tied to their days (the nights, the storm's Saturday, the comet's sky, the career guard), so starting one on another day is where things would break. A running event can be moved to its next phase, or ended early through eventFinish.
  • Without the Dev link nothing of this exists, as now. The checks prove it.
  • Section 26 (the controller, now adding a Dev card at the screen's edge) is not touched. The new rows are ordinary Dev panel buttons, so the controller reaches them the way it reaches the panel today.

6. The checks

  • devmode_check jumps (J0): every registered jump has a known group, a day within Set career's range, a landing room that exists, and an id used once; jumps behind a switch only appear with it on.
  • beats (J1), events (J2), ending (J3): every jump in its group is run: the clock, deck and room it lands at, the record it needs (the test set, the night's record, SHIP.event and its phase, SHIP.end), no miss, no notes Mail, no errors; one jump per group twice, byte-identical; Undo after one. Events also: next phase, end it now (one debrief, the log written). Ending: each answer (Stay, Go, Unsure) and each of Calder's four answers reaches the right post and page.
  • trad (J3): Go to its next showing lands on the real day with the cake or invitation in place.
  • dev_arrow_check s6, ev, end (J4): at each landing, the arrow names the right step (or the event's meeting) and points at the right deck and room; still none without #dev.
  • Timing: a jump is a whole new game run forward (Set career to Day 26 is in today's check). Each group is its own part so no part runs much over 5 minutes, and the full round spreads them over the six runners. J0 measures the real cost first.

7. Risks

  • Long jumps on your phone. A jump to Day 36 runs 36 ship days. If J0 finds it takes more than about 30 seconds on a phone, the fallback is to let a jump start from your Mark when the Mark is earlier the same run (question 9).
  • A piece's own record vs the table. Set career sets the career by a table, approximately. A later piece may read something the table doesn't set (a night's record, a side job). Each jump's check catches this; the fix goes in the table, not in the piece.
  • Your part in events is empty. Landing in the storm, the ship scored it without you so far. The debrief and commendation follow from what you then do. Approximate, and said so on the panel.
  • Traditions tied to an event can only be reached through the event's jump, not the Traditions tab. The tab will say so on their cards.
  • Section 26 is moving (the controller's Dev card). Nothing here touches it; the controller seat checks its card still opens the sheet after J0.
  • Merge clashes: the blocks and stub parts from J0 keep the three builders apart; the two build-number lines stay the build master's.
