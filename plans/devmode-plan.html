Dev mode: the plan

Dev mode planner DP1, 2026-10-20, version 2.1 (v2 plus both reviewers' re-checks: DR1 fc40cfb, DR2 785d88d), after two reviews and a helper's measurements:
  • DR1, technical: claude/devmode-review-1, plans/devmode-plan-review-1.md
  • DR2, use on a phone: claude/devmode-review-2, plans/devmode-plan-review-2.md and its addendum
  • DJ1, day-jump measurements: claude/devmode-dayjump
  • lead 20's call on the order (the playtest kit first)
It's based on claude/integration (0.64.2 and the step 2 ways of working), on branch claude/devmode-plan. Planning only: the game is unchanged. What changed since version 1 is in section 10; version 2.1's changes are at its end.
In short
  • Dev mode is a hidden testing panel for your own game.
    • The lead gives you a ready Dev link: the game's link with #dev on the end. Without it, Dev mode doesn't exist in the game, and that's proven every turn.
    • In Dev mode the place-and-clock card at the top left gets a gold outline and the word DEV, so you can always tell (in screenshots too). Tapping the card opens the panel.
  • It never touches your real game.
    • It has its own save.
    • Its bigger data is stored apart, so it can't fill the space your real game saves into.
    • "Clear Dev save" removes everything it stored.
    • A check proves your real save still writes.
  • Three stops, your next job first:
    • Stop 1, the playtest kit, in two parts. Stop 1a, the patch kit (published as soon as it passes, after four turns): move the clock, top up your needs, Take me to…, Mark this moment / Back to mark, Undo my last Dev action and copy your real game into Dev. Stop 1b adds starting any ship tradition. Altogether, for the playtest patch: move the clock, top up your needs, Take me to… any room or your next appointment, Mark this moment / Back to mark (retry the same minute), Undo, start any ship tradition, and copy your real game into Dev.
    • Stop 2, the day jump and the Career tab: arrive on any day from 0 to 30 with the career, rank, jobs, friendships and ship a steady player would have.
    • Stop 3, ship-wide events: start any, even one switched off; see its threads; end it, with "End and undo" so later events don't move.
  • The day jump replays a steady player from Day 0 with the game's own code, then loads you in.
    • It's exact, and repeatable once the game's one random number is fixed (Dev mode does that).
    • On a computer, Day 26 takes about 16 seconds in my run and about 43 seconds with the more careful player the trainings need (DJ1, to be confirmed).
    • On a phone the first long jump may take minutes. Kept days make later ones quick.
  • The big piece of work is the steady player.
    • It needs a line for each of the 17 career trainings and for the arrival week, written with each part's owner.
    • Some must be done in person: the first week's meetings only start with you in the room with the speaker.
  • About 13-17 build turns. There are 16 questions in section 8; 11-16 are new.

2. Opening it, and keeping your real game safe

  • The Dev link.
    • At each stop the lead gives you the playable link with #dev on the end, to tap or bookmark. In the Claude app there's no address bar to type into, and editing a long link on a phone is fiddly.
    • Stop 1 checks the link works in the Claude app, in a phone browser and through "Open in browser", and survives a reload. If it doesn't, the fallback is a separate Dev copy of the game (question 1).
    • Changing a link's ending in an open tab doesn't reload the page, so Dev mode notices and offers a reload.
  • The way in: the place-and-clock card (room, deck and clock, top left).
    • The phone's buttons leave no room for another one.
    • Today the card ignores taps, so a tap there walks you to that spot.
    • In Dev mode the whole card (about 60 points tall) becomes the button, with a gold outline and the word DEV in letters, not colour alone.
    • The panel's title says "Dev mode: not your real game".
  • Its own save.
    • The two names the game saves under get "-dev" added.
    • It doesn't use the tests' "no saving" switch, because that also switches off the arrival and the clothes.
    • With #dev, even the debug buttons (#dev.debug) write only the Dev save. A plain #debug still writes your real save today; that's a row for the small-bugs list.
  • Storage that can't crowd out your real save. Each site gets about 5 MB for saves, and when it's full the game stops saving without a word.
    • Dev mode keeps only three small things there: its save, one Mark and one Undo (about 120 KB each).
    • The kept days of the day jump go in the browser's separate, larger store (IndexedDB).
    • Dev mode checks the space before every write and never removes anything of your real game's.
    • devmode_check fills Dev storage to its limit, then proves the real save still writes.
  • Copy my real game into Dev. Found a bug while playing? This reads your real save (it never writes it) and makes it the Dev save, so you can test from that moment.
  • One fixed random number.
    • The game has exactly one random draw: a seed made when your career starts, deciding sick days and who comes looking for you. With saving on it differs every game.
    • DR1 replayed to Day 14 twice that way: one run was sick on Day 11 and ended with 92 xp; the other wasn't, and ended with 104.
    • Dev mode fixes the seed before the career starts: 1 by default, shown on the Jump tab, changeable for a different run of luck.
    • A copied real game has its random seed replaced by the Dev seed, so jumps and replays from a copy are repeatable too. (From that moment its luck, such as sick days, can differ from your real game's.)
    • devmode_check replays twice with saving on and requires identical saves.
  • Marked. Anything set by hand or replayed is noted in the Dev save ("xp set", "replayed to Day 14, seed 1"), so a bug found later can be traced.

6. Big events (Stop 3).

3.1 What every Dev action does first

  • Keeps an Undo. The Dev save is copied to the Undo slot first. "Undo my last Dev action" puts it back and reloads, so even a skip that got you let go can be undone.
  • Settles what's in progress.
    • A conversation on screen ends, and its gist goes to Mail, as when you walk away.
    • An activity stops, as sleeping does.
    • A game of Tiles or Lightdisc closes.
    • A claimed job stays claimed.
    • Nothing happens during a lift ride or sleep; the button says why.
  • Freezes the game while it works.
    • The ship's clock, the crew and the moment-to-moment bits (chats, the tour, Mail notices) normally move on every frame the phone draws.
    • During a Dev action the game is frozen the way Pause freezes it, so the result never depends on the phone's speed (DR1).
    • Pop-up messages are kept quiet rather than switched off, since switching tips off would itself change the save.
    • The crew on your deck are placed once, at the end.

3.2 Clock (Stop 1)

  • Buttons:
    • next morning (06:00), +1 hour, to a time…;
    • to 08:55 and to 16:55 (five minutes before each shift edge, for testing clocking in and out);
    • to 21:40, +3 hours, +1 day, +7 days.
  • Under the hood:
    • It uses the game's own time jump (skipTo, which sleeping uses), frozen, in 30-minute steps a day at a time, with a progress bar.
    • It doesn't use one big jump, because that skips the hourly things in between. DR1 found a single jump from Day 12 to 20 never got a player let go when steps did.
  • Your shift (question 5):
    • Excused (the default for quick hops). A skipped workday counts as leave. You're testing something else. Under the hood: a Dev-only "excused" list in the Dev save, read by careerOff, set before the day is planned. For today, part-way through a shift, one line in careerTick's missed-shift loop skips an excused day. Both are the career engineer's (section 20), reviewed first.
    • Counts as missed: the game's rule, for testing the career warnings. A skip-only player is let go on Day 20 (DR1); Undo brings them back.
    • Skipping while clocked in keeps you clocked in, and the shift counts as worked.
    • "Play it steady" (the steady player lives the hours) comes with Stop 2, and is then the default for whole days.
  • Needs (question 15): after a skip they're set as a normal day would leave them, not drained to 0. DR2 measured hunger, hygiene and bathroom at 0 after skipping to Day 2, and the warnings then take over the screen. Top up needs fills them any time. Under the hood: a Dev-only hook in section 15's skip step (its SIM_ADVANCE entry that calls needsDrain), the player engineer's, reviewed first.
  • Measured:
    • A plain skip takes 0.2 s for a day and 1.1 s for ten on a computer (DR2).
    • A plain skip from Day 1 to 27 leaves 15 missed shifts, the Captain's second warning and 0 xp (DR1). That's right for "counts as missed", and why "excused" is the default.

3.3 Quick (Stop 1): the playtest kit

  • Take me to…
    • "Next appointment": the next item in your Schedule.
    • "A room…": by deck.
    • Testing spots: the ladder, the time clock, the job board.
    It moves you the way the lift does (a fade, the deck built, you on the nearest free tile), and the clock doesn't move. A walk across two decks, 1-2 real minutes on a phone, becomes one tap.
    • Refused, with the reason on the button, during the arrival evening (Zeke's escort checks where you stand) and inside a meeting. Otherwise it first settles what's in progress (a conversation, a seated meal), like every Dev action. devmode_check tries it during the escort's walk and expects a refusal.
  • Mark here / Back to mark.
    • One saved moment: the whole Dev save, where you stand and the time.
    • "Back to mark" loads it, with a reload, through the game's normal load path, so it's exact.
    • Try the Day 2 08:40 meeting six times, each from the same minute.
    • It's the step repeated most, so Stop 1a checks on a phone that the reload keeps #dev and lands within a couple of seconds.
  • Top up needs, Undo my last Dev action (named in full so it isn't confused with the playtest patch's own undo for clocking in; it's only inside the panel), Copy my real game into Dev, Clear Dev save: see section 2.
  • The Day 2 meeting at Stop 1:
    1. Play Day 0 and Day 1 once in a new Dev game, as a player would (about 25 real minutes for Day 1), or Copy my real game if it's past Day 1.
    2. On Day 2, to 08:30.
    3. Take me to: next appointment.
    4. Mark here.
    5. Watch.
    6. Back to mark, as often as needed.
    Then every retry is one tap. Skipping Day 1 instead (Next morning, twice) gives a player who missed the Day 1 briefing: the first week's meetings need you in the room, and the briefing's Settling-in item and Zeke's Day 1 line read whether you were there. So when a skip passes a first-week meeting, the panel says so in words: "Skipped: the Day 1 briefing counted as missed. This isn't a real player's Day 2." That way a test result isn't trusted by mistake. Stop 2's jump makes a real Day 2 automatic.

3.5 Career (Stop 2)

  • Shows trainings by name, with the code small for bug reports ("Making it safe (m6)"): rank and tier, Works reputation (band and xp), tools, missed shifts, ship reputation.
  • Set: xp up or down, the next band, the next training, tools, department and ship reputation. Each is marked "set by Dev mode".
  • The promotion test (question 8). It opens only when the ladder opens it. That needs:
    • Day 9 or later;
    • "Relied on";
    • Making it safe trained;
    • no training under way;
    • Calder or Vance on shift;
    • before 14:00.
    So "Promotion test…" shows that list with ticks.
    • If only the day is missing, it offers "Open the test now". That calls the ladder's own ladderOpen, agreed with section 24's owner first.
    • Otherwise it says what's missing and offers "Jump to Day 9".
    • Once the test is open, "Next step ›" moves the clock to each beat: the job, the summons, the move to your cabin, the ceremony.
  • Set rank directly: offered, marked "approximate", because a real promotion also brings a cabin, a pip, a ceremony and Mail. It's the fallback while the steady player can't yet reach a rank.
  • Later ranks come from the career's own tables, so they appear when Pillar 5 Steps 5 and 6 build them.

3.6 Jump (Stop 2)

  • Shortcuts are the story's beats: Day 1 briefing, Day 2 solo job, Day 3 store, Day 9 test, Day 10 Officer, Day 16, Day 26, Day 30, and "Day…". Each lands you at that time and place.
  • Pick the player (steady; busy and lazy later) and the seed (1).
  • The design is in section 4.

3.7 Big events (Stop 3; the events plan's F6)

  • Lists every ship-wide event: the ion storm (on), the diplomatic visit (built, switched off) and the comet once drafted, each with its phases and threads named in words ("Calls home owed").
  • Start:
    • Phases are fixed hours (the storm's warning is 06:00 of its first day), and eventStart takes a day, not a minute.
    • So "Start" sets it for today if its first phase is still ahead, else tomorrow, and offers to move the clock there.
    • Starting at any minute would need a change to section 18, its owner's call (with the events plan).
  • Next phase moves the clock to the next phase's hour; earlier phases pass as if slept through. Jumps across several phases are labelled unreliable until the events plan's F4 ("safe through skips and reloads").
  • Threads show their numbers and bands, with − and +, plus the overall band, your part and today's effects.
  • End and undo / End and keep.
    • Starting keeps a copy of all event state: the running event, the log, traces, commendations, the event's Mail and the plans' cache.
    • End and undo (the default) puts it all back, so later events' dates don't move.
    • End and keep runs the event's real ending (score, traces, log, debrief Mail, commendation). That ending sits inside eventAdvance today, so it's split into its own function first: a change to section 18's shared code, its owner first, best done in the events plan's turns.
  • With the events plan: this tab is F6. Stop 3 builds start, threads and End and undo. Phase jumps and End and keep are finished with F4 and the ending split, so the events plan's F6 turn shrinks to those. To be settled with the events planner.

4. Jumping to a day: the design

4.1 The three options

(a) Replay a steady player(b) Snapshots made by the checks(c) Made-up state
HowA new Dev game; a script plays a steady player's days with the game's own code; then you're loaded inA check replays each day once per change; the game carries the savesFill in career, rank, friendships and ship by rules
Exact?Yes, and the same every time once the seed is fixed (measured)Only for the code it was made withNo
SpeedSeconds to a minute on a computer; minutes the first time on a phone; then kept daysInstantInstant
UpkeepThe steady player (needed anyway)Rebuilt on every change; ~120 KB a day, so +70% on the 5.1 MB gameRules kept by hand forever
Recommended: (a), with kept days.
  • A fresh page. DJ1 found that a replay after "New game" in a page that's been played doesn't give the same Day 10 (272 differences): New game doesn't reset everything in memory. So "Jump" writes a request ("day N, seed S, steady") into the tab's short-term storage and reloads, and the fresh page replays before anything moves.
  • Frozen (3.1), in half-day pieces with a progress bar and a Stop that answers. The screen draws between pieces but the game doesn't move.
  • Kept days.
    • The replay keeps a few days' saves in IndexedDB as it passes them: 0, 5, 10, 15, 20, 25 and the last one asked for (question 12).
    • The next jump starts from the nearest kept day before its target.
    • Each kept day is stamped with a fingerprint of the game's script (worked out in Dev mode only, a few hundredths of a second), the steady player's version and the seed.
    • Helpers no longer change the build number, so the build number can't be the stamp (DR1's blocker). Change one character of the game and the kept days are dropped.
  • Loading in goes through the game's normal "open a save" path, at the beat's time and place (06:00 in your bed for a plain day).

4.3 The steady player (DEV_PLAYER)

  • A table with one line per part of the game. The lines cover:
    • the arrival week, in person;
    • clocking in and out, meals and a wash, the board's top job;
    • each training (the meeting, watching, your own job), the drill and the inspection;
    • the incident call (24f's valve and mop), the round's marks, the test job, the promotion's beats;
    • two chats a day, a ready story moment answered with the first reply, and traditions.
  • Starting points: mech_check's PARTS (each mechanic's steps on paper, though they set some state by hand), intro_check's happy player, arc_check's diligent player, and DJ1's script.
  • Owners: each part's owner writes or reviews its line. This is the plan's biggest schedule risk: if a seat is busy, Stop 2 waits. The fallback is to jump with that rank set "approximate".
  • Checked:
    • devmode_check lists any career row in the build that has no line.
    • The milestone "Officer on Day 10, as in the Pillar 5 plan" starts as "not yet" (as mech_check does for rows not yet built), so the quick check never goes red while the lines are written.

8. Questions for you

Pick an option and add a comment if you like. Each question has an id for saving. My recommendation is marked. Questions 11-15 are new in version 2, and 16 in version 2.1.

1. How should Dev mode be opened?

It has to stay out of the normal game. Typing on the end of a link is hard on a phone, so the lead gives you the link.

Question 1

2. How should Dev mode save?

Either way your real save is never touched. With no saving, the arrival and the clothes are off too, and a reload loses your test.

Question 2

3. How should "jump to a day" work?

Section 4. Replay is exact and keeps up with the game by itself. Shipped snapshots go stale with every change and make the game 70% bigger. Made-up state is only approximate.

Question 3

4. Which kinds of player can you jump as?

The steady player alone is 4-6 turns, since every part of the career adds its line. Busy and lazy players would each add about a turn.

Question 4

5. When Dev mode moves the clock over a workday, what happens to your shift?

In the game, a shift you sleep through is missed, and a player who keeps missing is let go on Day 20. Usually you're testing something else.

Question 5

6. Does a day jump start with the arrival evening?

Skipping the arrival gives the same first friendships either way (intro_check checks it). Arriving costs a little time but tests the evening itself, and anything that depends on it.

Question 6

7. Starting a tradition today changes today's plans. How should that look?

Measured: 20 of the 74 crew are elsewhere at once, and 57 have a different evening. Nothing already past changes. A cake or invitation decided earlier won't appear.

Question 7

8. How far may Dev mode push the career by hand?

The promotion test needs Day 9, "Relied on", Making it safe, a teacher on shift and the morning. A real promotion also brings a cabin, a pip, a ceremony and Mail.

Question 8

9. Is this order right?

Your next job after Dev mode is the playtest patch, so the lead put the playtest kit first.

Question 9

10. Should Dev mode stay in the published game?

It does nothing without the Dev link. Keeping it means you can test the real published game any time.

Question 10

11. Should every Dev action be undoable?

A skip can end your Dev career for good (let go on Day 20). Undo keeps one copy of the Dev save from before the last action, about 120 KB.

Question 11

12. How many days should a day jump keep?

Each kept day is a save of about 60-125 KB, stored apart from your real save, so this is about phone space and speed, not safety.

Question 12

13. Is a first long jump of a few minutes on a phone acceptable?

On a computer Day 26 takes 16-43 s. On a phone, DJ1 estimates 3-5 minutes to Day 30 the first time, with a progress bar and a Stop button. After that, kept days make it quick.

Question 13

14. Do you want "Take me to…" and "Mark / Back to mark" in the first stop?

They make each retry a few taps instead of a walk across two decks (1-2 real minutes on a phone). One turn for both.

Question 14

15. When you skip time in Dev mode, should your needs be looked after?

After a plain skip, hunger, hygiene and bathroom can reach 0, and their warnings take over the screen.

Question 15

16. Should the patch kit be published on its own first?

The patch needs only the first four turns. Traditions (two more turns, and the riskiest part of Stop 1, since today's plans are remade) aren't needed for any patch item.
