2. How a jump works

Every jump is the existing Set career with a landing spot. That one choice is what keeps it safe:

  1. A fresh Dev game, started the Skip-to-Monday way, as Set career does now. Two taps ("Tap again: a new Dev game"), and Undo brings back the game you had.
  2. The ship runs forward day by day to the jump's day, with the career set each morning from the table, workdays excused, needs kept. Every ship-wide event in that time happens on its own calendar day by the game's own rules, so when you land in the storm it's the real storm with its real Mail, crew plans and broken machines.
  3. Your part on the way is filled in through the game's own functions, at the moment you'd have done it: Calder's question answered with cqAnswer, the offer heard with endOfferDone, your answer given with endSay. Dev mode never writes those records by hand. The runner pauses its day-by-day skip at each of these minutes (a list of stops, each a time and what to do: Calder's question at the Day 26 tea about 19:10, the offer on Day 32 from 16:00, your answer the same evening), then carries on, so the Captain's address on Day 35 settles your answer as it would in play.
  4. The career is set by the table only up to the table's last day (Day 26 now). After that the game keeps what it wrote itself on the way (the nights, mentoring and Calder's question on your Record), and before that the morning's setting adds to what the game wrote rather than replacing it. CA1 found the every-morning setting would otherwise wipe those by Day 32.
  5. Plain Set career past Day 32 says Stay to the offer, so Days 33-40 are the endgame, staying on: a state a player can reach. Only the ending's Go jumps say Go. The panel says which answers were used.
  6. The clock stops a few minutes before the moment, and you're placed in the room. Then you play it.

Safety rules, from DEV_NOTES "Rules that keep it working":

  • Your real save: never written. All of this lives in the Dev save (keys ending in "-dev"), with the space check before every write.
  • No R(): nothing in section 25 calls it. A jump only uses the game's seeded parts, so two jumps to the same moment give the same save, byte for byte (the check proves it for one jump in each group).
  • The crew's planner only reads records it's allowed to (the settled ones the game itself makes: Basil's day, Calder's question). Where a jump settles one, that day's plans are made again, as the mentoring shortcut already does.
  • Events on their calendar day (question 1). Since 3.1, events are tied to their days (the nights, the storm's Saturday, the comet's sky, the career guard), so starting one on another day is where things would now break. A running event keeps 3.1's tools: Next phase, the threads with − and + (greyed once the event's result is settled, as the game then ignores them) and End and keep (the game's own eventFinish: the debrief, the log, the traces; then the day's crew plans are made again, as a Dev tradition start does). End and undo (question 8): recommended dropped, since later events never move now and Undo my last Dev action covers going back; or the jump keeps a copy of the event's first morning to go back to.
  • Without the Dev link nothing of this exists, as now. The checks prove it.
  • Section 26 (the controller, now adding a Dev card at the screen's edge) is not touched. The new rows are ordinary Dev panel buttons, so the controller reaches them the way it reaches the panel today.

  • devmode_check jumps (J0): every registered jump has a known group, a day within Set career's range, a landing room that exists, and an id used once; jumps behind a switch only appear with it on. And a Day 36 jump still has the nights, mentoring and Calder's question on your Record.

Events also: Next phase; threads − and + change the band; End and keep (one debrief, the log written, the event not started again, the crew replanned); threads greyed once settled. Ending: each answer (Stay, Go, Unsure) and each of Calder's four answers reaches the right post and page, run from one Day 26 landing with Undo between answers, not as twelve fresh runs; plain Set career to Day 38 is in the endgame.
  • trad (J3): Go to its next showing lands on the real day with the cake or invitation in place.
  • dev_arrow_check s6, ev, end (J4): at each landing, the arrow names the right step (or the event's meeting) and points at the right deck and room; still none without #dev.
  • Timing: a jump is a whole new game run forward (Set career to Day 26 is in today's check). Each group is its own part so no part runs much over 5 minutes, and the full round spreads them over the six runners. CA1 measured a jump at 12-15 seconds on a test machine (two runs to Day 26 and Day 36 gave the same save byte for byte), so a part of 16-18 jumps is 4-6 minutes; J0 measures a whole part, and the halves let a part split without editing the shared list.

7. Risks

  • Long jumps on your phone. CA1 measured a jump to Day 36 at 15 seconds on a test machine and 65 seconds slowed to a phone's pace. You accepted that kind of wait with a progress bar (v2.1 question 13), so the panel shows its progress, has a Stop button, and says "about a minute" on the late jumps.
  • The table wiping the game's own records. Fixed in J0 (above) and checked: the table only adds, and stops after its last day. This is also a bug in today's Set career: a Set career to Days 27-30 can lose what the game wrote on Day 26 (it's in the backlog as DM1-1, and J0's fix covers it).
  • A piece's own record vs the table. Set career sets the career by a table, approximately. A later piece may read something the table doesn't set (a night's record, a side job). Each jump's check catches this; the fix goes in the table, not in the piece.
  • Your part in events is empty. Landing in the storm, the ship scored it without you so far. The debrief and commendation follow from what you then do, and the threads' − and + move the result. Approximate, and said so on the panel.
  • Traditions tied to an event can only be reached through the event's jump, not the Traditions tab. The tab will say so on their cards.
  • Section 26 is moving (the controller's Dev card). Nothing here touches it; the controller seat checks its card still opens the sheet after J0.
  • Merge clashes: the blocks and stub parts from J0 keep the three builders apart; the two build-number lines stay the build master's.
