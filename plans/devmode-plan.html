Dev mode: the plan

Dev mode planner DP1, 2026-10-20, version 3: the lean Dev mode, after your answers (12:05) and your talk with lead 20. Branch claude/devmode-plan, based on claude/integration (0.64.2 and step 2). Planning only: the game is unchanged. Version 2.1, with both reviews behind it, is in git history (6c37f52). The full day-jump replay is parked in section 7, with its design and measurements.
In short
  • Dev mode is a hidden testing panel. You open it with a ready Dev link (the game's link with #dev). Without that link it doesn't exist in the game.
  • Three stops, about 9-10 turns:
    • Stop 1a, the patch kit: move the clock (workdays excused), keep your needs and top them up, "Take me to…", "Mark / Back to mark", "Undo my last Dev action", "Copy my real game", "Clear Dev save".
    • Stop 1b: start any ship tradition.
    • Stop 2, Set career: pick a day from 0 to 30. Dev mode moves the clock there and gives you what a steady player has by that day: rank, trainings, tools, reputation and the story beats so far. This is approximate, and marked so. The Career tab comes with it.
    • Stop 3, Big events: start, phases, threads, End and undo.
  • The steady-player replay is parked, not deleted. That's the exact day jump, where the game plays a player's days from Day 0. It's much bigger: 13-17 turns, with every career part's owner writing a line. Its design and measurements are kept in section 7 for later.
  • 3 new questions are in section 6. Your earlier answers are recorded there as decided.

1. Opening it, and keeping your real game safe

  • The Dev link (your answer to q1).
    • The lead gives you the playable link with #dev on the end, to tap or bookmark.
    • Stop 1a checks that it works in the Claude app, in a phone browser and after a reload.
    • In Dev mode the place-and-clock card at the top left gets a gold outline and the word DEV. Tapping the card opens the panel, and the panel's title says "Dev mode: not your real game".
  • Dev mode's own save.
    • Your q2 answer was "no saving". After your talk with the lead, the plan keeps a separate Dev save, because "Mark / Back to mark", "Undo" and a page reload all need somewhere to keep your test.
    • Your real save is never touched either way. The game's two storage names get "-dev" added in Dev mode.
    • Dev mode doesn't use the tests' "no saving" switch, because that also turns off the arrival and the clothes.
    • You confirmed this after the talk.
  • Storage.
    • Dev mode keeps three small things: its save, one Mark and one Undo, about 120 KB each. They're well inside the browser's space for this site.
    • There's a space check before every Dev write, it never removes anything of your real game's, and "Clear Dev save" empties it.
    • A check proves your real save still writes, and is byte for byte the same after a Dev session.
    • With no kept days there's nothing big to store, so the separate store (IndexedDB) isn't needed.
  • Copy my real game into Dev: reads your real save (it never writes it), so you can test from the moment you found a bug.
  • Every Dev action:
    • keeps an Undo first;
    • settles what's in progress (a conversation ends with its gist in Mail, an activity stops, a game closes);
    • refuses during a lift ride or sleep;
    • freezes the game while it works, so the result doesn't depend on the phone's speed.

3. Stop 1: the playtest kit

Stop 1a, the patch kit (D1-D4)

  • Clock: next morning, +1 hour, to a time…, to 08:55, to 16:55, to 21:40, +3 hours, +1 day, +7 days.
    • It uses the game's own time jump (what sleeping uses), in 30-minute steps, frozen.
    • Workdays you skip are excused (your q5), as leave days. Under the hood, the career engineer adds a Dev-only list read by careerOff, plus one line in careerTick for today.
    • Skipping while clocked in keeps you clocked in.
  • Needs (your q15) are kept as a normal day would leave them, and Top up needs fills them. This is a Dev-only hook in the player engineer's skip step.
  • Take me to… (q14): your next appointment, any room, or the testing spots (ladder, time clock, job board).
    • It moves you as the lift does, and the clock doesn't move.
    • It's refused, with the reason, during the arrival evening and inside a meeting.
  • Mark here / Back to mark (q14): one saved moment. Back to mark reloads the page into it, which is exact because it's the same save.
  • Undo my last Dev action (q11), Copy my real game, Clear Dev save.
  • The Day 2 meeting recipe:
    1. Play Day 0 and Day 1 once in a new Dev game, or Copy my real game.
    2. On Day 2, go to 08:30.
    3. Take me to: next appointment.
    4. Mark here.
    5. Watch, then Back to mark as often as needed.
    If a skip passes one of the first week's meetings, the panel says so in words ("Skipped: the Day 1 briefing counted as missed"), because those meetings need you in the room.

Published as Stop 1a (your q16) with your Dev link and phone screenshots, so the playtest patch can be checked against it.

Stop 1b, traditions (D5)

  • Search all 60 traditions and start one: in 20 minutes, at its usual time, or tomorrow, with its Mail if you want it. The panel shows how many are going and offers Take me there.
  • Under the hood:
    • A Dev list in the Dev save, read by one line in eventsOn. The traditions engineer reviews it first.
    • Today's plans are made again, so about 20 of the 74 crew move at once and 57 evenings change. The deck refreshes under the lift's fade (your q7).
    • Things decided earlier, like a cake or an invitation, don't appear for a Dev-started tradition, and the panel says so.
  • Handled:
    • birthdays and rotation teas ask whose;
    • flag traditions set their flag in the Dev save only;
    • it's refused on Day 0 and during a big event, with the reason.

4. Stop 2: Set career (D6-D7)

4.1 What it does

  1. Pick a day from 0 to 30. The shortcuts are the story's beats: Day 1, the Day 2 solo job, Day 5, the Day 9 test, Day 10, Day 12, Day 16, Day 26, Day 30.
  2. Dev mode starts a new Dev game by shuttle (new question 2), then moves the clock day by day to that day at 06:00, with every workday excused.
  3. Each morning, before that day runs, it sets the career to what a steady player has by then, from the table below. The ladder then sees those trainings as done and doesn't ask for them again.
  4. The ship itself (machines, jobs, galley, Mail, traditions, the ion storm, the crew's habits) runs through those days with the game's own catch-up code, as when you sleep. Your part in the storm is "none".
  5. Set career doesn't depend on the first week's meetings happening, so it works even while the Day 2 meeting is broken. Before it sets rank and trainings, it marks the arrival week as attended: the Day 1 briefing, the Day 2 solo job, the Settling-in items, and the Day 1 shadow shift the career starts from. Later systems then treat you as having been there.
  6. The first week's beats are marked as met, the promotion's beats as done (ceremony, cabin 4-23, the pip), and friendships with the people a steady player meets get a starting level (new question 1).
  7. You wake at 06:00 in your bed: the bunk room as a Junior, your cabin as an Officer.

Everything Set career gave you is listed on the panel ("What's set"), and the Dev save is marked "career set to Day 12, approximate".

7. Parked for later: the steady-player replay

Parked on 2026-10-20, the user's call: a lean Dev mode first. It's kept in full in version 2.1 (git 6c37f52, plans/devmode-plan.html), with DR1's and DR2's reviews of it. Its names (DEV_PLAYER, kept days) stay retired for it.

What it was, and what was measured
  • The design:
    • A shared steady player (DEV_PLAYER), with one line per career part written by that part's owner. It plays the days from the Day 0 arrival with the game's own code, in a fresh page, frozen, with a fixed career seed.
    • The first week's meetings are attended in person.
    • Kept days go in IndexedDB, stamped with a fingerprint of the game's script, the player's version and the seed.
    • Exact and repeatable. 13-17 turns.
  • Measured (headless, 4 cores, page load not counted):
    • From the Day 0 arrival with saving on and seed 1: Day 2 08:40 in 1.2-1.4 s, Day 10 in 6.3-7.3 s, Day 26 in 15.7-17.1 s, the same twice.
    • From Day 1 with no saving: Day 10 in 7.5-8.1 s and Day 26 in 18.9-20.5 s; slowed 4×, 40.4 s and 101.9 s.
    • DJ1, a careful player (to be confirmed): Day 10 in ~11 s, Day 26 in ~43 s, Day 30 in ~56 s; a phone estimate of 3-5 minutes to Day 30. Officer by Day 16, 19 trainings by Day 26.
  • What it found:
    • The simple players stayed Juniors because they never answered the incident call for "Making it safe".
    • Trainings need minute-level steps.
    • A replay after New game isn't repeatable, so it needs a fresh page.
    • With saving on, the career's random seed makes runs differ unless it's fixed.
    • The first week's meetings need the player in the room on the drawn deck.
  • When to unpark: when approximate career state stops being enough, for example for testing friendships and stories at a given day (the "Talk a while" pack), or for checks that want a believable day without playing it.

8. What changed in version 3

  • Stop 2 is now Set career: a table, the excused skip and the beats set. It replaces the replay, which is parked in section 7.
  • No kept days and no IndexedDB. No fixed career seed either, which was only needed for the replay's repeatability; Mark and Undo restore exactly.
  • About 9-10 turns, down from 13-17.
