Ship-wide events: the plan

Events planner EP1, 2026-10-20, version 3.2: all your answers are in, and there are no open questions. Branch claude/events-plan, from build 0.64.2. Planning only: the game hasn't changed. Your decisions are now in design-doc.md ("Ship-wide events"). Earlier versions and the reviews are in git (claude/events-plan-review).
In short
  • Four events before the career ends (Day 26), plus a finale:
    • Crossing the Line, a new one-day event on Sunday Day 7, your second day off, so your Saturday stays completely free;
    • the ion storm, moved a day later to Days 11-15;
    • comet viewing on Tuesday Day 16;
    • the diplomatic visit as a weekend finale, Days 26-30.
    Everything else stays parked (section 6).
  • Crossing the Line is the one extra short event you asked for. The Wren crosses into the Veyli reach. In the afternoon the whole crew gathers on the plaza to welcome everyone crossing for the first time, you included. In the evening the Sunday supper becomes the Line supper. That's two big crowd moments, and it sets up the Veyli visit at the end.
  • More big crowds in every event, as you asked after the playtest: a candlelit supper in the mess in the middle of the storm, and the whole crew lining the shuttle bay to greet and see off the Veyli.
  • The ready room and the Captain's table stay separate, as you chose. Each is spaced out by the day and the hour, so that nothing lands on a test day or a night shift and there's no more than one of the Captain's scenes per day (section 1). Your commendation letter comes at the storm's all-clear (your answer). Crew who are called in on their day off say so: there are lines and chatter about it.
  • Build order: the shared base, then the ion storm, then Crossing the Line, then the comet, then the visit. Each is built switched off and switched on after your review. About 36-45 build turns, plus the head writer's lines.
  • No questions left. Your answers are listed in section 6, and the design doc records them.

The career dates, and the steady, busy and slow players
  • The career ends on Day 26, the same day for a steady and a busy player, because each step has a first possible day. Today's game has the Officer promotion (Day 10), and Pillar 5 steps 5-6 add Senior Officer on Day 16 and Engine Specialist on Day 23. Pillar 5 now comes after the events, so the event calendar holds its days in advance (F1), and steps 5-6 fit around the events' fixed days.
  • Day 26 is a target: Pillar 5 still needs "not before" days for its two later tests, and a reputation pace a player can keep up with.
  • A slow player becomes an Officer around Day 29. Events go by the calendar (your answer 5), with small parts that depend on rank.
  • A player who skips work is let go on Day 20, so the visit never comes for them.

2. The ion storm: what changes (your answers)

The audit from version 2 still stands: 13 items, checked by the reviewer. The storm runs today, but it was built before most of the game. Your answers decide how it's fixed:

  • Dates: one day later and a shorter afterglow (Days 11-15), with the hours of the Day 11 team job and ceremony kept free.
  • Your help counts:
    • fitting the ballasts shortens the blackouts;
    • taking the batteries up keeps the bridge fed;
    • helping with a call-booth rota shortens the queue.
    Left alone it comes out bumpy, and with good help, smooth. The threads count from the front, not from the warning.
  • The rec deck: its first all-hands gets its own date, so it no longer moves when the storm does. This is built before Crossing the Line, so that the Captain's Day 7 address doesn't count as the rec deck's first all-hands. The rec deck closes from the front to the all-clear.
  • Your commendation comes at the all-clear (your answer). It's earned from storm jobs and meetings up to the all-clear, so the afterglow's tidy-up no longer counts. The threads stop at the all-clear too, so the result and the debrief agree. The summons is spaced out as shown above.
  • New: the lantern supper on the Saturday evening, a big crowd in the mess by emergency light (44 seats, plus standing places).
The 13 update items (from version 2, with your answers)
  1. It lands on your promotion: moved to Days 11-15, with the career-beat guard (F0, F1).
  2. The 89 storm lines never play: phase notes (F2).
  3. Nothing you do changes the result: prep jobs and help move the threads, left alone it's bumpy, and the blackout odds are tuned so a ship at full power comes out smooth (your answer 6).
  4. The device misses meetings, Tracked offers places that are closed, and there's no status line or Record entry (F3).
  5. Skips and reloads lose phases and meetings (F4).
  6. Calls home are blocked only on the front day, and for any event: fixed so they read "comms down" (F8).
  7. There's no proper "event day" for puzzles and incidents: eventDay(d) becomes true only for the front, the escalation and the all-clear (F1).
  8. The rec deck is tied into the storm: its first all-hands is unhooked and it closes during the storm (your answer 7). Blackouts still skip the rec deck (the lead's earlier call).
  9. Your briefing choice never holds a job: the jobs now go up after the briefing.
  10. The Captain's banner covers the HUD on phones (the HUD seat).
  11. Small parts by rank (F5; the head writer writes the lines).
  12. The ready room and the Captain's table: kept separate and spaced out (your answer 8).
  13. Small things: old placeholder code, the framed menu's prop, a reload replaying the jolt, too many traditions on the party evening, and draw calls in Deck 4's common room at 454 (budget 450).

3. Crossing the Line (your choice): Sunday Day 7

A gentle first taste of a ship-wide event, a week after you arrive. It's on Sunday, your second day off, so Saturday stays a completely free day. It's one day long with no score, like the comet. It teaches the event pattern before the storm: the crew talk about it first, then Mail comes, then the Captain speaks, then a gathering, then talk afterwards.

Crossing the Line · Sunday Day 7 · about 4-5 turns
  • What it is: the Wren crosses into the Veyli reach, the first new border in months. An old spacers' custom welcomes everyone crossing for the first time: you, the new hands from Friday's tea, and anyone who's never been this far out.
  • Warning: talk from the Friday new-hands tea onward, and Vance's Mail on Saturday at 18:00. Crew ask "first crossing?" when you tap them.
  • The moment (Sunday 14:30, felt everywhere): the ship's horn sounds on every deck, and the hull lights turn gold until the supper ends. The Captain makes a short address. She's been called in on her day off, and jokes about it.
  • Big crowd 1: the ceremony on the plaza, 14:30-15:15, with about 50 crew. That's after the market (ends 12:30) and before the reading corner (16:00). The first-timers are called forward. The Captain reads the old oath, and you get one reply to choose (proud, nervous, joking). Then the "dunking": a mug of Brennan's famously awful Line punch.
  • Big crowd 2: the Line supper, 18:00-19:15. It takes the Sunday table's place in the mess, with an extra batch and a cake. Brennan is on duty. It's up to about 60 (your answer), so standing places go in around the mess's 44 seats. Before it, at 17:15, "one week aboard" in the bow lounge stays as built and now talks about your crossing.
  • Afterglow (Monday): your Line certificate on your shelf (a keepsake), a small plaque on the plaza with the date, and crew retelling who pulled the worst face.
  • Touches: the Commons (plaza, mess), the galley's extra batch, the hull lights and decorations. No machines and no strain.
  • Reuse: gatherings, the address, the meeting panel, decorations, the cake, keepsakes, and the big-crowd spots on the plaza (71). New, from other seats: the gold hull-light state (performance), the plaque and certificate props (deck architect), the oath scene with a choice and the called-in lines (writer), and the supper replacing the Sunday table (galley and traditions).
  • Why Sunday works: Brennan is on duty and the Captain is called in. The plaza is free in the afternoon, and the Sunday table's slot is already the evening's big meal. Saturday as a fallback is noted below.
  • Risks: it's a ceremony rather than a "phenomenon". Up to 60 people on the plaza needs a draw-call check (crowd size barely moves draw calls today). The ceremony mustn't run into the reading corner (16:00) or "one week aboard" (17:15), both in the bow lounge. Fallback: Saturday Day 6 at 17:00 still works (checked in version 3) if Sunday ever can't be used.
Not chosen: B. Power surge (it stays parked)
  • What it is: on Saturday at 14:00 a junction on Deck 6 trips, and Decks 4 and 5 go dark until about 18:00. Engineering's weekend crew fix it. You can hand out lamps, a Junior's job.
  • Big crowd: everyone ends up in the lit Commons, and the galley crew put on a "dark supper" in the mess (44 seats, plus standing places).
  • Reuse: the storm's blackouts and its jobs. Risk: a power event five days before the storm takes away some of the storm's surprise, and you'd meet the blackouts twice in a week.
Not chosen: C. Letters from home
  • What it is: the Wren passes a relay buoy for half a day, and a backlog of messages from home arrives. The call booths are busy all day.
  • Big crowd: a letters evening in the bow lounge, crew reading bits aloud, with photos on the screen (about 40).
  • Why: it shows you the call booths just before the storm closes them.
  • Risks: the "letters evening" was scratched once before (design doc, "Calling home"), and calls home are limited to one a day.
