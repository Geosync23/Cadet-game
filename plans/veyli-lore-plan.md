## In short

- **Decided history (A, "The ones who went on"):** about six hundred years ago, early in the age of leaving
  Earth, a slow ship went much further than anyone else. It took four lifetimes to get past Tavessa, and its people
  settled a world out there. Nobody heard from them for centuries, and the Concord grew up behind them among the near
  stars. About eighty years ago a Concord survey ship reached the Line and found them. The Veyli say: **"We did not
  leave. We went on."**
- **What grew different** comes from the long voyage, so every custom already written now has a reason: the knot
  cord (the voyage counted its months in knots, read by touch when the lights were low), tea at the same hour (the
  ship's still hour), silence (on a long ship you listen), warm rooms, no bowing, single names, their own language.
- **The theme:** the Veyli are what reaching into the distance does to people. They're still people, still kind,
  and changed by how far they went. The Wren follows where they went first, and the player, far from home, is doing
  the same thing on a small scale.
- **How you learn it:** a plain fact from the first week ("They're people. They just went further out."), then
  deeper, a little at a time, through the month: Ferreira, the Envoy's tea, the Line, the storm, the comet, the
  visit. About **23 new lines and 17 changed ones**. Nothing about why Saruth is aboard, Holt's beats or the visit's
  turns.
- **Pronouns (decided: each Veyli has their own, like the crew):** proposed Saruth she, Teshavi she, Kesrin he,
  Moleth they, Ithra she, Taavek he, following the game's own words where they exist (section 4). The old rule "the
  Veyli are not described in human terms" is retired.
- **What must change:** 17 on-screen lines ("humans" set against the Veyli, and five pronouns), writers' notes,
  and the canon in facts.md and design-doc.md (ready-to-apply text in section 6). Nothing in Holt's arc changes. The full audit is in section 5.

## 1. Who the Veyli are (three histories)

Already written, and every option must keep it:
- the knot cord on the door, a new knot each month aboard
- tea kneeling at a low table, same hour every day
- silence preferred, and many words for "quiet"
- warm cabins
- "do not bow, most of you do it wrong"
- a council, and students of the cord
- Veyli seedlings
- their own language (Taavek has "no common words for most of the parts")
- Veyli ships are rare visitors, and "a frontier shared with the Veyli"
- Holt's bad day "years ago" with a Veyli ship's six-hour silence
- the Wren in the reach two years ago, on a quiet charting run
- Saruth aboard five months, and "the Veyli homeworld"
- Tavessa, "a star cluster the Veyli named"
- the Line, where the Concord's charts grow thin

### A. "The ones who went on" (decided, vl-q1 a)

- **When and why:** about six hundred years ago. Early in the age of leaving Earth, one slow ship set out to go
  further than anyone had. It wasn't fleeing anything and there was no quarrel. It wanted to see how far people could
  go. The voyage took four lifetimes. Past Tavessa they found a world and stayed.
- **How long apart:** centuries with no word either way. The Concord formed among the near stars without them. About
  eighty years ago a Concord survey ship reached the Line and met a Veyli ship. Since then the two have been careful,
  courteous neighbours: rare ships and a few envoys.
- **What grew different (all from the voyage):**
  - **The knot cord.** On the long ship they counted the months in knots, a cord you could read by touch in the dark.
    It's still their calendar, their record and their way of saying things that matter. Saruth's "a new knot each
    month aboard" is exactly this: they're on a voyage too. "Students of the cord" learn its knots and meanings.
  - **Tea at the same hour.** The long ship kept one still hour a day, so the day had a fixed point. ("A fixed point is
    a kindness you give yourself.")
  - **Silence.** On a long ship you listen to the air and the hull, so quiet is a kind of attention. That's why there
    are many words for "quiet". Their silence rites stay secret (section 3).
  - **Warmth.** The voyage ran cold, so a warm room is hospitality.
  - **No bowing.** The Concord bows the way old Earth pictures show it. The Veyli kept bowing and changed it, so a
    Concord bow looks to them like an old drawing done wrong.
  - **Names.** One name each, no family name (already true of all six: Saruth, Teshavi, Kesrin, Moleth, Ithra,
    Taavek).
  - **The language** grew out of old Earth tongues over six centuries and is now its own. Here and there an old root
    still shows, which is a small joy for Ferreira.
  - **Seedlings.** They come from seeds the long ship carried from Earth, changed over centuries in Veyli soil.
  - **Looks** (as drawn today): ordinary people, often tall and slim, with a cord band at the brow. Some dye their
    hair in cord colours (teal, white, grey). Nothing else.
- **Their own saying:** "We did not leave. We went on." It's gentle pride, not a dispute.
- **Why A:** it explains every custom already written without changing one. It fits the oath ("we left the near stars
  behind us") and the user's "reaching into the distance" best. Eighty years of acquaintance sits fine with the
  Wren's run two years ago, Veyli ships being rare and a frontier "shared". No war anywhere in it.

### B. "A quiet parting"

About a hundred and fifty years ago, a movement on the Concord's own worlds wanted a slower, more ritual life and
went out past the Line together. They were never lost, so there are family stories on both sides.
- **Weaker:** 150 years is too short for a language with "no common words" and "many words for quiet". It's more of a
  neighbour than a distance. And "parted from us" can sound like a falling-out, which needs care to stay gentle.

### C. "Lost and found"

A colony from over a thousand years ago lost all contact and kept only legends of Earth until recontact.
- **Weaker:** it brings questions the game doesn't want ("did they know they were human?"), and a "lost colony" can
  read as a mystery to solve rather than a people to meet. It's also hard to square with their pride and their
  council-and-envoy courtesy.

## 2. Why the Veyli matter to the theme: "reaching into the distance"

- **They are the reach, made into people.** They're the far edge of how far humans have gone, and they're still
  people: tea, courtesy, a joke a sentence late. Distance changed their customs, not their kindness.
- **The Wren follows where they went first.** Past the Line (Day 7) the stars have Veyli names because the Veyli got
  there first. "Guests in someone else's sky" now has a warm second meaning: it's a cousin's sky.
- **The oath answers the Veyli saying.** The oath is "We left the near stars behind us, and we did not go alone." The
  Veyli say "We did not leave. We went on." Both are about going far together. The page never spells this out; the
  player can notice it.
- **The player is reaching too.** You're the newest hand, far from Mum and Gran, counting your days aboard. Saruth is
  the one person aboard further from home than you, alone of their people, counting months in knots. Their tea is
  your mirror.
- **The visit is near meeting far:** two branches of one family, careful and curious. Moleth's forty questions are
  about what each side kept.
