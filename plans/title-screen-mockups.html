Title screen mockups

Four ways the game could open. Each is drawn at real phone size (390 by 844, held upright) and as a small landscape view. These are pictures only: nothing in the game has changed. Your answers go in the box at the bottom of the page.

How it opens today

There is no title screen. The page loads three.js, builds the deck and drops you straight into your save (or a new game by shuttle). For the first second or two you see an empty navy screen with the HUD buttons while the deck is built. A title screen would give the game a front door: a name, a Continue that tells you where you left off, and a calm place for New game, Settings and Credits away from the play screen.

What every option shares: Continue goes first and is the biggest target; New game still asks "Start a new game? Your current game will be lost." on the page; the ship's clock does not run while the title is up; touch targets are at least 48px; a controller moves a highlight with the D-pad or left stick, A picks, B backs out of Settings or Credits, and Start is Continue.

A

The ship behind glass

Recommended

The deck from your save, already loaded, sits behind the title with the crew going about their morning. The camera drifts slowly round it, and Continue drops you in exactly there with no second load.

On it
The title over the stars at the top. Continue with your day, time and place underneath; New game; then Settings, Saves and Credits in one row. The build number small at the bottom.
Feels like
Looking in through a window at a home that is already busy. It says "the ship runs itself" before you touch anything.
Touch, controller
Big Continue at thumb height; the rest below it. The pad's highlight starts on Continue. Landscape moves the title and buttons to the right third so the deck fills the left.
Cost
Medium A slow camera drift, a DOM panel, the sim held still while it shows, and the pad panel registration. No new art.
Risks
No extra draw calls or memory: it is the deck you were going to load anyway. The title has to appear before the deck is ready (a plain title on stars for a second, then the deck fades in). The drift and the held sim go behind a switch so regressions stay pixel-identical with it off.

B

The crew terminal

The game opens on your device's old-school screen as it boots: a few lines of crew-terminal text, the title in glowing type, and the menu as a list with a cursor. It doubles as the loading screen.

On it
Boot lines, the title, Continue (with your day and deck), New game, Saves, Settings, Credits, and a loading bar for the deck underneath.
Feels like
Switching on your own kit at the start of a shift. Familiar from the first time you open the device in play.
Touch, controller
Each line is a full-width button. The pad moves the highlighted box; A picks. Reads well on a controller and on a TV.
Cost
Small DOM and CSS only, using the device's skin, its three phosphors and its screen-effects setting.
Risks
None for phones: zero draw calls, and it can show before three.js has even arrived, so it hides the load. It looks like a menu more than a poster for the game; the flicker follows reduced motion.

C

The Wren in the stars

A first look at the Wren from outside: the whole ship gliding slowly across the stars, six decks of lit windows, the Tern shuttle coming in to the stern bay. A poster-style title and a plain menu.

On it
A big title, the ship as the picture, and a text menu with an amber marker on the chosen line.
Feels like
The cover of the game. The most "1.0" of the four, and the only one that shows the ship as a whole.
Touch, controller
Text lines are tall touch targets; the marker moves with the pad. The ship can slowly turn with a drag, for fun.
Cost
Large A new low-poly model of the Wren's outside, which has never been designed. It needs its own plan page and your sign-off on the look (the curved hull, the elliptical bow, where the windows fall).
Risks
A small scene of its own (about 30 to 60 draw calls) built before the deck, so a little more load time and memory on phones. Kept as a model, it could later play the shuttle arrival.

D

Your posting order

The title is the paper that sent you to the Wren: a Concord Survey Service posting order with your berth and where to report, typed and stamped. It fills in as you play: your post, your bunk or cabin, your day aboard.

On it
A letterhead, the title, a few typed lines about you, and Continue, New posting (New game), Saves, Settings and Credits as tabs.
Feels like
Warm and personal, like the start of a story. Fits the arrival by shuttle and later promotions ("Senior Officer" typed in).
Touch, controller
Tabs are big and clear; the pad moves the dark tab. Works with one thumb.
Cost
Small to medium DOM and CSS, paper drawn in code, the typed lines read from your save.
Risks
Zero draw calls. It brings in a third visual style (paper, next to the 3D ship and the phosphor screen), and the typed lines are words to keep in the word files.

My recommendation

A, the ship behind glass. It shows the real game, which is the best thing we have, it costs no extra load or draw calls, and Continue puts you back exactly where the title showed you. It is honest to the pillars: the ship is already running when you arrive.

If you like B's terminal too, the two fit together: the terminal shows for the second or two while three.js and the deck load, then fades into A. That mix is still medium, and B alone is the cheapest good option if we are short of time before 1.0. C is the most striking but needs the outside of the ship designed first, so I would park it for after 1.0.
