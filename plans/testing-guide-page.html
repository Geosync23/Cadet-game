Starship Cadet · the team's testing rules

How Starship Cadet is tested

Every change to the game is checked in full before it reaches you. This page explains how, in plain words. It's the short version of the team's testing guide.

The rule

Every change to the game runs the full round of checks before it is merged or published. Your words: "We just need to keep testing fully."

The full round is 33 checks. Run one after another it would take about 3 hours, so it's always shared between six check runners at once. Each runner is a separate session with its own copy of the game. Together they finish in about 30 to 35 minutes.

The team also has a quick test picker that guesses which checks a change needs. It's only a helper while someone is working. It never decides whether a change is safe; the full round does.

Who runs what

The split is fixed, so everyone knows their share. It's balanced on how long each check really takes.

CR1 about 34 min
  • player (the longest single check, so a light share)
  • devmode, bubble, puzzle, social, talk
CR2 about 35 min
  • intro (the whole first week)
  • perf and cadet_test, both compared with the last release
  • path, arc, lines (and its day part)
CR3 about 32 min
  • day check on Days 0, 1, 5 and 7
CR4 about 32 min
  • day check on Days 12, 13, 16 and 19
CR5 about 32 min
  • day check on Days 21 and 27
  • interactions, events, career
CR6 about 35 min
  • the quick check (words, names, decks, machines, leave, device)
  • mech, bigevents, fridays, disc, tiles, join, food (two runs), clothes

How a runner does its share

  1. Take the exact version of the game the build master names.
  2. Check its fingerprint (a sha256 code) matches the one the build master sent. If it doesn't, stop: they'd be testing a different file.
  3. Run the checks in its share one at a time, keeping each result.
  4. If a check fails, run it again on the last release. If it fails there in exactly the same way and it's on the known list below, it's an old problem. Anything else is new, and it's reported plainly.
  5. Write the results in its own notes file and tell the build master and the lead: "OK", or which check failed and whether it's old or new.

When runners talk to each other

The six runners, the build master and the lead message each other directly when:

  • a fingerprint doesn't match: everyone is told and everyone stops;
  • a problem could spoil other shares, like a broken build or the game failing to start;
  • someone fixes a setup problem, so the others get the fix;
  • someone finishes early: they offer to take a check from the slowest runner, agree it between them and tell the lead, so no check runs twice by accident;
  • any check fails that isn't on the known list: the build master and the lead hear at once.

How a release is tested

  1. The build master puts the release together and runs the quick check.
  2. They send all six runners the version and its fingerprint, plus the last release to compare with.
  3. They wait for all six reports.
  4. They merge and publish only when every runner says OK, or every failure is a known old one.
  5. Any new failure goes back to the person who made the change. Nothing is merged or published until it's fixed.

Known old problems

A failure counts as known and old only if it has a row on the team's list of small known bugs, it fails the same way on the last release, and its message matches. Today there are two:

known The career check says a very busy player reaches "Relied on" before Day 5. It's waiting for your balancing pass. (List row 12.)

known The performance check sees two extra shapes kept in memory after walking between decks. (List row 14.)

fixed in 0.64.14 Three that used to fail now must pass: the Day 12 rec deck, the Tiles board count, and the harvest salad on a farewell-supper day. If one fails again, it's treated as a real failure.

What each check looks after

The quick check (before every save to GitHub)
words

Everything the writers wrote is baked into the game correctly.

names

The game's code reads properly, and nothing is defined twice.

deck

Every room and everything you can tap can be reached on every deck.

machine

The ship's machines wear, break and get repaired sensibly over two weeks.

leave

Booking leave, sick days, missed shifts and what happens if you keep missing them.

device

Every app on your device opens and shows the right things.

Your days
intro

Your arrival and the whole first week: the tour, the meetings, saving and reloading at every step.

player

Your needs, sleep, activities, walking speed and that your save stays small.

interactions

Tapping things starts the right activity, the job board works, and the arrow points the right way.

clothes

Changing clothes, the laundry hamper and the washers.

career

Your Works career: training, reputation and promotion for a steady, a very busy and a lazy player.

mech

Each repair skill you learn, from its training to doing the job yourself.

puzzle

The head-scratcher repair cases.

devmode

Dev mode does nothing without its link and never touches your real save.

The crew and the ship's life
day

A whole ship day: everyone sleeps, eats, works and nobody gets stuck (on each day listed above).

food

The galley has food ready on time and nobody waits too long.

events

The ship's traditions happen when they're due, and people go to them.

bigevents

The storm, the comet and the diplomatic visit, on their days, in their rooms, without clashes.

fridays

Short Fridays and the Captain's address.

join

Tapping a group's leader lets you join what they're doing.

social

Friendships, news and chats among the crew.

talk

"Talk a while" picks fresh topics and doesn't repeat itself.

arc

Every story can reach its ending.

lines

The writers' files are tidy, and nobody talks too much in a day.

Looks, speed and games
cadet_test

The game looks exactly the same as the last release wherever nothing was meant to change, pixel for pixel.

perf

The busiest moments stay smooth enough for a phone, and memory doesn't grow.

bubble

Speech bubbles follow whoever is speaking without shaking or jumping.

path

People find the same routes around the ship as before.

tiles

The Tiles game's rules, fairness and the board on a phone.

disc

The Lightdisc game's rules, fairness and a game on the deck.

Words used here

Check
A small program that plays part of the game by itself and says OK or FAIL.
Full round
Every check, all 33, including the special days.
Quick check
A 5-minute set of checks run before every save to GitHub. GitHub also runs it by itself.
Check runner
A team member session that runs one sixth of the full round.
Build master
The one session that puts changes together into the shared version of the game and publishes it.
Release
A version of the game published for you to play.
Merge
Bringing one piece of work into the shared version of the game.
Fingerprint (sha256)
A code worked out from a file. Two files with the same code are exactly the same.
Known old problem
A failure that was already there before, is on the list of small known bugs, and fails the same way on the last release.
Test picker
A helper that guesses which checks a change needs. Useful while working, never the final word.
