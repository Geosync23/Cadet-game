The idea in one minute

  • Everyone aboard has a job. A work pool is a set of talks only people in that job can have: the Watch talk about rounds and doors, the galley about the dinner rush, Survey about samples and the sky.
  • They're more personal than the shared pool: what they love and hate about the job, why they chose it, who taught them, a mistake that stuck, what they want next, how the work follows them home. The deeper ones wait until you're friends.
  • Every talk keeps the question: they ask you something, you pick one of three replies (curious, about you, light), and they answer. Same as now.
  • Same rules as the rest of the pack: no repeats (once a talk is heard, it's done for the save), people's own stories never told early, cozy and light, nothing romantic.

What makes them "a little more personal"

The shared pool is about the ship and the day. Work talks are about them, through their job. They climb with friendship:

Acquaintance: the job up closeA day in the job, a favourite corner of it, a small gripe, what the work leaves on you (oil on your hands, a song stuck in your head from the bar).about 3 in 10 talks
Colleague: why this jobWhy they chose it, who taught them, the part nobody sees, what they'd change, a skill they're proud of.about 4 in 10
Friend: what it costs and what it meansA mistake that stuck with them, how the work follows them to bed, doubts, what they want next, would they choose it again.about 3 in 10
  • No stranger-tier work talks: nobody tells a stranger about their worst mistake. Strangers still get the shared pool.
  • Still cozy. Mistakes get fixed, doubts are ordinary, nobody is bad at their job. Serious lines stay rare and only behind friendship (the voice bible's rule).
  • Never ahead of someone's story. Torres's Specialist list, Pell's practical, Rina's exam, Tomas's exam, Brennan's offer, Calder's "well done": the work talks skip those people where they'd tell it early, and skip people whose struggle theme is about work (not good enough, burnout, leaving).

Where they sit in the picking order

My recommendation (question 1 has the other choices):

their own talks→their work pool→shared pool→small talk
  • Because work talks start at acquaintance, strangers go straight to the shared pool, so first chats stay light and the work talks arrive as you get to know someone.
  • No repeats, once per save, like the shared pool: if a Watch rating has told you about the two hundred and six doors, no other Watch rating tells it again. (Small teams especially need this: the two storekeepers would otherwise tell you the same story.)
  • How the run-out changes: each person now has more to say before small talk. With about 140 work talks on top of the 100 shared ones, a keen player who talks to four or five people a day reaches small talk in roughly two months instead of one, and the people you work with have the most to say.

Samples: a feel for the tone

Ten samples from five pools. Final text gets the usual checks and TR1's review.

work.works.first-fault · colleague · Works
Do you remember your first proper fault? Not a job off the board. A real one.
Mine was a pump on Deck 6 at two in the morning. I was shaking. It was a loose clip.
What's the job that made you feel you belong here?
LISTENDid anyone come to help?
Nobody. I fixed it. Then I sat on the floor for ten minutes. Then I told someone.
SHAREThe first one I didn't check twice.
Good. That's the day it stops being a test and starts being yours.
CHEERAny job that ends at the tea corner.
Fair. That's most of them, if you plan it right.
work.works.hands · acquaintance · Works
Coil oil. Two washes, and my hands still smell of it at dinner.
My bunkmates call me "the engine room". I've stopped arguing.
What does the job leave on you, at the end of a day?
LISTENDo you mind it?
No. It's proof I did something. Only the soap minds.
SHARESore feet. And a head full of numbers.
That's Works. The numbers go quiet after dinner. Mostly.
CHEERGrease. In places I can't explain.
Welcome. That never stops either.
work.works.choose-again · friend · Works
Some long shifts I wonder if I'd choose Works again. Knowing what I know now.
Then the board clears, the whole bay goes quiet, and I know. Yes. Every time.
Do you ever wonder if you picked the right job?
LISTENWhat nearly made you pick something else?
Someone said I'd be good with numbers in an office. I'd have been bored by Tuesday.
SHARESometimes. Less, every week.
Less every week is the right answer. Tell me if it turns round. I mean that.
CHEEROnly on scrubber days.
Everyone wonders on scrubber days. That's what scrubbers are for.
work.infirmary.waiting-room · colleague · Infirmary
People think the hard part's the cuts and burns. It's not. It's the waiting room.
Someone sits there pretending they're fine. You learn to see it from the door.
How do you tell, when someone's not all right?
LISTENHow do you see it?
They sit very straight. Fine people slouch. Nobody believes me. It's true.
SHAREThey go quiet. Or too loud.
That's it exactly. You'd be good in here.
CHEERThey say "I'm fine" twice.
Twice is the giveaway. Once is usually true.
work.infirmary.one-nurse · friend · Infirmary
I chose this because of one nurse. I was nine, my arm was broken, and she explained everything.
Every bone, every step. I stopped being scared halfway through.
Did someone show you what you wanted to be?
LISTENDo you explain things like she did?
I try. Every patient. Even the ones who'd rather I just got on with it.
SHARESomeone back home. They fixed everything.
Then you're doing their job out here. They'd like that, I think.
CHEERA very patient mechanic.
The best kind of teacher. Patient and greasy.
work.galley.rush · acquaintance · Galley
Dinner rush is seventy-five people in three hours. You stop being a person. You're a pair of hands.
Then it's over, you sit on an upturned crate, and it's the best seat on the ship.
When in your day do you finally get to sit down?
LISTENDo you ever get to eat hot?
Never. Cold dinner, standing up, half eight. Wouldn't change it. Mostly.
SHAREAfter my shift. The first chair I find.
The first chair's always the best one. That's science.
CHEERI don't. I just lean on things.
Leaning's sitting for people with jobs. You'll do.
work.galley.again · friend · Galley
My first month, I burnt a whole tray of the Sunday bread. Brennan's bread.
She didn't shout. She handed me the flour and said "Again." We were there till midnight.
What's a mistake you're glad you made?
LISTENWhat did she say after?
Nothing. Next Sunday she let me take it out of the oven. That said everything.
SHAREOne where someone stayed late to help.
Those are the ones you remember. Not the mistake. The staying.
CHEERMost of them. Better stories.
True. The bread's my best story. Don't tell Brennan I tell it.
work.watch.door-sounds · acquaintance · Watch
A good door goes "tunk". A tired one goes "tank". I know them all by sound now.
I report the tanks. Then someone from Works turns up with a spanner.
Is there a sound on this ship you'd know anywhere?
LISTENHow many doors on a round?
Too many to count out loud. I counted once. Then I stopped. It felt like bragging.
SHAREThe lift chime. It means nearly home.
Good one. That's on my list too.
CHEERThe mess bell. I'm well trained.
So is the whole ship. Brennan rings, we run.
work.watch.switch-off · friend · Watch
On the Watch you think about what could go wrong, all shift. Then you go to dinner and still think it.
I check my own bunk ladder before I climb it. Every night. My bunkmates laugh.
How do you leave the day behind, at the end of it?
LISTENDoes it ever switch off?
On rest days, by midday. Well. Teatime. Nearly.
SHAREA shower, and somebody to talk to.
Somebody to talk to. Yes. That's most of it, I think.
CHEERI don't. I bring it to dinner.
Then sit with me. We'll worry about doors together.
work.bridge.long-turn · colleague · Bridge (helm)
A long turn takes four hours. You hardly touch anything. You watch, and wait, and stay ready.
People think helm is steering. It's mostly patience in a very big chair.
What's the most patient you've ever had to be?
LISTENWhat do you think about, all that time?
The numbers. Then home. Then the numbers again. It goes round.
SHAREWaiting for news from home. Days, once.
That's harder than any turn. I'm glad the news came.
CHEERWaiting for dinner. Every evening.
The longest wait aboard, that. Even for the helm.
