The idea in one minute

  • Everyone aboard has a job. A work pool is a set of talks only people in that job can have: the Watch talk about rounds and doors, the galley about the dinner rush, Survey about samples and the sky.
  • They're more personal than the shared pool: what the job is really like, why they chose it, who taught them, what it means to them, who they do it for, a moment they're proud of. The warmer ones wait until you're friends.
  • Every talk keeps the question: they ask you something, you pick one of three replies (curious, about you, light), and they answer. Same as now.
  • Same rules as the rest of the pack: no repeats, people's own stories never told early, cozy and light, nothing romantic.

What makes them "a little more personal"

The shared pool is about the ship and the day. Work talks are about them, through their job, climbing with friendship:

Acquaintance: the job up closeA day in the job, a favourite corner of it, a small gripe, what the work leaves on you (oil on your hands, a tune stuck in your head from the bar).about 3 in 10 talks
Colleague: why this jobWhy they chose it, who taught them, the part nobody sees, a skill they're proud of.about 4 in 10
Friend: what it meansWhat the job means to them, who they do it for, a moment they're proud of, a habit it gave them that they can't switch off, what they'd teach you if you had a week.about 3 in 10
  • No stranger-tier work talks. Strangers still get the shared pool.
  • Still cozy. Nobody is bad at their job; serious lines stay rare and only behind friendship (the voice bible's rule).
  • Not the struggle stories. "A mistake that still haunts me", "I'm not sure I'm good enough", "should I leave?", "I can't say no": those belong to the crew's own struggle stories (42 of the 86 crew have one, or a story arc). Work talks stay off them, so they don't spoil those stories and don't have to skip half of each team.
  • Never ahead of someone's story. Torres's Specialist list, Pell's practical, Rina's and Tomas's exams, Brennan's offer, Calder's first chief: work talks skip those people where they'd tell it early.

Where they sit in the picking order

My recommendation, after TR1's review (question 1 has the other choices):

their own talks→work and shared, taking turns→small talk
  • Taking turns: after someone's own talks, their work talks and the shared talks alternate, preferring whichever you haven't had with them lately. Otherwise your Works crewmates, the people you see most, would talk only shop for weeks.
  • Work talks start at acquaintance, so strangers get the shared pool. A work talk that isn't ready yet (tier too low) waits, like own talks do; it isn't lost.
  • No repeats: you chose "once per save" for the shared pool on 20 October. My suggestion is that work talks follow it: if a Watch rating has told you how a tired door sounds, no other Watch rating tells it again (question 2).
  • How the run-out changes: with about 120 work talks on top of the 100 shared ones, a keen player reaches small talk in roughly two months across the whole ship instead of one; sooner with your own department, whose pool you'll use fastest. More talks were already planned for when the game grows past Day 30.

Samples: a feel for the tone

Ten samples from five pools, with the conditions they'd carry. Final text gets the usual checks and TR1's review.

work.works.first-fault · colleague · Works · not Calder, not on the "not good enough" story
Do you remember your first proper fault? Not a job off the board. A real one.
Mine was a pump on Deck 6 at two in the morning. I was shaking. It was a loose clip.
What's the job that made you feel you belong here?
LISTENDid anyone come to help?
Nobody. I fixed it, wrote it up, and went back to bed grinning.
SHAREThe first one I didn't check twice.
Good. That's the day it stops being a test and starts being yours.
CHEERAny job that ends at the tea corner.
Fair. That's most of them, if you plan it right.
work.works.hands · acquaintance · Works juniors
Machine oil. Two washes, and my hands still smell of it at dinner.
My bunkmates call me "the engine room". I've stopped arguing.
What does the job leave on you, at the end of a day?
LISTENDo you mind it?
No. It's proof I did something. Only the soap minds.
SHARESore feet. And a head full of numbers.
That's Works. The numbers go quiet after dinner. Mostly.
CHEERGrease. In places I can't explain.
Welcome. That never stops either.
work.works.asleep-upstairs · friend · Works
Know what I think about on a long shift? Who's asleep upstairs because the air's right.
Seventy-odd people. Not one of them knows my name was on that job. I like that.
Who do you do the job for, really?
LISTENDoesn't it bother you, nobody knowing?
No. I know. Calder knows. That's plenty of people.
SHAREMy family. And the crew, now.
Both is right. Out here they're the same thing, nearly.
CHEERWhoever's on the tea rota.
Honest. Tea's a fine reason to keep a ship running.
work.infirmary.bench · colleague · Infirmary · suits Oduya best
People think the hard part's the cuts and burns. It's not. It's the bench by the door.
Someone sits on the bench pretending they're fine. You learn to see it from the door.
How do you tell, when someone's not all right?
LISTENHow do you see it?
They sit very straight. Fine people slouch. Nobody believes me. It's true.
SHAREThey go quiet. Or too loud.
That's it exactly. You'd be good in here.
CHEERThey say "I'm fine" twice.
Twice is the giveaway. Once is usually true.
work.infirmary.one-nurse · friend · Infirmary · not Rina, not Oduya, not on the exams story
I chose this because of one nurse. I was nine, my arm was broken. She explained it all.
Every bone, every step. I stopped being scared halfway through.
Did someone show you what you wanted to be?
LISTENDo you explain things like she did?
I try. Every patient. Even the ones who'd rather I just got on with it.
SHARESomeone back home. They fixed everything.
Then you're doing their job out here. They'd like that, I think.
CHEERA very patient mechanic.
The best kind of teacher. Patient and greasy.
work.galley.rush · acquaintance · Galley · not the bartenders
Dinner rush: seventy-five people in three hours. You stop being a person. You're hands.
Then it's over, you sit on an upturned crate, and it's the best seat on the ship.
When in your day do you finally get to sit down?
LISTENDo you ever get to eat hot?
Never. Cold dinner, standing up, half eight. Wouldn't change it. Mostly.
SHAREAfter my shift. The first chair I find.
The first chair's always the best one. That's science.
CHEERI don't. I just lean on things.
Leaning's sitting for people with jobs. You'll do.
work.galley.again · friend · Galley · not Brennan
My first month, I burnt a whole tray of the Sunday bread. Brennan's bread.
She didn't shout. She handed me the flour and said "Again." We were there till midnight.
Who taught you the thing you're proudest of?
LISTENWhat did she say after?
Nothing. Next Sunday she let me take it out of the oven. That said everything.
SHARESomeone who stayed late to help me.
Those are the ones you remember. Not the hard bit. The staying.
CHEERNobody. I'm self-taught. Badly.
Self-taught still counts. Brennan would say so too.
work.watch.door-sounds · acquaintance · Watch · not Holt
A good door goes "tunk". A tired one goes "tank". I know them by sound now.
I report the tanks. Then someone from Works turns up with a spanner.
Is there a sound on this ship you'd know anywhere?
LISTENWhat happens to the tired doors?
Works gets a note. Then someone like you fixes it. That's the circle.
SHAREThe lift chime. It means nearly home.
Good one. That's on my list too.
CHEERThe kettle at the tea corner.
So's the whole ship. It boils, we come.
work.watch.switch-off · friend · Watch juniors
On the Watch you think about what could go wrong, all shift. Then you take it to dinner.
I check my own bunk ladder before I climb it. Every night. My bunkmates laugh.
How do you leave the day behind, at the end of it?
LISTENDoes it ever switch off?
On rest days, by midday. Well. Teatime. Nearly.
SHAREA shower, and somebody to talk to.
Somebody to talk to. Yes. That's most of it, I think.
CHEERI don't. I bring it to dinner.
Then sit with me. We'll worry about doors together.
work.bridge.long-turn · colleague · Bridge, helm only
A long turn takes four hours. You hardly touch anything. You watch, wait, stay ready.
People think helm is steering. It's mostly patience in a very big chair.
What's the most patient you've ever had to be?
LISTENWhat do you think about, all that time?
The numbers. Then home. Then the numbers again. It goes round.
SHAREWaiting for news from home. Days, once.
That's harder than any turn. I'm glad the news came.
CHEERWaiting for the lift on a Monday.
The longest wait aboard. Even the helm can't hurry it.
