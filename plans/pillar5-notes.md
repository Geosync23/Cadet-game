# Pillar 5: questions for the user (draft, 2026-10-17)

From the Pillar 5 planner, for the lead to show the user. Not a plan. Each question has a suggested answer: if the user says nothing, the plan goes with it.

**The direction so far (the user, 2026-10-17):** Pillar 5 is about two things, each standing on its own: **(1) expanded core gameplay** and **(2) ship-wide events.** It overlaps with Pillar 4, which is still being built, so the core gameplay comes first (it lives in parts of the game Pillar 4 barely touches: the machines and jobs, the player's needs and activities, the device's work apps), and the ship-wide events come after Pillar 4's wave 3, when Pillar 4 is mostly writing.

## Part A: expanded core gameplay (your Works job)

1. **What should "expanded core gameplay" mean?** Today the job loop works (take a job at the board, inspect, fetch a part, repair, turn in), but the crew catch nearly every fault early, so you rarely meet a real one, and there is no way to grow past Tier 1.
   *Suggested:* make the Works job a career. In order: **more and better faults for you** (Q2), **diagnosing as real play** (Q3), **your first promotion** to Technician with your own cabin (Q4-Q6), **skill** that grows with practice (Q7), and **tools** that let you read new kinds of faults (Q8).

2. **How many real faults should reach you?** Today about 3-6 jobs a day are left for you, but most are routine servicing.
   *Suggested:* the same number of jobs, but about half of them real faults (a machine that's misbehaving, with a cause to find), by having the crew leave more of your tier's faults for you and catching worn machines later. A slack day still has something to do; a busy day has a choice of priorities.

3. **How does diagnosing look?** (An open question since v4.)
   *Suggested:* a simple panel in the device's screen style when you inspect a machine: its 3-5 parts (filter, pump, relay, seal...) each with a reading. You work out which part is failing from the readings and the symptoms you saw on the deck (the drip, the flicker). Picking wrong just costs time (you fetch the wrong part, and the machine tells you), never a fail. No mini-games: it's reading and deciding, then the physical fetch-and-fix as now.

4. **Your first promotion (Tier 2: Technician, Officer rank, your own cabin).** The design has it; nothing is built.
   *Suggested:* yes, Pillar 5 builds it end to end: experience from jobs, Calder's progress talks telling you how close you are, a **supervised certification job** on a Tier 2 machine (an air scrubber or the water recycler), a small promotion moment (Calder, then the Captain's congratulations by Mail, a new badge on your uniform), and the move to **your own cabin** on Deck 4's officers' corridor, which Pillar 4's "Come over" visits then use. Roughly 2-3 ship weeks of steady work in (about 10-14 real hours).

5. **Can you be passed over, or only delayed?** (An open question.)
   *Suggested:* **only delayed.** If the certification job goes badly, Calder tells you what to work on and sets another in a week. Nobody is ever passed over.

6. **What does Tier 2 work feel like?** The design says "connected systems: a fault can start upstream; trace it from the manifold room."
   *Suggested:* yes: Tier 2 faults show their symptom in one place (weak showers on Deck 4) and their cause somewhere else (the recycler on Deck 3, or a pipe junction), and you trace them with the manifold room's board and the pipe galleries. The Deck 5 machine rooms and the spine's crawlways open with your Tier 2 key. **Tiers 3 and 4** (planning shutdowns around the crew; leading team jobs on the engines) **wait for a later pillar.**

7. **Skill: should repairs get faster as you practise?** The design says yes, per tier.
   *Suggested:* yes, per kind of machine: the 10th oven repair is noticeably quicker than the first, shown in the Record as a short line ("Ovens: you know these well"), **no numbers or skill points** (the user found "what you know" counts too gamey in Pillar 4).

8. **Tools.** The design lists a diagnostic scanner, a pipe kit, a conduit tester and a calibration rig.
   *Suggested:* the **diagnostic scanner** in Pillar 5 (it shows worn machines on your rounds before they fault, so prevention becomes a real choice) and the **pipe kit** with Tier 2. The other two wait for Tiers 3-4.

9. **Night on-call duty.** (An open question: should you ever work nights?)
   *Suggested:* yes, from Tier 2, **opt-in** on the device: about once a week you're on call, and if a machine breaks overnight your device wakes you; fix it and go back to bed, and you get a lie-in the next morning. Off by default.

10. **New things to do off shift?** Today you can eat, wash, sleep, work out, call home, shop, sit, use what the crew use.
    *Suggested:* a few small ones that grow from the job: **tinkering** in the machine shop on your own time (making small things, which Pillar 4's machine-shop gifts can use), **furnishing your own cabin** once you have one (bought at the store or made in the shop), and **logging stars** at the observatory telescope for Survey (a little Survey friendship). No new rooms.

11. **Your device's work apps.**
    *Suggested:* light changes only: the Jobs app gains your **work log** (what you fixed, when, and who noticed), the Record shows **how close certification is** in words, and a **Tools** line shows what you carry. The device engineer adds them.

12. **The provisional weekday drill (Wednesday afternoon) and bunk inspection (Thursday).** They're in the crew's week but you take no part.
    *Suggested:* make them real and small: the **drill** is a 15-minute muster at your station (turn up and it counts toward trust; skip it and Calder notices), and **inspection** checks your bunk and uniform (a grubby uniform or a messy locker gets a gentle remark, a tidy one a nod). Both are core gameplay, not events.

13. **Other jobs (galley, infirmary, survey) as transfers.** The design says Works only for version 1.
    *Suggested:* still Works only. Not in Pillar 5.

## Part B: ship-wide events

14. **Which events in Pillar 5?** Built so far: the ion storm (on); the diplomatic visit (built, switched off); the comet viewing (drafted on paper, not in the game). The agreed order after those: crew rotation day, something in the garden, power surge, shipboard illness, the Founding Day festival, nebula drift, the late supply shuttle, salvage, a Veyli vessel needs help, the déjà vu day.
    *Suggested:* in Pillar 5, **switch on the comet viewing and the diplomatic visit**, then build **something in the garden, the power surge, shipboard illness and the late supply shuttle** (they grow from systems already built: the garden, the machines, the infirmary, the supplies). Then, if the pillar has room, **nebula drift and salvage**. The **festival** waits for the real-phone crowd test; **the Veyli vessel and the déjà vu day** go to a later pillar (the déjà vu day replays a whole day, the riskiest thing on the list).

15. **"Each event standing on its own": what does that mean for you?**
    *Suggested:* each event is one self-contained block that can be switched on or off by itself, has its own test, and doesn't depend on another event having happened. The agreed order stays for a new game, but any event can be started on any day for testing and for you to try it (a "try this event" switch for review builds). One event = one reviewable piece, played and signed off by you like a deck.

16. **Crew rotation day.** Pillar 4 already builds crew coming and going (transfers, goodbyes, new cadets).
    *Suggested:* **no separate event**: Pillar 4's rotation Sundays are the rotation day. Pillar 5 doesn't duplicate it.

17. **Emergencies, drills and inspections as events?** Beyond Q12's weekly drill and inspection.
    *Suggested:* a few **small incidents** (an hour or two, not days) between the big events, from the machines: a burst pipe floods a corridor, the lift sticks with someone in it, a fire alarm in the galley (a scorched pan, nobody hurt). They're on-call jobs if you're on call, crew talk about them, and they fill the gap between main events. About one a week. A ship-wide **inspection by a visiting Fleet inspector** (a two-day event: the ship is cleaned and fixed up, the inspector tours, a report by Mail) is a good new main event if you want one.

18. **How often, and what happens after the list ends?** One main event every 1-2 ship weeks means the list lasts about 14 ship weeks (about 65 real hours).
    *Suggested:* keep that pace. After the list, events **come back in variations** (another storm, another visit), rarer, so the ship never goes quiet for good.

19. **Your rank in events.** Events already show different parts by rank; with Tier 2 in Pillar 5, you can reach new parts.
    *Suggested:* yes: each Pillar 5 event has a Tier 2 part (for example, tracing the surge's cause at a junction, or keeping the air plant going while half the crew are ill), and **the conference table** (a senior briefing invitation, built in Pillar 3 but waiting for an event to use it) gets its first real use.

## Part C: how big and how fast

20. **Staffing.** Pillar 4 runs about 15 helpers in one game file.
    *Suggested:* Pillar 5 starts small, **3 helpers** for the core gameplay now (systems/jobs, the player's progression and activities, and the device's work apps folded into the device engineer's queue), then **2-3 events engineers** after Pillar 4's wave 3, so the total never passes about 18 active sessions. Check-ins with you after the promotion is playable, and after each event (or every two).
