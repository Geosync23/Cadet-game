# The Captain's Friday addresses: the lines (for review)

Events writer EW1, 2026-10-20, branch `claude/events-lines` (from claude/integration 0.64.5). Words only: the game is unchanged.

**What this is.** Every Friday the day shift stops at half past three, and at four Captain Marrow speaks to the whole crew in the rec deck auditorium (the short-Fridays plan, revision 4). There are four Fridays in your first month, Days 5, 12, 19 and 26, and each gets its own full speech that follows what's happening to you and the ship. Her lines appear as speech bubbles over her, one about every three ship minutes, from 16:00 to about 16:50, at normal clock speed. Anyone who isn't there gets a short Mail instead.

**How to read it.** Each speech is below, line by line. Lines marked *(if ...)* change with what really happened: the storm's outcome, your rank, whether you helped. A player who's behind (still a junior when the others are Officers) hears the version for where they actually are. `{me}` is your name.

**Her voice** (from her character card): calm, unhurried, curious, short plain sentences with a quiet joke folded in, and "we" for the ship. She never raises her voice and never gossips.

**Things kept out on purpose (no spoilers):** her daughter, retiring and what the Admiral is (her story); Chief Holt's past with the Veyli; Chief Calder's question on Day 26; the four-weeks cake (it's a surprise, so she only says "Works has something for you"). On Day 5 she doesn't know the storm is coming: she only says "a quiet ship, I hope".

- **Marrow:** We meet here at four, and dinner's at five. A week is long. We end it together.
- **Marrow:** A few stay on duty: the bridge, a medic, the cooks. They get this by Mail, with my thanks.
- **Marrow:** On Sunday afternoon the Wren crosses the Line, into the Veyli reach.
- **Marrow:** At half two the horn sounds on every deck and the hull lights go gold. Hold your mugs.
- **Marrow:** First crossing? Come to the plaza. There's an old oath and Brennan's Line punch. Be brave.
- **Marrow:** The week behind. The board was busy, and Works kept up with it. Thank you, Chief Calder.
- **Marrow:** This week's nod goes to the galley. Three meals a day, and the biscuit tin never ran dry.
- **Marrow:** Somebody tell Chef Brennan I said so. She'll pretend not to be pleased.
- **Marrow:** And a welcome to our newest hand, {me}, five days aboard. You picked a good ship.

*Mail for anyone not there:* "All hands: the Captain's Friday address": Short Friday, short speech. Why we stop at half three, the Line on Sunday (14:30, the plaza: first-timers, come forward), the galley's nod, and a welcome to our newest hand, {me}. Dinner at five.

## Day 12: Steady in the storm

The ion storm hit at 14:00, two hours before (the jolt and her short "We're in it" message). As you asked, it's the heart of the speech. Your Officer promotion was this week (Wednesday and Thursday), so this is the first time she says "Officer {me}" in front of everyone. Beats: what the storm is and how long; what's down, what's held and who's fixing it; what she needs this weekend; your promotion, with a joke; the all-clear she expects.

- **Vance:** Settle, please. The Captain. Quickly; I'm due back on the bridge.
- **Marrow:** Afternoon, everyone. If the lights dip while I talk, that's the storm. Not my speech.
- **Marrow:** The rec deck is shut for the storm. I've borrowed this room for an hour, then it shuts.
- **Marrow:** At two we flew into an ion storm. Dr. Sato says it's bigger than a moon. She's thrilled.
- **Marrow:** It can't hurt us. It can make the lights flicker, the comms go quiet, and Works very busy.
- **Marrow:** We expect it to blow through by Sunday morning. Two nights, perhaps a little less.
- **Marrow:** What's down: calls home and outside Mail. The booths and the bow lounge are shut for now.
- **Marrow:** I know that's hard. Your people will be there on Sunday. So, I'm afraid, will the queue.
- *(if the shielded lamp ballasts were fitted in time)* **Marrow:** What's held: the new lamp ballasts went in yesterday. That's why the lights only flicker.
- *(if they weren't)* **Marrow:** Not every lamp got its new ballast in time. Expect a dark corridor. Walk, don't run.
- *(if the battery packs reached the bridge (and you didn't carry them))* **Marrow:** The bridge has its spare battery packs, carried up from the bay by hand. Thank you.
- **Marrow:** Chief Calder's crew have been on the board since the jolt. They'll be on it all weekend.
- *(if you carried the battery packs)* **Marrow:** {me} carried the bridge its battery packs. It says thank you, between sandwiches.
- *(if you fitted ballasts)* **Marrow:** {me} fitted some of those ballasts. If your corridor's bright, that's who to thank.
- **Marrow:** What I need from you. Stay in when you can. Check on your neighbours. Eat the stew.
- **Marrow:** Some of you will be called in on your days off. I'm sorry. And thank you, in advance.
- **Marrow:** Tomorrow at six, supper in the mess by lantern light. If you're staying in, come and sit.
- *(if you're an Officer (on track))* **Marrow:** Happier news. Works gained an Officer this week. Officer {me}, stand up a moment.
- *(if you're an Officer (on track))* **Marrow:** There. First time I've said it in front of everyone. Sit down before the lights go.
- *(if you're still a junior (behind))* **Marrow:** Happier news. {me}, not two weeks aboard and learning fast. The Chief has noticed.
- *(if you're still a junior (behind))* **Marrow:** She says you ask good questions. Keep asking them. A storm is full of answers.
- **Marrow:** The all-clear, I hope, on Sunday morning. I'll tell you myself. Until then: steady.
- **Marrow:** Dinner's at five. Enjoy your weekend, those of you who have one.

*Mail for anyone not there:* "All hands: the Captain's Friday address": The storm: what it is, what's held, and what I need this weekend. Stay in when you can, look after each other, and come to the lantern supper tomorrow at six. All-clear expected Sunday morning.

## Day 19: After the storm

The all-clear was Sunday, the comet Tuesday, your commendation talk in the ready room yesterday. Your night shifts start tonight, so if you're on track you're asleep and get this by Mail (she names you anyway). Beats: the storm's tally and who carried it; the comet; commendations and ranks; the night watch, a goodbye and a welcome; the week ahead.

- **Vance:** Settle, please. The Captain.

- **Marrow:** Afternoon, everyone. Our fourth Friday. For some of you, that's every Friday you've had.

- **Crew going, and talking about it afterwards:** "Captain at four. Back row's mine. Don't tell anyone." / "She always says one thing you remember all week. Come and hear it." / "'Those of you who have one.' She says it every Friday. I wait for it."

## Judgement calls

1. **Length.** Each speech is 16 to 19 lines, depending on the version (about 16 was the aim). I suggest the lines are spread evenly from 16:00 to about 16:48, so a longer version speaks a little faster rather than running past 16:50.
2. **Day 12 and the closed rec deck.** The plan closes the rec deck for the storm, but the address is in the auditorium. She says so: "I've borrowed this room for an hour, then it shuts." The builders need to open it for that hour.
3. **Commander Vance** opens each address ("Settle, please. The Captain.") and has one dry line on Day 26 about his list.
4. **She talks to the room, not only to you.** You get two or three lines each Friday; the rest is the ship's week.
