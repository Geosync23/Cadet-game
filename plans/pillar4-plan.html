In short

1. You arriveEvery new game opens on a Sunday evening as your shuttle settles in the Deck 5 bay. A Works officer meets you at the ramp and walks you to your bunk, hands you your device, and takes you to dinner, where Chief Calder stops by. You always walk yourself; there are no cutscenes.
2. Your first weekPeople teach you, not pop-ups. Monday you shadow a real job from start to finish. Through the week, the crew each show you one thing. An optional "Settling in" list on your device keeps track. Your first Saturday, you plant a seedling of your own.
3. A voice for everyoneAll 75 crew get their own way of talking and their own lines: greetings, moods, work talk, reactions to what really happens, and conversations you overhear as you pass. Ten main characters get story arcs. Mostly light, with real moments as you grow close. No romance.
4. Deeper friendshipsDesigned by the new social system designer: crew remember what you did, crew friendships change on their own, small gifts, friends visiting your bunk.
5. Many more traditionsFrom a dozen to about 35: new weekly evenings, monthly days, four ship seasons tied to the garden, a midyear and a year's end, and life moments like your first week.
How it's built15 helpers in four waves. A head writer gathers the writers' work and one engineer gathers the tradition designers', so the lead isn't swamped. Three check-ins with you. Starts after Pillar 3 is finished.

The arrival evening (Sunday)

Monday stays Day 1, so nothing else in the calendar moves. The arrival is "Day 0": about two and a half ship hours, or 5 to 10 real minutes. The escort walks ahead talking in speech bubbles, waits if you fall behind, and the goal arrow points at him if you wander. The important conversations use the same dialogue panel as the storm briefing, and you can walk away from any of them (the gist then comes by Mail).

  1. 17:40The shuttle bay. Fade in at the top of the Tern's ramp, holding your kit bag. The one and only "how to walk" tip. Halloran waves from the bottom.
  2. 17:44The haulway. He walks ahead, telling you about the ship ("mind the carts, they don't mind you").
  3. 17:46The Works bay door. "That's us." The job board, the tea corner, the Chief's glass office. "Tomorrow, ten to eight."
  4. 17:48The lift. "Four, please." You press the button yourself, and he rides up with you.
  5. 17:53Your bunk. You put your bag in your locker. He hands you your device; it switches on for the first time and shows a new "Settling in" list. Tomas, your bunkmate, is delighted not to be the newest any more.
  6. 18:12Dinner in the mess. Lift down to Deck 3, a tray at the servery, a table with Halloran (Pell joins if he's there).
  7. 18:30Chief Calder stops by. She sits with her mug for exactly one minute. You can reply one of three ways; there's no wrong answer. "I'm off tomorrow and Tuesday. Halloran has you. Wednesday, quarter to eight, my office."
  8. 19:00You're free. "Bunk's yours from eight. I'm on Four, number 13, the one that smells of bread." Tomas comes to find you for your first proper chat.
  9. from 20:00Bed. Sleep skips to Monday 06:00: your first shift.

If you wander off, Halloran waits and then moves on to the next stop. If you miss dinner, Calder sends a Mail and Brennan leaves you a plate. Nothing can get stuck. Saving works at any point: reloading puts Halloran back at the last stop.

Who meets you, and your boss

Emeka Halloran, your escortWorks systems officer, one rank above you, as you asked. He runs the job board, so he's the right person to teach it. He's on duty Sunday to Wednesday and off Thursday and Friday, which is exactly when the game lets you loose. Kind and loyal, tells terrible jokes at breakfast, bakes bread on his days off, maintains the tea corner's coffee machine (which is always slightly wrong). Lives on Deck 4, so your bunk is on his way home.His heartfelt side, later: he's never trained a new hand before, and the last junior he looked out for, years ago, didn't make it through. You're quietly his second chance.Alternative: Zeke Torres, engine officer, but he's off on Sundays and works down on Deck 6.
Chief Ines Calder, Head of WorksFew words, all exact. Numbers instead of adjectives ("Sixty-one. Not sixty."). Never raises her voice; gets quieter when annoyed. Humour so dry you notice it a beat later. Rare, plain praise: "Good. Clean work." Her creed: "Nobody thanks the air. That's how you know it's working."How she judges you: by your Record, not your words. Catching faults early and finishing what you start count most; leaving a job half-done counts against you more than being slow. She notices who you helped.Her heartfelt side, later: the battered toolbox on her shelf belonged to her first chief, who has written to her; she hasn't answered.
A quirk we turned into storyIn the crew rota, Calder's days off are Monday and Tuesday. Rather than change it, she tells you so at dinner, and your first solo job comes from her on Wednesday. (Question 2 below.)

Later new games

Once you've arrived once, New game asks: arrive by shuttle, or skip to Monday morning (today's start, with your device, the same friendships, and the usual tips).

How we check it works

A new automatic check plays the whole week three ways: someone who follows along, someone who keeps wandering off, and someone who stands still all week. It also saves and reloads at every step, checks the skip option, and makes sure the rest of the game is unchanged.

A voice for everyone

Writers write plain text files, not code. A small program turns them into the game's lines, and an automatic checker catches mistakes (wrong names, lines too long, a serious line too early). About 8,000 to 9,000 short lines in all.

Voice cardsOne for each of the 75 crew: how they talk, what they care about, their running joke, how they are with juniors. Built from what the crew already have (their traits and life details).
Their own linesGreetings that change as you get to know them, moods, work talk, the ship around them, their lives, favours, invitations, gentle remarks ("Waited for you at the gym yesterday").
OverheardTwo crew chatting as you walk past, in alternating bubbles: about the ship, a tradition, the storm, each other. About 150 little conversations.
ReactionsTo things that really happened: "That lamp's been blinking since Tuesday. Nicely done." The storm, a harvest, a birthday, a guest arriving.
Story arcsTen main characters: Halloran, Tomas, Pell, Rina, Calder, Brennan, Commander Vance, the Captain, Chief Holt, and Zainab Kovac (a Works junior secretly afraid of the lift, to show anyone can matter). Plus small threads for 20 others.
Not too chattyOne bubble on screen at a time, no line repeated to you within a week, at most one serious moment a day.

What a writer's file looks like

@crew halloran-emeka == greet ? tier=stranger - Evening. You'll be the new hand. ? tier>=acquaintance time:morning - {hi}, {me}. Coffee's terrible. I made it. #funny == overheard ? pair:works+works topic:board A: Board's light today. B: Then it'll be heavy tomorrow. It always is.

"? tier=stranger" means the line is for people who don't know you yet; {me} is your name and {hi} is how this person says hello, both filled in by the game; #funny is a tone tag.

How lines are checked for voice and tone

Writer→Automatic checker→Head writer reads every batch→Lead samples→You approve the main voices

Generally light, cozy and funny; warmth over wit. Serious moments come only with friendship, and are about ordinary things: homesickness, a failed exam, a parent getting older, self-doubt. Nobody dies on screen. No romance. You'll see a Voices sampler page (each main character with ten lines) before the bulk is written.

Deeper friendships

This part is designed by the social system designer, a separate helper hired at the same time, in their own plan. This plan schedules it and makes sure the pieces fit: what crew remember about you, crew friendships and rivalries that change on their own, small gifts, friends visiting your bunk, and the heartfelt moments friendship opens up. Two social engineers build it.

The joins between the parts are agreed early: lines can depend on how well someone knows you, what they remember, their mood and their friendships; one shared "ship notes" signal tells everyone when something happens (a repair, a favour, a tradition you went to); after the arrival evening you're already on first-name terms with Halloran and Tomas.

Many more traditions

A ship day is 40 real minutes, so a week is about 4⅔ hours of play and four weeks about 19. So most new traditions are weekly (what you live with) or every four weeks (occasions); seasonal and yearly ones are rare treats. Three tradition designers propose them; you pick from a calendar page at the first check-in.

In what order

NowPillar 3 finishes. This plan and the social designer's plan are agreed with you. If you agree, writers and tradition designers can start writing early (nothing in the game changes).
Wave 1: foundationsThe writing format and its checker; the escort's walking; Sunday's schedule; the traditions calendar; the style guide and every crew member's voice card. Nothing visible changes yet.
Wave 2: the arrival eveningPlayable from the shuttle to your bunk and dinner; the first voices, overheard talk and reactions; the first new weekly traditions.Check-in A: play the arrival; approve the main voices; pick traditions from the calendar page.
Wave 3: the first weekThe whole first week; everyone's lines; story arcs begin; gifts and visits; more traditions.Check-in B: play a whole first week.
Wave 4: finishArcs to their ends, the seasonal and yearly traditions, tuning from your notes, a phone speed check at the busiest moments.Check-in C: Pillar 4 final, then it goes into the main game with your OK.

What could go wrong, and what we'll do

  • Slow on phones (the busy mess, decorations, two bubbles): a limit for each tradition's decorations, only one bubble at a time, and the busiest moments checked automatically.
  • Fifteen helpers, one game file: each owns its own part; the writing is generated, never merged by hand; a fixed merge order.
  • Too much for the lead to review: the head writer and the traditions engineer gather the others' work first.
  • Usage limits stopping everyone at once: helpers start in waves, and the lead's hourly check-in restarts anything that stalls.
  • Crew sounding alike, or contradicting each other: voice cards and one fact sheet first; the head writer reads everything; you approve the main voices early.
  • Too much talk: one bubble at a time, no repeats within a week.
  • The introduction breaking if you wander or reload: stops to wait at, Mail as a fallback, and the "wanderer", "idler" and reload tests.
